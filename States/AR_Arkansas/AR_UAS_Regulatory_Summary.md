## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Arkansas's UAS-specific statutory framework centers on protecting critical infrastructure: Ark. Code Ann. § 5-60-103 makes it a misdemeanor to use a UAS to surveil, gather evidence about, or record critical infrastructure (a broadly defined list including energy, chemical, communications, railroad, food-processing, and correctional facilities) without the owner's written consent, paired with a civil-liability statute (§ 16-118-111) giving the infrastructure owner a private damages action. Property-owner and retained-contractor exceptions matter directly for AEC work. Two additional criminal statutes name "unmanned vehicle or aircraft" as a covered instrument in voyeurism offenses, and a narrower statute restricts UAS possession by higher-tier registered sex offenders. Arkansas State Parks is widely reported to require a Director's Special Use Permit for UAS use, though this was not confirmed against a primary source in this pass. No statewide UAS-preemption statute was located.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Ark. Code Ann. § 5-60-103 — Unlawful Use of an Unmanned Aircraft System
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2015, last amended 2021*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it a Class B misdemeanor (Class A for a second or subsequent offense) to knowingly use a UAS to conduct surveillance of, gather evidence or collect information about, or photographically/electronically record "critical infrastructure" — defined to include electrical generation/delivery systems, petroleum refineries, chemical/rubber manufacturing facilities, petroleum/chemical storage, railroad operating facilities, communication towers, food processing/manufacturing facilities, and correctional/detention facilities — without the owner's prior written consent. The statute's UAS definition excludes a UAS used under an FAA certificate of authorization (COA), or used by the federal government, the state (after Governor consultation), or law enforcement/emergency-management agencies for specified purposes. The statute does not prohibit a property owner, or a third person retained by the owner, from using a UAS to survey, inspect, or record the owner's own property (or property held under a lease, right-of-way, permit, or similar right), nor insurance-related underwriting/damage-investigation use.

### Ark. Code Ann. § 16-118-111 — Civil Actions Against UAS Operators
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2015*

**Objective Summary:** Gives a critical-infrastructure owner a private civil action against a person who violates § 5-60-103, for the greater of actual damages or $10,000; three times actual damages (or $10,000, whichever is greater) where the violation resulted in profit or monetary gain; plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees.

### Ark. Code Ann. §§ 5-16-101(b), 5-16-102(b) — Video Voyeurism and Voyeurism (Unmanned Vehicle or Aircraft)
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2015, last amended 2021*

**Objective Summary:** § 5-16-101(b) makes it unlawful to knowingly use an unmanned vehicle or aircraft (among other listed recording devices) that is concealed, operated to escape detection, or disguised, to secretly/surreptitiously record or view another person's body where a reasonable expectation of privacy exists, without consent (Class B misdemeanor, elevated if distributed/posted or on a prior conviction). § 5-16-102(b) separately criminalizes using an unmanned vehicle or aircraft, for sexual arousal or gratification, to look without consent into a private place in a public accommodation or into a dwelling under specified privacy-invading circumstances (Class A misdemeanor up to Class C/D felony depending on victim age and prior convictions).

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### Ark. Code Ann. § 5-14-138 — UAS Restriction for Registered Sex Offenders
*Binding law | Current*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits a person required to register under the Sex Offender Registration Act who has been assessed as a Level 3 or Level 4 offender from purchasing, owning, possessing, using, or operating a UAS, unless required as part of employment. A violation is a Class D felony.
