## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Pennsylvania regulates UAS chiefly through a single 2018 enactment (Act 78), which created a targeted criminal statute (18 Pa.C.S. §3505) and a broad state preemption of local UAS-operation ordinances (53 Pa.C.S. §305). There is no separate Pennsylvania state UAS registration beyond the federal FAA requirement. State agencies diverge sharply on land-access policy: the Department of Conservation and Natural Resources defaults to prohibiting drones in state parks (with six designated exceptions) while generally permitting them in state forests subject to specific operational conditions and, for commercial work, a required Commercial Activities Agreement — a distinction worth building directly into project-siting decisions. A pending 2025-2026 session bill (S.B. 303) would create a drone-assisted game-recovery exception to the Game Commission's current prohibition, but has not been enacted.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### 18 Pa.C.S. § 3505 — Unlawful use of unmanned aircraft
*Binding law | Enacted by Act 78 of 2018*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it a crime to use a UAS intentionally or knowingly to: (1) conduct surveillance of another person in a "private place" (a place where a person has a reasonable expectation of privacy) — summary offense, up to $300 fine; (2) operate in a manner placing another person in reasonable fear of bodily injury — summary offense; or (3) deliver, provide, transmit, or furnish contraband in violation of 18 Pa.C.S. §5123 or 61 Pa.C.S. §5902 (contraband into a correctional, mental health, or detention facility) — felony of the second degree. "Surveillance" is defined as using a UAS to observe, record, or invade the privacy of another.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Standard survey/mapping/inspection flight paths over commercial, industrial, or public infrastructure sites are unlikely to implicate the "private place" surveillance provision, but keep camera angles and loiter time away from residential yards, windows, and other areas with an expectation of privacy during any corridor or site-documentation flight. Route flight paths to keep clear standoff from any correctional or detention facility given the felony-level contraband-delivery provision, even though delivery isn't the intent — proximity alone can raise questions worth avoiding.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Because "private place" and "reasonable fear of bodily injury" are judgment-based standards with only a $300 summary penalty for the first two prongs, the practical legal risk is lower than in states with felony-level general surveillance statutes — but document flight purpose and altitude near residential areas as a matter of good practice. Treat the correctional/detention-facility contraband provision as a bright line requiring advance coordination for any project genuinely needing to fly near such a facility.
### 53 Pa.C.S. § 305 — Preemption of local unmanned aircraft regulation
*Binding law | Added by Act 78 of 2018*

**Objective Summary:** Provides that 18 Pa.C.S. §3505 preempts and supersedes any municipal ordinance, resolution, rule, or other enactment regulating the ownership or operation of UAS; a municipality may not regulate UAS ownership or operation unless expressly authorized by statute. A narrow carve-out permits a municipality to operate its own UAS for municipal purposes and to regulate that specific use, and municipalities may still regulate takeoff/landing on municipally owned property.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Pennsylvania's broad state preemption of UAS-operation ordinances means the primary statewide compliance obligation is §3505 itself rather than a patchwork of city/county drone rules — the one exception to watch for is takeoff/landing on municipally owned property, which local governments can still condition or restrict.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Cite §3505 as the controlling statute rather than any local ordinance if a municipal drone rule is raised during a project — the preemption is broad, though local takeoff/landing conditions on municipal property remain a live issue to check per site. (Local ordinance research itself is out of scope for this phase per current research instructions.)
## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### Aviation / DOT — Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT), Bureau of Aviation
*Advisory guidance*

**Objective Summary:** Despite being titled "Register an Unmanned Aircraft System/Drone," PennDOT's page confirms that registration is with the FAA (drones between 0.55 and 55 lbs), not a separate Pennsylvania state registry — the page functions as a pointer to the federal FAA DroneZone system and to PA UAS law (§3505), plus general safety courtesies (e.g., coordinate with airports within 1 mile).

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Do not budget time or a fee for a separate Pennsylvania state drone registration — confirm current FAA Part 107/DroneZone registration is in order, which satisfies the state page's requirement.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Correct any internal assumption that Pennsylvania has its own state UAS registry distinct from the FAA's; the PennDOT page title is easy to misread as implying a state-specific registration requirement when it is not.
### Fish and Wildlife — Pennsylvania Game Commission
*Binding law and administrative regulation*

**Objective Summary:** 34 Pa.C.S. §2308 and implementing regulations at 58 Pa. Code Ch. 141 prohibit using a drone to locate, drive, harass, or take any game or wildlife. The Game Commission interprets this to cover tracking or recovery of wounded or downed deer as part of the act of hunting — drone-assisted deer recovery is currently not legal in Pennsylvania, and hunters have been cited (fines up to $2,500, potential loss of hunting privileges). Senate Bill 303 (2025-2026 session) would carve out a recovery exception but has not been enacted.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** For environmental-monitoring, habitat-mapping, or corridor-survey flights on or near State Game Lands, plan flight paths and altitude to avoid any appearance of pursuing, driving, or harassing wildlife — steady survey-pattern flights at planned altitude are the safest posture.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document flight purpose (survey/mapping, not wildlife-related) for any AEC work on State Game Lands, and monitor SB 303's status, since a recovery exception would not by itself broaden this statute's application to commercial survey work.
### Senate Bill 303 (2025-2026 session) — proposed drone-assisted game recovery exception — did not pass

**Objective Summary:** Introduced by Sen. As of this research pass, the bill has not passed either chamber and is not current law — the Game Commission's existing prohibition (previous section) remains the operative rule.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Not applicable — recreational hunting legislation, not an AEC commercial-operator authority.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process; the bill has not been enacted.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Do not advise a client that drone-assisted game recovery is or will soon be legal in Pennsylvania on the strength of this bill — it is pending, not enacted. Monitor for enactment in a future legislative session.
### Parks and Public Lands — PA Department of Conservation and Natural Resources (DCNR), State Parks
*Binding administrative regulation*

**Objective Summary:** DCNR enforces a default ban on launching, landing, or operating a UAS within Pennsylvania's 124 state parks absent a special activities agreement, except at six pre-authorized designated flying sites (Beltzville, Benjamin Rush, Hillman, Lackawanna, Prompton, and Tuscarora State Parks). Benjamin Rush State Park (Philadelphia) is the only dedicated drone site inside the city.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Assume any Pennsylvania state park project requires a special activities agreement unless the site is one of the six designated flying parks — build that approval process into project timelines early.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Start with the site manager or permitting office for PA Department of Conservation and Natural Resources (DCNR) before scheduling fieldwork, because property-specific conditions may control the route and timing. Request the current form, lead time, fee, insurance and FAA-document checklist, and site restrictions, then keep the signed approval and conditions in the mission file.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Retain the special activities agreement in the project file before any state park flight outside the six designated sites; do not treat the existence of designated sites elsewhere in the system as authorization for a different park.
### Forestry and Natural Resources — PA Department of Conservation and Natural Resources (DCNR), State Forests
*Official agency policy | citation and detail corrected 2026-08-06 against DCNR's own policy PDF*

**Objective Summary:** Unlike the state park default prohibition, DCNR's state forest drone policy generally allows UAS operation on Pennsylvania's 2.2 million acres of state forest land across 20 forest districts, subject to specific operational conditions set out in DCNR's own published policy: fly below 400 ft AGL; no launch, landing, or operation in designated Natural and Wild Areas or other posted-closed areas; no flight within an active FAA Temporary Flight Restriction; do not fly over or within 100 meters (328 ft) of wildlife; maintain visual line of sight; stay at least 5 miles from an airport or backcountry airstrip; and a maximum aircraft weight of 55 lbs. Commercial operation requires contacting the local forest district office first and obtaining a written Commercial Activities Agreement (CAA), with a remote pilot certificate and registered aircraft available on request.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** State forest land is a comparatively easier launch/land environment than state parks for timber, environmental-monitoring, or corridor-survey work, but treat the Commercial Activities Agreement (CAA) requirement as a real precondition, not a formality — contact the specific forest district office before mobilizing, keep the flight under 400 ft AGL and 5 miles from any airport, and route around designated Natural and Wild Areas and any active TFR (e.g., a nearby wildfire).
- **Agency Practitioner:** Contact the specific forest district office for the property before scheduling any commercial UAS work; request the current Commercial Activities Agreement (CAA) form, lead time, and any district-specific conditions, and have remote pilot certification and aircraft registration on hand.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Retain the executed Commercial Activities Agreement (CAA) in the project file for any commercial state-forest UAS work; the policy's operational conditions (Natural/Wild Area avoidance, TFR compliance, 100-meter wildlife buffer, 5-mile airport buffer, 55 lb weight limit) are DCNR's own stated terms, not merely aspirational guidance.
### Professional Licensing — PA State Registration Board for Professional Engineers, Land Surveyors and Geologists
*Binding general licensing statute | No UAS-specific board guidance located*

**Objective Summary:** A PE or PLS licensed under the Pennsylvania code must practice and perform work only in fields in which they are, by education and/or experience, fully competent and proficient — a requirement secondary sources describe as especially applicable to UAS technology used in mapping. Photogrammetry is recognized as an accepted measurement method for engineering land surveys. No standalone UAS-specific Board guidance document was located, though Board-accepted continuing-education courses on small UAS and aerial photogrammetry for land surveyors exist.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Route drone-derived photogrammetry/LiDAR products through a Pennsylvania-licensed PE or PLS with demonstrated UAS competency (e.g., relevant continuing-education credit) before delivery on any official survey/engineering deliverable.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Before relying on an unlicensed or exempt delivery path, give PA State Registration Board for Professional Engineers, Land Surveyors and Geologists a written description of the proposed UAS-derived product, its stated accuracy, and whether it will establish or certify boundaries, elevations, or authoritative locations. Ask the board to confirm the applicable license, responsible-charge, certification, or exemption path and retain the response with the project quality plan; the research packet does not establish a UAS-specific turnaround time.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Absent UAS-specific Board guidance, rely on the general competency requirement and document the reviewing licensee's relevant training/experience in the project QA/QC record.
### Emergency Management — Pennsylvania Emergency Management Agency (PEMA)
*Advisory guidance*

**Objective Summary:** PEMA, together with State Police and PennDOT, has publicly emphasized that official emergency response activities take priority over drone use near an incident scene and has warned that drones can interfere with emergency helicopters and delay response. This is public-safety messaging rather than a codified PEMA rule; the "do not interfere with emergency response activities" obligation is separately stated in PennDOT's UAS operating guidance and reflects general FAA expectations.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** For any emergency/disaster-damage-documentation flight in Pennsylvania, coordinate with the incident commander or PEMA/local emergency management before flying, and treat "we didn't intend to interfere" as an insufficient justification if a helicopter or ground response is anywhere in the vicinity.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Because this is advisory messaging rather than a specific statute, the operative legal hook for interference is more likely to be TFR violations or FAA enforcement than a dedicated PA interference statute — document airspace/TFR checks before any fire- or emergency-adjacent flight as the primary compliance record.
