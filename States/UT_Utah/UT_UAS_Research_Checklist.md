# Utah UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Utah (UT)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current Utah Code Titles 65A, 72, and 76 and the enacted 2026 session were reviewed. The packet includes commercial registration, preemption, public-agency data, prohibited uses, safe operation, procurement, privacy, trespass, wildfire, corrections, and livestock provisions. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | Utah State Parks definitions and commercial-use permit rules are included. No separate current statewide administrative rule supplying the final UAS registration fee and portal workflow was located; UDOT states the final portal is still in development. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Current Utah executive-order materials were searched. No current statewide operator-facing UAS executive order was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Utah appellate decisions and Attorney General materials were searched. No controlling reported decision or formal opinion specifically applying the current UAS provisions to ordinary commercial AEC operations was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | Utah Code §§ 72-10-109 and 110 require state registration for covered commercial UAS operations. UDOT currently directs commercial and government operators to pre-register before the announced final portal launch. Section 72-10-1002 contains state safe-operation rules. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | State Parks commercial-use rules and park-specific permit pages are included. Utah Code § 65A-3-2.5 governs wildfire scenes, and § 76-13-214 prohibits UAS harassment of livestock. General wildlife rules lacking an express UAS provision were excluded under the scope gate. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Utah Code §§ 72-10-802, 803, 903, 1002, and 1202 address law-enforcement data, corrections, transit and surface critical infrastructure, and public-entity critical-infrastructure inspection. The superseded Title 63G emergency chapter was not treated as current authority. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | Utah Code §§ 76-12-302, 76-6-206, 76-13-214, and 76-2-106 expressly address UAS-related privacy defenses, criminal trespass, livestock harassment, and commission of another offense with a controlled UAS. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable source found | Utah Code §§ 72-10-1201 and 1202 restrict covered-foreign-entity UAS used by public entities or their direct contractors for critical-infrastructure inspection and prescribe an offline/data-removal/software-control alternative. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Utah engineering and land-surveying board materials were searched. No current UAS-specific board rule, opinion, or bulletin was located. Section 76-12-302 contains a land-surveyor data defense but is a criminal privacy statute, not a licensing-board rule. |
| 11 | State preemption | Applicable source found | Utah Code § 72-10-701 broadly preempts political-subdivision regulation of private UAS use, preserves stated airport-operator authority, and creates statewide recognition of one qualifying Utah local business license. Local ordinance research remains deferred. |

## Open verification items

1. Monitor UDOT’s announced late-summer-2026 registration-portal launch and capture the final fee, certificate, renewal, waiver, suspension, and documentation rules when published.
2. Before a surface-critical-infrastructure mission, confirm the facility’s statutory classification and obtain prior written authorization from the facility operator.
3. For a public-entity critical-infrastructure inspection, determine whether the aircraft manufacturer or assembler is a covered foreign entity and document the public entity’s required offline, data-removal, and video-software controls.
4. Confirm the specific state park’s current commercial Special Use Permit, separate drone form, application lead time, insurance, fee, closure window, and approved operating area.
5. Confirm the correctional-property boundary and avoid payload delivery, removal, or conduct that could interfere with security; the statute publishes no general commercial permission path.
6. Recheck Utah’s codified session update and UDOT procedure immediately before relying on this packet because registration implementation is actively changing.
