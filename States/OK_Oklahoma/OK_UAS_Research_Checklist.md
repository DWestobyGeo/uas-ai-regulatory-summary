# Oklahoma UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Oklahoma (OK)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current Titles 3 and 21 and the completed 2026 session were reviewed. Sections 3-322 and 21-1743 are included. S.B. 1441 adds criminal exposure to § 3-322 beginning November 1, 2026; proposed bills that did not enact, including the proposed foreign-drone procurement bill, were excluded. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | The current State Parks aircraft and permit rules directly regulate drone takeoff and landing. A Department of Agriculture rule barring unmanned aircraft for permitted aerial management of depredating animals was reviewed but omitted because it does not materially affect commercial AEC work. No additional generally applicable UAS rule was located. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no operator-facing UAS requirement located | The Governor’s December 2024 direction to acquire counter-UAS detection systems was reviewed. It is an agency procurement direction and creates no additional private commercial flight rule, so it was not included as an authority record. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific controlling source located | Oklahoma appellate and Attorney General materials were searched. No controlling state decision or formal opinion specifically applying §§ 3-322 or 21-1743 to ordinary commercial AEC operations was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Reviewed — no additional operator permit located | The Oklahoma Department of Aerospace and Aeronautics is the statutory UAS/AAM clearinghouse under § 3-421 and maintains a state-agency UAS registry, but it does not publish a separate general commercial flight permit. ODOT UAS benefit and mission-support material adds no outside-operator requirement. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | Oklahoma State Parks requires a Director-issued Event Permit for drone use and expressly prohibits unpermitted takeoff or landing. No separate current UAS-specific hunting or fishing statute material to commercial AEC work was located. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Section 3-322 governs qualifying critical infrastructure facilities and S.B. 1441 changes the consequences beginning November 1, 2026. No separate current correctional-facility or emergency-scene UAS restriction was located. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | Section 21-1743 directly regulates intentional drone surveillance, privacy intrusion, specified private-property entry, device installation, and unauthorized landing, with narrow incidental-business and emergency-landing exceptions. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Reviewed — no enacted applicable UAS-specific source located | S.B. 488 proposed foreign-drone purchase restrictions but did not enact. Current state procurement and security materials revealed no statewide UAS manufacturer, country-of-origin, component, or cybersecurity restriction applicable to commercial consultants. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Engineering and surveying statutes, rules, mapping standards, and board materials were searched. General airborne photogrammetry standards contain no direct UAS provision and were excluded under the scope gate. |
| 11 | State preemption | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | No current Oklahoma statute expressly preempting municipal or county UAS rules was located. Local ordinance research remains deferred. |

## Open verification items

1. Map the complete § 3-322 facility definition against each proposed mission and confirm whether the asset is fenced or posted in the manner the statute requires.
2. For a critical-infrastructure mission, document the specific subsection C exception being used, including the client or facility relationship, written consent, and applicable FAA authorization; do not assume Part 107 status alone resolves every fact.
3. Recheck § 3-322 and § 21-1792 for missions on or after November 1, 2026, when S.B. 1441 begins attaching the separate critical-infrastructure penalty framework.
4. Define collection boundaries and camera practices that avoid intentional surveillance or observation of people in private places, and document landowner or lessee consent for any planned private-property landing.
5. Before a state-park mission, identify the current Director-issued Event Permit application, submission route, fee, insurance terms, processing time, designated locations, and any park-specific conditions.
