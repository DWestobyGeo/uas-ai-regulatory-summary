# Virginia UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Virginia (VA)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current Titles 2.2, 5.1, 15.2, 18.2, and 19.2 and the 2025–26 updates were reviewed. Applicable UAS provisions are included, including 2025 critical-infrastructure and defense-facility amendments and the 2026 public-body-use amendment. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | Current Virginia Aviation Board, Department of Conservation and Recreation, and Department of Wildlife Resources UAS rules are included. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | The current governor's executive-action index and in-force prior executive actions were searched. No current operator-facing statewide UAS executive order was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Applicable source found | No controlling Virginia appellate decision specifically applying current state UAS law to ordinary commercial AEC operations was located. Attorney General Opinion 22-046 directly construes the local-regulation limits in § 15.2-926.3 and is included. The superseded 2014 temporary-moratorium opinion was not treated as current authority. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | Section 5.1-5 exempts unmanned aircraft from Virginia aircraft registration. The Aviation Board's Part VIII controls how political subdivisions may regulate takeoffs and landings on their property; no separate state commercial-pilot credential was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | 4VAC5-30-400 prohibits voluntary landing or unlawful operation of UAS within state parks. 4VAC15-20-240 prohibits unauthorized drone use on Department of Wildlife Resources lands, hunting-related use, and wildlife harassment, with stated agency and wildlife-management exceptions. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Section 18.2-121.3 covers correctional facilities, federal UAS security restrictions, critical infrastructure, utilities, maritime-security facilities, and contracted defense facilities. Section 19.2-60.1 regulates public-body investigative use and weaponized public UAS. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | Sections 18.2-121.3, 18.2-130.1, 18.2-324.2, and 19.2-60.1 contain current UAS-specific trespass, peeping, protected-person, and public-body surveillance provisions. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable source found | Section 2.2-5515 prohibits specified state agencies and law-enforcement departments from acquiring or purchasing weaponized UAVs and restricts continued use absent a waiver. No enacted statewide manufacturer, country-of-origin, or general insecure-UAS procurement ban was located. The broader 2025 HB 2038 proposal failed. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Current Virginia engineering and surveying board materials were searched; no UAS-specific rule, opinion, or bulletin was located. General professional-practice requirements were excluded under the scope gate. |
| 11 | State preemption | Applicable source found | Section 15.2-926.3 broadly preempts political-subdivision regulation of privately owned UAS while allowing rules for takeoff and landing on subdivision-owned property under Aviation Board procedures and statutory exceptions. Opinion 22-046 confirms that this does not authorize local zoning restrictions on private-property launches and landings. |

## Open verification items

1. Before using state-park property, confirm with the named park whether DCR will authorize the proposed government, contractor, research, emergency, or other mission and what written instrument it uses; 4VAC5-30-400 does not state a general commercial UAS application process.
2. Before using Department of Wildlife Resources land, confirm whether the director or designee will authorize the specific non-wildlife AEC mission and what documentation and lead time are required.
3. For political-subdivision property, check the Department of Aviation's current published ordinance/regulation inventory and the particular property's map, hours, and safety or natural-resource conditions. Local-rule research itself remains deferred.
4. For infrastructure or defense-facility imaging, confirm the facility classification, property boundary, authorization, and whether imagery could reveal controlled technical information; the 2025 defense-facility provision applies notwithstanding the general exceptions in subsections A–C.
5. Recheck future sessions for an enacted successor to failed 2025 HB 2038 or another manufacturer, country-of-origin, component, or cybersecurity restriction.
