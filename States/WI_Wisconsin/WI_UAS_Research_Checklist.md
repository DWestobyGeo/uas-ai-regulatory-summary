# Wisconsin UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Wisconsin (WI)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current Chapters 29, 114, 175, 941, and 942 were reviewed together with the 2025–26 session. The register includes the existing drone provisions and 2025 Wisconsin Acts 189 and 194, enacted in April 2026. No other enacted 2025–26 UAS measure applicable to commercial AEC operations was located. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | Wis. Admin. Code NR 45.04(1)(c) expressly restricts uncrewed-aircraft activity on listed DNR properties to areas posted for use. Current DNR hunting materials also expressly prohibit hunting with the aid of drones. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Current gubernatorial executive-order materials were searched. Executive Order 184 concerns cybersecurity risks on state-issued devices generally but does not expressly regulate UAS or commercial UAS procurement, so it was not entered as a UAS authority. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Applicable source found | *Brown v. Kemp*, 86 F.4th 745 (7th Cir. 2023), invalidated Wis. Stat. 29.083(2)(a)7's hunter-monitoring restrictions while leaving the statute's physical-interference provisions outside the challenge. No separate controlling Wisconsin appellate UAS decision or attorney-general opinion applicable to ordinary commercial operations was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | WisDOT's UAS page identifies state aircraft statutes applicable to drones. WisDOT Standard Contract Provision 107.27 establishes a detailed written-approval, safety, insurance, documentation, and equipment-compliance process when incorporated into a highway contract. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | NR 45.04(1)(c), DNR's flying-activities page, current hunting regulations, Wis. Stat. 29.083, and the limiting decision in *Brown v. Kemp* are included. No broader statewide public-land UAS permit system was located. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Wis. Stat. 114.045 prohibits unauthorized correctional-institution overflight and now adds school-property and utility-facility restrictions. Wis. Stat. 175.55 governs Wisconsin law-enforcement drone surveillance. No separate statewide emergency-management permit was located. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | Wis. Stat. 942.10 directly prohibits intentional drone observation or recording of a person where the person has a reasonable expectation of privacy. Wis. Stat. 114.04 addresses low interference, danger, and nonconsensual landing; 175.55 addresses law-enforcement surveillance. General tort theories were not entered without an official UAS-specific application. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable source found | WisDOT ASP-6 section 107.27.1(5), effective with February 2026 lettings, requires UAS and components used under covered contracts to comply with American Security Drone Act and OMB M-26-02 federal guidelines. No generally applicable statewide commercial-fleet manufacturer or country-of-origin restriction was located. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Wisconsin DSPS professional land-surveyor and engineer pages, examination materials, statutes, and rules were searched. No UAS-specific professional-board rule, opinion, or bulletin was located. General surveying and engineering practice rules remain outside the register absent an official UAS application. |
| 11 | State preemption | Applicable source found | Wis. Stat. 114.105 generally bars cities, villages, towns, and counties from regulating drone ownership or operation except for strictly conforming ordinances and limits on their own governmental drone use. |

## Open verification items

1. Confirm which posted DNR areas, if any, currently authorize UAS activity under NR 45.04(1)(c), and obtain property-specific written direction before launching from a listed property.
2. For a WisDOT mission, confirm that the project contract incorporates the current ASP-6 and obtain the engineer's current application format; section 107.27 states required content but does not provide a single statewide form in the source reviewed.
3. Confirm how WisDOT evaluates American Security Drone Act and OMB M-26-02 compliance for a proposed aircraft, controller, payload, and component set, including what documentary evidence the engineer will accept.
4. For school-property work, confirm the identity of the school authority or designee that can grant express authorization and preserve the response to the request; the statute requires a response within five business days but does not prescribe a statewide form.
5. For work near a listed utility facility, verify the parcel boundary, facility classification, altitude, and whether the operation falls within the commercial/FAA-compliance exception before relying on that exception.
