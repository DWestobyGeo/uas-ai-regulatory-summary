# New Mexico UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** New Mexico (NM)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Reviewed — no enacted general UAS statute located | Current New Mexico statutes and legislative materials were searched for UAS, drone, unmanned aircraft, privacy, infrastructure, and commercial-operation provisions. The official 2026 S.B. 136 page shows the broad surveillance and critical-infrastructure proposal died; earlier drone bills were also not treated as law. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable sources found | Current NMAC materials were searched. 19.31.10 NMAC directly governs drone use affecting protected species and hunting; 12.8.2 NMAC expressly includes data from UAV/UAS sensors in New Mexico's professional-surveying standards. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable commercial-operator requirement located | Current executive-order materials were searched. Public-safety and National Guard drone-program actions were not converted into commercial operator records because they do not establish a general state requirement for AEC missions. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no controlling UAS-specific authority located | New Mexico appellate and Attorney General materials were searched for construction of UAS-specific state law. No controlling published decision or formal Attorney General opinion applicable to ordinary commercial AEC operations was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Reviewed — no separate UAS-specific state permit located | NMDOT, state aviation, right-of-way, and permitting materials were searched. Ground occupancy, traffic control, filming, or property access may require project-specific approval, but no general New Mexico commercial-UAS flight permit was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable sources found | State Parks' current alerts and 19.5.2.9(C) NMAC establish park-specific posted restrictions rather than one categorical statewide rule. Game Commission rule 19.31.10 directly restricts wildlife harassment and drone-derived hunting assistance. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Reviewed — no enacted general commercial restriction located | Agency materials and legislative sources were searched. Proposed 2026 S.B. 136 would have created a critical-infrastructure offense but died and is not law. No separate current statewide commercial critical-infrastructure buffer was located. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Reviewed — wildlife-specific source found; no enacted general UAS privacy statute located | The wildlife-harassment rule is recorded. General property, tort, privacy, voyeurism, and criminal laws may apply factually, but were not restated as UAS records without a direct UAS hook. The surveillance provisions in 2026 S.B. 136 did not become law. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Institution-specific source found; no statewide restriction located | State procurement and risk-management materials were searched. No enacted statewide UAS manufacturer or foreign-vendor prohibition was located. NMSU separately requires EHS&RM review of grant or contract terms involving UAS purchase or flight for federally banned or state-restricted systems. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Applicable source found | The Board's 12.8.2 NMAC standards expressly include UAV/UAS sensor data within digital geospatial data and apply surveying classification, competency, accuracy, reporting, supervision, certification, and recordation requirements according to the professional service performed. |
| 11 | State preemption and local authority | Reviewed — no express blanket UAS preemption located | No current statute expressly occupying the entire field of local UAS regulation was located. Local enactments remain outside this state-level pass and must not be inferred from the absence of a statewide general drone statute. |

## Open verification items

1. Classify each geospatial deliverable before collection and confirm whether New Mexico professional-surveying licensure, responsible charge, accuracy, certification, or recordation applies.
2. Check current State Parks alerts and the exact park page immediately before mobilization; obtain written superintendent or agency direction if the proposed operation conflicts with or is not resolved by the posted information.
3. For NMSU work, obtain a sponsoring department, current tier requirements, evidence of insurance and credentials, the safety plan, and final EHS&RM authorization within the stated lead time.
4. For UNM work, submit the current Risk Services form at least two weeks ahead with the requested pilot, authorization, insurance, commercial acknowledgement, and flight-plan materials.
5. Recheck enacted legislation for any successor to S.B. 136 and recheck procurement directives before purchasing equipment for public-sector work.
