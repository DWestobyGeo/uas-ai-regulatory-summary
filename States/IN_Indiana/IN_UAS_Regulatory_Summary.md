## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Indiana regulates UAS through a cluster of targeted criminal statutes rather than one omnibus drone act, several of which are private-operator-facing and already in force — a notable contrast with states whose comparable provisions remain only proposed. IC 35-33-5-9 requires a search warrant before a law-enforcement officer may use a UAV on or over private property or to surveil it. Separately, Indiana's Title 35 contains three private-operator-facing UAV offenses: remote aerial voyeurism (IC 35-45-4-5(g)-(h)), remote aerial harassment (IC 35-45-10-6) — recently and substantially expanded, effective July 1, 2026, to reach drone-based harassment of livestock, crop damage, and interference with farm operations after a wave of farmer complaints tied to avian-flu fears — and public safety remote aerial interference (IC 35-44.1-4-10), which bars using a UAV to obstruct law enforcement, fire, EMS, or search-and-rescue personnel. Indiana's 2024 Senate Enrolled Act 182 added UAV-specific contraband-trafficking offenses at correctional facilities (IC 35-44.1-3-5, -3-6), and a long-standing wildlife statute (IC 14-22-6-16) bars using a UAV to scout game during a defined pre-season/season window, subject to an agricultural-monitoring exception — a statute now under its first-ever prosecution (2026). On state land, Indiana DNR's administrative rule (312 IAC 8-2-8(i)) is an unqualified prohibition on UAV landing/launch on DNR property, though DNR's own guidance describes a discretionary licensing practice outside the rule's text. A new counter-UAS statute (IC 10-22, 2026) designates the Indiana State Police as the statewide coordinating agency for federally-authorized detection and mitigation activity near critical infrastructure, correctional facilities, and airports, though its substantive authority remains contingent on a federal trigger that had not been confirmed as satisfied as of this research date. Governor Braun's Executive Order 25-73 (October 2025) created a state drone-industry task force, not an operator-facing rule. No statewide preemption of local UAS ordinances, no UAS-specific Attorney General opinion or court decision, and no general state-agency foreign-manufacturer procurement restriction (of the kind Illinois' IDOT has adopted) were located.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Unmanned Aerial Vehicles; Search Warrant; Exceptions — IC 35-33-5-9
*Binding law | Current, originally P.L.170-2014, most recently amended by P.L.136-2019*

**Objective Summary:** Requires a law enforcement officer to obtain a search warrant to use a UAV (1) on or over private property, or (2) to search, surveil, photograph, or video private property or persons/items/structures on private property, without the affected owner's consent. A warrant is not required if one would not be required for the same search conducted without a UAV. This statute governs law-enforcement conduct only; it does not directly regulate private/commercial UAS operators.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Design acquisition and data-handling workflows to minimize unnecessary capture of people and private activity. Confirm the source-supported consent or project-purpose basis before flight and carry that limitation through processing, access control, retention, and delivery.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document the applicability analysis and any exception relied upon in the project file. Align the scope, client representations, and operator procedures with the verified authority, and seek state counsel if material facts fall outside its clear terms.
### Remote Aerial Voyeurism — IC 35-45-4-5(g)-(h)
*Binding law | Current, remote-aerial-voyeurism subsections added by P.L.107-2017*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it a Class A misdemeanor (Level 6 felony for a repeat offense, or if the material is published, posted online, or transmitted to another person) for a person, with intent to peep, to operate a UAV so that it enters the airspace above or surrounding another person's occupied dwelling to capture images, photographs, video, or audio of that person while inside the dwelling or on adjoining land not visible from a public area, without consent. This is a subsection of Indiana's broader voyeurism statute and is distinct from the separate remote aerial harassment offense below.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Do not operate a UAV with intent to peep or capture images of a person inside an occupied dwelling or on adjoining land not visible from a public area, without consent — this is a felony-eligible offense distinct from the broader voyeurism statute. Routine AEC survey, inspection, and mapping photography is not the conduct this subsection targets, but frame flight paths and camera angles to avoid incidental capture into private dwelling interiors, and never publish, post, or transmit any such incidental capture given subsection (h)'s felony-elevation trigger for publication.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Treat the cited authority as a documented compliance gate where applicable. Preserve the facts supporting any exception or consent theory, allocate client and operator responsibilities clearly, and seek state counsel when project facts approach ambiguous or penal language.

### Trafficking With an Inmate / Trafficking With an Inmate Outside a Facility (UAV-delivered contraband) — IC 35-44.1-3-5, IC 35-44.1-3-6
*Binding law | Current, amended 2024 by P.L.22-2024 (Senate Enrolled Act 182, signed March 11, 2024)*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it trafficking with an inmate, a Class A misdemeanor (Level 5 felony for certain contraband), to deliver, carry, or receive an article to or from an inmate or juvenile-facility resident without the facility head's prior authorization, expressly including delivery, carrying, or receipt "through the use of an unmanned aerial vehicle" (Sec. 5). A parallel offense (Sec. 6) covers delivering or placing contraband by UAV for an inmate being transported outside a facility. Both sections were amended in 2024 after the Indiana Department of Correction reported more than 30 drone sightings and over a dozen confirmed drone-delivered contraband drops at state correctional facilities in 2023.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Secure the facility head's prior written authorization before any UAV flight at or near a correctional or juvenile facility, regardless of the flight's purpose — the statute's prior-authorization requirement is the only avoidance mechanism and applies to any person, not just inmates or visitors. Confirm the facility's specific point of contact, required lead time, and any site-specific conditions well before mobilization.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Contact the protected facility's owner, operator, administrator, commander, or other official identified by the authority before mobilization and ask whether the proposed AEC mission qualifies for written approval or an employment/contractor exception. Provide the site, purpose, dates, flight area, crew and aircraft details the facility requests, and retain the signed authorization and security conditions; do not assume ordinary site access or a client work order is sufficient.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Make the required approval and compliance with its conditions a documented mobilization gate. Allocate responsibility for applications and schedule impacts in the contract, and obtain counsel if the project's facts do not fit the stated authorization pathway.
### Public Safety Remote Aerial Interference — IC 35-44.1-4-10
*Binding law | Current, originally P.L.107-2017, amended by P.L.22-2024*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it a Class A misdemeanor (Level 6 felony on repeat offense) to operate a UAV in a manner intended to obstruct or interfere with a law enforcement officer (including a correctional officer), a firefighter, an emergency medical person, or a member of a search-and-rescue team or mission, while that individual is performing official duties. The 2024 amendment added correctional officers to the list of covered officials. This is a private-operator-facing offense already in force in Indiana, distinct from and broader than the law-enforcement-only search-warrant statute above.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Screen the project map for protected facilities and active emergency operations before dispatch. Establish conservative stand-off and escalation points, brief the crew on the applicable restriction, and document any source-supported authorization before entering the affected area.

### Use of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles to Aid Hunting — IC 14-22-6-16
*Binding law | Current, originally P.L.111-2016, most recently amended by P.L.25-2024*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits knowingly using a UAV to search for, scout, locate, or detect a wild animal covered by a hunting season, as an aid to taking it, during a window beginning 14 days before that season and ending at the close of legal hunting hours on the season's last day. UAV use with infrared capability to locate and recover an already-legally-taken animal is expressly permitted, but does not authorize entering another's property without permission. Exceptions cover DNR and its designees, government agents performing official duties, educational/research institution personnel for bona fide purposes, veterinary care of a specific wild animal, and monitoring of agricultural production or nuisance wild animals.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Plan environmental and mapping flights to avoid conduct that could be characterized as locating, pursuing, disturbing, or harassing wildlife. Coordinate mission timing and stand-off distances with the land manager when project work overlaps sensitive habitat or hunting activity.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### Indiana Department of Natural Resources — Airborne-Device Prohibition on DNR Property (312 IAC 8-2-8(i))
*Binding administrative regulation | Current, most recently amended/readopted 2024; exception practice RESOLVED in this retrofit pass*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits a person from landing, taxiing, taking off, parking, or mooring an unmanned motor-driven airborne device (or an airborne human transportation device) on DNR-owned, -leased, or -managed property — including state parks, state forests, state recreation areas, nature preserves, fish and wildlife areas, and reservoir properties — except at a site the Department has designated for that purpose. The rule's own text contains no enumerated exceptions. This retrofit pass located and directly fetched DNR's own official implementing guidance — State Form 56490's "Commercial Film and Photography Application and Agreement Instructions" — resolving the citation/discrepancy flag left open in the original pass. DNR's guidance confirms drones are "generally prohibited on DNR properties to protect the privacy and safety of our guests and to protect the facilities and natural and cultural resources we conserve," but describes four specific exception categories, each still requiring advance permit-application review and an FAA license (three of four also require proof of insurance): (1) professional journalists covering natural/cultural-resource or agency stories; (2) university researchers under an appropriate DNR research permit; (3) documentary filmmakers producing a non-for-profit documentary in association with a news agency or nonprofit; and (4) state, regional, or county tourism agencies producing promotional material. This is more precise and complete than the three-category description found on secondary drone-law aggregator sites (which omitted the documentary-filmmaker category).

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Do not assume any of the four DNR exception categories applies to standard AEC survey, mapping, or inspection work — none of them cover commercial engineering, construction, or infrastructure-inspection UAS use. Treat any DNR-property launch or landing for AEC purposes as requiring a site-specific request to the property manager well in advance (DNR recommends 90 days for filming/photography permits).
- **Agency Practitioner:** Contact the relevant DNR division directly based on property type: the Outreach Coordinator (317-233-3864) for state parks/reservoirs/recreation areas, the Assistant State Forester (317-232-4101) for state forests, or the Assistant Director for Public Lands (317-232-8059) for fish & wildlife areas and nature preserves. Submit the DNR Commercial Film and Photography Agreement and budget at least 90 days for review.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** DNR's discretionary exception practice is documented in agency guidance, not the rule itself, so treat it as agency policy subject to change rather than a codified right. Confirm current DNR practice directly before advising a client that any exception category applies to a specific AEC engagement.
### Indiana State Police — Counter-Unmanned Aircraft System Authority (IC 10-22)
*Binding law | Current (effective July 1, 2026); federal trigger condition now satisfied — see below*

**Objective Summary:** New IC Article 10-22, added by Senate Bill 227 (2026 Reg. Sess.), designates the Indiana State Police as the statewide coordinating agency for counter-UAS activities authorized under federal law. It authorizes the state police to seek federal authorization to acquire, deploy, and operate counter-UAS systems; to designate local law-enforcement "participating agencies"; to designate Indianapolis as a pilot-project location; and to designate "high-risk sites" including critical infrastructure, correctional facilities, airports, and large public/special-event-rated events. Counter-UAS systems must appear on the federal C-UAS Technology List. By its own terms, the article's substantive detection/mitigation authority applies only once a qualifying federal law is enacted and the Governor has published notice in the Indiana Register. **Update from this retrofit pass:** the federal-law half of that trigger has since been satisfied — DOJ and DHS published an interim final rule implementing the federal SAFER SKIES Act's counter-UAS authority for state, local, tribal, and territorial law enforcement and correctional agencies in the Federal Register on July 6, 2026 (effective July 1, 2026). Independent secondary reporting states Indiana's own state-law authority "became effective" March 4, 2026 in anticipation of this federal action. Direct confirmation that the Governor has published the required notice in the Indiana Register (the second half of the trigger) was not located in this pass.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** This article governs Indiana State Police and designated local law-enforcement counter-UAS authority, not private AEC operators — it does not restrict where or how an AEC firm may fly. At any designated "high-risk site" or in the Indianapolis pilot-project area, confirm with the site owner or event organizer whether counter-UAS detection or mitigation activity may be active before mobilizing, since a legitimate AEC flight could be misidentified as a threat, particularly now that the federal trigger condition has been satisfied.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** This is not a categorical purchasing ban, but it creates potential equipment-loss exposure near protected locations, now that the federal authorization trigger has been satisfied. Consider loss risk, insurance treatment, geofencing configuration, and replaceability when assigning aircraft to work in the affected environment.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Confirm current operational status directly with the Indiana State Police before assuming active counter-UAS detection or mitigation operations are underway at any specific Indiana site — the federal trigger is satisfied, but gubernatorial notice publication was not independently confirmed in this pass.
### Professional Licensing — Indiana Land Surveyors Statute (IC 25-21.5-1-7)
*Binding law | MAJOR FINDING in this retrofit pass — a specific photogrammetry/remote-sensing supervision requirement*

**Objective Summary:** Defines the "practice of surveying" broadly, and subsection (c) lists activities — including "the creation of maps and geo-referenced data bases representing authoritative locations for boundaries, fixed works, or topography, either by terrestrial surveying methods or by photogrammetric or GNSS locations" — that must be accomplished under the responsible charge of a professional surveyor unless specifically exempted under subsection (d). This retrofit pass fetched and read subsections (d) and (e) directly for the first time (the original pass located only the general framework via a secondary aggregator). Subsection (d) lists categories NOT included in the practice of surveying most relevant to AEC UAS work — general illustrative/educational maps, GIS transcription clearly not intended as authoritative, an organization's own internal facility-inventory maps/databases (with appropriate metadata and disclaimers), and natural-resource maps by foresters, geologists, soil scientists, or biologists. Separately, and previously unlocated, **subsection (e) provides that "the use of photogrammetric methods or similar remote sensing technology to perform any part of the practice of surveying... may be performed only under the direct control and supervision of a professional surveyor or professional photogrammetrist who maintain[s] a current title of 'Certified Photogrammetrist' from a national scientific organization"** — a specific supervision requirement directly applicable to UAS-derived photogrammetric survey products, independent of the subsection (c)/(d) authoritative-location analysis. No standalone Indiana Professional Licensing Agency or State Board of Registration for Professional Surveyors rule, advisory opinion, or FAQ specifically addressing UAS platforms (as distinct from photogrammetric methods generally) was located.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Classify each UAS-derived deliverable against subsections (c) through (e), not just a general competency framework. Authoritative boundary/fixed-works/topographic products must be produced under the responsible charge of a licensed PLS, OR under the direct control and supervision of a professional surveyor or a nationally certified photogrammetrist per subsection (e) — a distinct, separately-applicable requirement for photogrammetric/remote-sensing methods. General-purpose, non-authoritative maps or internal inventory databases with appropriate metadata and disclaimers (subsection (d)) may fall outside licensed practice, but document that classification decision.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Before relying on an unlicensed or exempt delivery path, give the Board a written description of the proposed UAS-derived product and confirm both whether it falls within subsection (c)/(d)'s authoritative-location analysis AND whether subsection (e)'s photogrammetrist-supervision requirement is separately satisfied. Retain the board's response with the project quality plan.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Subsection (e)'s supervision requirement applies specifically to photogrammetric and remote-sensing methods — squarely covering most UAS-derived mapping products — independent of whether the deliverable also falls within subsection (c)'s authoritative-location categories. Confirm which licensed or certified individual is exercising the required supervision for any UAS photogrammetry deliverable, and document that chain, before delivery.
### Governor's Office — Executive Order 25-73, Indiana Initiative for Drone Dominance Task Force
*Official agency policy | Task force established; statutory strategic-plan deadline of July 31, 2026 has passed*

**Objective Summary:** Governor Mike Braun signed Executive Order 25-73 (on or about October 29-30, 2025), establishing the "Indiana Initiative for Drone Dominance Task Force," a multi-agency, university, and industry body directed to develop a strategic plan supporting federal "American drone dominance" policy, drawing on assets such as NSWC Crane, Camp Atterbury, and Muscatatuck Urban Training Center. Stated priorities include a cross-sector aerial test corridor, expanding public-safety UAS applications at major events, strengthening Indiana's drone manufacturing supply chain, and exploring counter-UAS infrastructure. The task force owed its strategic plan and recommendations to the Governor by July 31, 2026. Indiana was separately designated a national drone test site in January 2026, consistent with the task force's test-corridor priority. A search in this retrofit pass, conducted one week after the statutory deadline, did not locate a published version of the completed strategic plan; its status should be confirmed directly with the Governor's office. This record previously combined this affirmative finding with several negative-finding categories (no AG opinion, no court decision, no general state-agency UAS procurement restriction beyond IC 10-22, no local-ordinance preemption statute); those categories have been moved to the checklist in this retrofit pass per `Agent_Instructions.v6.md` §5.2, since the prior combined record's own `source_type` field said "not included in the final source register."

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** This order does not itself impose an operator-facing requirement, but AEC firms with drone-manufacturing, testing, or supply-chain interests should monitor the task force's strategic plan (status not yet confirmed as published) for emerging state incentive programs or test-corridor access opportunities.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
