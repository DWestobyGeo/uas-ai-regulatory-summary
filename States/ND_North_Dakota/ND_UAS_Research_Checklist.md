# North Dakota UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** North Dakota (ND)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable sources found | Chapters 2-03, 2-05, 12.1-17, 20.1-01, and 29-29.4 were reviewed. The final 2025 chapter laws for H.B. 1429 and H.B. 1613 were checked to distinguish enacted language from introduced proposals. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable sources found | Parks and Recreation chapter 58-02-08 and Aeronautics Commission chapter 6-02-02 were reviewed. The former contains a direct UAS authorization rule; the latter supports the current unmanned aerial-applicator licensing framework. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no separate current commercial-operator requirement located | Current gubernatorial executive-order materials were searched for drone, unmanned aircraft, emergency, privacy, and procurement provisions. No enduring general commercial-flight rule was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no controlling UAS-specific construction located | North Dakota Supreme Court and Attorney General materials were searched for published construction of chapters 2-03, 12.1-17, 20.1-01, and 29-29.4 as applied to UAS. No controlling UAS-specific interpretation was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable sources found | Aeronautics Commission registration, aerial-application, and UAS pages were reviewed. NDDOT's current UAS survey guidelines establish project-specific technical and deliverable expectations. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable sources found | Parks and Recreation requires director-level authorization on Department lands. Wildlife statutes prohibit intentional aircraft harassment and expressly prohibit unmanned aerial vehicle use to disrupt lawful hunting. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Surveillance and emergency provisions found; no separate general facility buffer located | Chapter 29-29.4 covers law-enforcement surveillance and catastrophe exceptions. Corrections, emergency management, and critical-infrastructure materials were searched; no enacted statewide commercial UAS stand-off distance was located. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable sources found | Chapter 29-29.4 governs specified government surveillance. The 2025 harassment and stalking amendments expressly include remotely piloted aircraft. General low-flight interference and landing-consent rules are in chapter 2-03. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Public-fleet program found; no blanket private-use ban located | The 2025-2027 state UAS replacement appropriation and current university implementation pages were reviewed. They address state or federally funded equipment compliance and do not prohibit general private possession or operation. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Agency surveying and pesticide requirements found; no separate UAS professional-board rule located | NDDOT UAS survey requirements and pesticide/aerial-application credentials were recorded. Engineering, land-surveying, photogrammetry, and professional-board materials were searched; no separate UAS-specific licensure rule was located. |
| 11 | State preemption and local authority | Reviewed — no express blanket UAS preemption located | Current statutes and UAS amendments were searched for statewide local preemption. None was located. This does not validate any local rule and does not expand this pass into municipal or county research. |

## Open verification items

1. Confirm how chapter 2-03's broad aircraft provisions are currently applied to the specific UAS and operation, especially low-flight interference and landing consent.
2. For a government surveillance mission, obtain the agency's current warrant or exception analysis and written data-management instructions before collection.
3. Obtain the Parks and Recreation Department's current authorization process, lead time, insurance terms, property map, and site conditions.
4. Check current hunting seasons, wildlife restrictions, sensitive species, landowner access, and any Game and Fish project permit or coordination need.
5. For aerial application, validate the proposed aircraft weight and configuration, current NDAC rule text, application, aircraft registration, pilot/chief-pilot records, pesticide certificate, insurance, product label, and Part 137 authority.
6. For NDDOT work, reconcile the statewide guideline with the executed contract, project survey manual, accuracy specification, deliverable schema, and professional-responsibility requirements.
7. For NDSU property, confirm that the linked external-flight procedure remains controlling and obtain the current forms and approval in writing.
8. For public or federally funded work, check the solicitation and funding terms rather than assuming the state replacement program applies to a private fleet.
