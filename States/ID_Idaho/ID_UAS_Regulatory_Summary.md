## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Idaho's UAS regulatory framework centers on a single privacy statute (§21-213) that includes an express carve-out for mapping and resource-management flights, with no statewide UAS registration or business license requirement. The most significant recent development is a new correctional-facility airspace restriction (Idaho Code §20-251, effective July 1, 2026), enacted in response to drone-delivered contraband incidents. Idaho Fish and Game's public-land use rule expressly defines "aircraft" to include UAS, creating a binding launch/landing restriction on Wildlife Management Areas.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Idaho Code §21-213 — Restrictions on Use of Unmanned Aircraft Systems
*Binding law | Current, amended 2020*

**Objective Summary:** §21-213(1)(b)(ii) excludes UAS "used in mapping or resource management" from the statute's coverage. Absent that exclusion, §21-213(2)(a) bars using a UAS to surveil/record specifically targeted persons or private property (dwelling/curtilage or farm/ranch/commercial/industrial property) without written consent; §21-213(2)(b) bars non-consensual recording intended for publication. §21-213(4) permits a facility owner/easement holder to aerially inspect their own facilities on land owned by another. Enforcement is civil — a private right of action for the greater of $1,000 or actual damages plus attorney's fees, not a criminal prosecution.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Because the mapping/resource-management exclusion is purpose-based, log the survey/mapping purpose of each flight at planning time rather than after the fact — that record is what lets you rely on the exclusion instead of the consent requirement. The easement-inspection exemption directly supports utility/infrastructure inspection performed under a right of occupancy, so keep a copy of the easement or access agreement in the project file.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* This carve-out is a defense to a civil claim, not immunity from being sued — a property owner can still bring an action and force the firm to establish that the flight was genuinely for mapping/resource management. Keep contemporaneous flight logs, mission plans, and deliverable descriptions tied to each flight so the mapping purpose is documented before any dispute arises, not reconstructed afterward.

### Idaho Code §20-251 — UAS restricted airspace over Dept. of Correction facilities
*Binding law | New, effective July 1, 2026*

**Objective Summary:** Enacted by House Bill 522 (signed March 31, 2026). Makes it unlawful to operate a UAS within the "restricted airspace" of an Idaho Department of Correction facility — surface to 400 ft AGL within the facility's fenced/posted perimeter. Misdemeanor: up to $1,000 fine, up to 6 months jail, and/or drone forfeiture. Authorizes IDOC and law enforcement to use mitigation measures (e.g., jamming) against a drone operating "in a nefarious manner" in that airspace. Lack of knowledge that the area is a correctional facility is not a defense.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Map the facility perimeter and 400 ft AGL ceiling into pre-flight planning before any job near an Idaho correctional facility, and treat active jamming as a real operational risk to project equipment — not just a legal abstraction — since the statute authorizes it against drones in the restricted airspace.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* This is a brand-new statute that most third-party drone-law summaries won't yet reflect, so don't rely on older secondary sources. Obtain written IDOC coordination before any flight near a correctional facility; the "lack of knowledge is not a defense" provision means good-faith ignorance of a facility boundary will not protect the operator or the firm.

### Idaho Code §21-114 — Aircraft registration
*Binding law, but not UAS-specific*

**Objective Summary:** Weight-based registration ($0.03/lb, $20 min–$600 max) tied to FAA airworthiness certificates and physical decals; the framework is written for manned aircraft and does not reference drones/UAS. No primary source imposing a state-level UAS registration, license, or business permit in Idaho beyond FAA Part 107 was located.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* No state registration fee or process to build into equipment-onboarding checklists for Idaho at this time — confirm this remains current at the start of each fiscal year, since other states are actively adding UAS-specific registration.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Do not represent to a client that Idaho has no UAS registration requirement without a fresh check each contract cycle; this is an area of active legislative change nationally.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### Parks and Public Lands — Idaho Department of Parks and Recreation
*Binding administrative regulation*

**Objective Summary:** IDAPA 26.01.20 §.175.11: drone operation in a state park may be authorized by the park or program manager when it does not interfere with traditional park uses and is consistent with resource preservation — a discretionary, per-park determination, not a blanket ban or an automatic right.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Contact the specific park manager early in project scheduling — this is a discretionary, per-park approval, so lead time can vary widely by park.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Get the park manager's authorization in writing (email is sufficient) and keep it in the project file; a verbal go-ahead is not a reliable compliance record.

### Fish and Wildlife — Idaho Fish and Game Commission
*Binding administrative regulation*

**Objective Summary:** IDAPA 13.01.03.010.1 defines "Aircraft" to expressly include "any unmanned aircraft system." Rule 100.01.20 prohibits landing or launching aircraft on Department-owned/controlled land and water except on public airstrips, absent specific authorization from the Commission, Director, Regional Supervisor, or designee. A separate hunting-related restriction (Idaho Code Title 36 and IDFG rules, with a 2026 update reported in secondary sources) bars using aircraft/drones to locate big game for same-day hunting or to harass wildlife.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Treat any Wildlife Management Area or IDFG access site the same as a state park for launch/landing purposes — get prior authorization before staging a crew there for environmental monitoring or habitat-mapping work. Flying over WMA land from an off-site launch point avoids this specific rule but should still respect wildlife-harassment concerns.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Because "aircraft" is expressly defined to include UAS, do not treat this as a gray area — obtain and retain written IDFG authorization before any launch/landing on Department land, the same way as a state park permit. The hunting-related restriction carries low legal exposure for standard survey work but is worth flagging to field crews during hunting season.

### Professional Licensing — Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses (DOPL)
*Advisory guidance interpreting binding licensure statute*

**Objective Summary:** The Idaho Society of Professional Land Surveyors FAQ directly addresses whether a PLS or Certificate of Authorization is required to perform aerial surveys with drones, and points to a DOPL board guidance letter (dated November 24, 2015) as the clarifying answer. The letter was located but was not machine-readable in this research pass (scanned/image PDF).

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Get a readable copy of this letter before finalizing an Idaho staffing model — it directly determines whether a PLS or Certificate of Authorization needs to be on record for drone survey work, which affects hiring and subcontracting decisions.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Treat the letter as agency guidance interpreting the binding licensure statute (Idaho Code Title 54, Ch. 12), not a substitute for it; request written confirmation directly from DOPL if the letter's scanned content can't be verified, since relying on an unread guidance document is not a defensible compliance position.

### Procurement / Equipment Restrictions
*Category reviewed — no state-specific restriction located*

**Objective Summary:** No Idaho state law or executive order restricting government purchase/use of drones by manufacturer or country of origin was located. A federal executive order (June 2025) directs a Covered Foreign Entity List restricting federal agency/contractor UAS procurement, which may affect Idaho agencies receiving federal funding, but this is a federal action.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* No fleet-equipment restriction currently limits Idaho commercial project work; if bidding on federally funded Idaho projects, separately confirm current federal covered-list compliance requirements.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Do not represent to a client that Idaho has no equipment restrictions without noting the federal overlay for federally funded work — the state/federal distinction matters for contract representations.
