## 1. State overview

Wyoming's direct UAS authorities are targeted rather than consolidated into one drone act. The principal small-UAS issues located are the 2025 critical-infrastructure statute, the correctional-facility prohibition and WDOC request process, and wildlife/hunting restrictions. Wyoming also maintains a voluntary state Drone Hub notification channel. Title 10 contains additional unmanned-aircraft rules, but its definition expressly excludes federally defined “small unmanned aircraft,” so those provisions generally concern aircraft outside a typical under-55-pound Part 107 fleet.

No Wyoming-specific commercial small-UAS registration, operator license, or statewide equipment-manufacturer restriction was located in this pass. Private-property overflight and privacy remain partly governed by unsettled common-law principles described in an official legislative memorandum. State-park treatment of UAS under its general “aircraft” rule could not be confirmed and remains an open item.

## 2. Statewide laws

### WY-001 — Critical infrastructure and critical systems

**Authority:** W.S. 19-13-501 and 19-13-503
**Status:** Current / in force; enacted as 2025 Chapter 112 after a veto override

W.S. 19-13-503 prohibits specified intentional UAS conduct over or near a broadly defined list of critical infrastructure and critical systems, including energy, water, communications, rail, public-safety, correctional, military, hospital, mining, and manufacturing facilities. It lists exceptions for FAA-authorized compliant operations, government-owned UAS, owners or their agents, specified emergency users, and private landowners on their own property. A first offense is a misdemeanor and a later offense a felony; law enforcement may disable, damage, or remove a violating aircraft.

### WY-002 — Correctional-facility UAS prohibition

**Authority:** W.S. 6-5-214
**Status:** Current / in force

Except as authorized by the person in charge, W.S. 6-5-214 prohibits intentional UAS trespass over or in a penal institution, correctional facility, or its grounds for recording or surveillance, conveying contraband, or delivering a deadly weapon. Recording and contraband offenses are misdemeanors punishable by up to one year and $2,000; a deadly-weapon delivery offense is a felony punishable by up to ten years and $10,000. Specified federal, state, law-enforcement, and emergency uses are excluded, with reasonable-notice requirements for listed excluded uses.

### WY-004 — Aircraft use affecting wildlife

**Authority:** W.S. 23-3-306(a)
**Status:** Current / in force

W.S. 23-3-306 prohibits using aircraft to harass, pursue, hunt, shoot, or kill wildlife that requires a license or permit, is protected, or is federally threatened or endangered. It also prohibits using aircraft to aid the taking of wildlife—other than predatory animals—by spotting, locating, communicating, or otherwise assisting. For this section, “aircraft” expressly includes an unmanned aerial vehicle. Government agencies and their employees, contractors, or designees performing lawful duties are exempt; violations are classified as high misdemeanors.

### WY-006 — Large-UAS aeronautics and accident reporting

**Authority:** W.S. 10-1-101(a)(x), 10-3-201(j), and 10-3-301
**Status:** Current / in force; limited by statutory definition

Title 10 directs the Aeronautics Commission to adopt reasonable rules governing where statutory “unmanned aircraft” may take off and land, permits certain operating rules subject to federal limits, and requires immediate reporting to the Commission of aircraft accidents or injuries in Wyoming. The same title defines “unmanned aircraft” by reference to 14 C.F.R. 1.1 but expressly excludes “small unmanned aircraft” as federally defined. These provisions therefore do not establish a Wyoming accident-reporting or takeoff/landing rule for a typical under-55-pound small UAS.

### WY-007 — Low or dangerous flight and landing consent for large UAS

**Authority:** W.S. 10-1-101(a)(x) and 10-4-303
**Status:** Current / in force; limited by statutory definition

W.S. 10-4-303 states that flight by covered aircraft is lawful unless it is low enough to interfere with the owner's existing land or water use, is imminently dangerous to persons or property, or violates federal air-commerce rules. Landing on another's land or water without consent is unlawful except for a forced landing, with liability for actual forced-landing damage. An unlawful unmanned-aircraft operation under subsection (a) is a misdemeanor. Title 10's definition expressly excludes federally defined small unmanned aircraft, limiting this provision's UAS application.

### WY-008 — Official memorandum on small-UAS trespass and privacy

**Authority:** Wyoming Legislative Service Office, *Topic Summary: Drones and Trespass* (May 13, 2022), pp. 5–6
**Status:** Nonbinding official research; no controlling Wyoming UAS decision located

The official LSO memorandum describes small-UAS trespass law as unsettled. It states that Wyoming recognizes the common-law tort of intrusion upon seclusion and that the tort may apply to a UAS flying closely over private property when the intrusion into private affairs would be highly offensive to a reasonable person. It also explains that common-law aerial trespass may turn on entry into the immediate reaches of the airspace plus substantial interference with use and enjoyment, while noting that no Wyoming case on point was located and Title 10 excludes small UAS.

## 3. State agency requirements and guidance

### WY-003 — WDOC Form 366 request channel

**Authority:** Wyoming Department of Corrections public-records/media-request page
**Status:** Current agency procedure

WDOC's official webpage states that, under W.S. 6-5-214, the department has a response plan and a request form for UAS operations over a penal institution or correctional facility. It directs a requester to “WDOC Form #366 Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Request.” The public webpage does not state a review lead time, approval duration, required attachments, or decision criteria. The page establishes the department's request channel; the underlying statute makes authorization by the person in charge the operative exception.

### WY-005 — Game and Fish aircraft spotting rule

**Authority:** Wyoming Game and Fish Commission Chapter 2, Sections 2 and 12
**Status:** Current 2026 regulation; updated annually

Current Chapter 2 defines “aircraft” to include an unmanned aerial vehicle. Section 12 prohibits using aircraft with intent to spot, locate, and aid in taking any big-game animal from August 1 through January 31 of the following year. It also prohibits using aircraft to spot, locate, and aid in taking any trophy-game animal during an open season. The rule excludes ordinary aircraft operation when there is no attempt or intent to locate game. The Commission adopted the 2026 Chapter 2 regulation as proposed.

### WY-009 — Wyoming Drone Hub voluntary pre-flight notification

**Authority:** Wyoming Department of Homeland Security Drone Hub operator page
**Status:** Current advisory program; voluntary

The Wyoming Department of Homeland Security's Drone Hub asks operators to provide planned flight information so reported sightings can be compared with known lawful activity. Its operator page links a pre-flight notification form and frames the request as “Please let us know when/where you'll be flying,” not as a license, permit, or mandatory state filing. The page therefore documents a voluntary coordination channel rather than an operator authorization requirement. It is distinct from any FAA authorization and does not replace property-owner or state-agency approval.
