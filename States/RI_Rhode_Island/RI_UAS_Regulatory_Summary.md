## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Rhode Island directly incorporates applicable FAA requirements into state UAS law, attaches misdemeanor exposure, and limits municipal regulation of UAS operations. RIAC separately maintains an active aeronautics rule with a UAS-specific federal-compliance section and an all-aircraft state-registration framework whose small-UAS application mechanics are not clear from the conventional-aircraft form. On RIDEM parks and conservation management areas, UAS operation requires official written permission; State Parks restricts drone approvals to commercial use and uses a special-use permit process with substantial lead time.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### RI-001 — Statewide FAA-compliance requirement, penalty, and municipal preemption
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Rhode Island requires every operation of a qualifying UAV or UAS in the state to comply with all applicable FAA requirements. The statute defines covered UAVs as powered, unmanned, remotely piloted or autonomous vehicles weighing less than 55 pounds, and exempts operations under specified federal authority. A violation is subject to the misdemeanor penalty incorporated from § 1-4-19: a fine up to $500, imprisonment up to one year, or both. Municipalities may not enact or enforce UAS-operation ordinances except as otherwise authorized by FAA regulation or guidance or Rhode Island general law.

### RI-002 — Current Aeronautics Regulations UAS provision
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** The active Rhode Island Airport Corporation Aeronautics Regulations apply to civil aircraft operations and expressly define an unmanned aircraft as an aircraft operated without direct human intervention from within or on it. Section 1.17 requires all UAS operations to comply with federal statutes, rules, and regulations, including Part 107. RIAC may issue orders and take other authorized action to enforce the regulation and applicable Rhode Island aeronautics law. The current UAS-specific section does not state a separate RIAC flight permit, inspection-on-request duty, or operational checklist; those provisions appeared in older versions but were removed by the 2025 amendment.

### RI-003 — State registration of federal aircraft certificates
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force; UAS application mechanics unresolved*

**Objective Summary:** Section 1-4-6 requires owners and operators of all aircraft based or primarily used in Rhode Island to register their federal aircraft certificates with RIAC, and states that nonresidents operating commercially intrastate shall register. The annual fee for aircraft under 2,000 pounds is $30. The active RIAC rule defines unmanned aircraft as aircraft and directs covered owners and operators to submit the federal registration certificate, annual application, and fee. However, the rule and current published application use conventional-aircraft terms such as N-number and airworthiness or experimental certificate, leaving the exact filing and onboard-document mechanics for ordinary Part 107 small UAS unresolved.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### RI-004 — Public-reservation written permission and State Parks commercial drone permit process
*Permit or property-use requirement | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** RIDEM rules allow UAS operation on, over, or from a public reservation only with official written permission from the public reservation manager and prohibit using UAS to harass or disturb users, wildlife, or natural resources. A violation may be fined up to $100 for each violation. Rhode Island State Parks lists drone use as a special-use activity requiring a permit and restricts approval to commercial use. Applicants must first contact the relevant regional or park office; ordinary applications are due at least 30 business days in advance, large activities may require 90 days to one year, and final approval can depend on insurance and activity-specific conditions.
