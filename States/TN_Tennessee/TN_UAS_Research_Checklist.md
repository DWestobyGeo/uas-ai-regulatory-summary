# Tennessee UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Tennessee (TN)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Titles 4, 12, 39, 49, and 70 and the enacted 2025–2026 session were reviewed. The packet includes lawful image capture, privacy and image handling, events, fireworks, schools, correctional facilities, critical infrastructure, law enforcement, emergency management, procurement, and wildlife provisions. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | TDEC Rule 0400-02-02-.33 governs UAS activity in state park and natural areas. University of Tennessee Rule 1720-01-02-.06 governs University property. Current Fish and Wildlife Commission implementation materials for drone recovery were also reviewed. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Current Tennessee executive-order materials were searched. No current statewide operator-facing UAS executive order was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific controlling source located | Tennessee appellate and Attorney General materials were searched. No reported controlling Tennessee decision or formal opinion specifically applying the current UAS statutes to ordinary commercial AEC operations was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | TDOT’s official UAS compliance page was reviewed and used to cross-check the state privacy and law-enforcement provisions. Section 39-13-902 expressly covers TDOT and its contractors for transportation planning, location, design, construction, maintenance, and operations. No separate general Tennessee commercial-UAS registration or operator permit was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | TDEC’s park and natural-area permit rule, the Hunter Protection Act, the 2025 deer-recovery authorization, and current TWRA commercial recovery registration and reporting requirements are included. General hunting provisions with no UAS content were excluded. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Sections 39-13-903, 39-13-609, and 39-13-902 address correctional overflight, critical-infrastructure data collection, school surveillance, law-enforcement use, emergency response, and TEMA retention. The effective July 1, 2026 renumbering was tracked. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | Sections 39-13-902 through 905 define lawful capture, prohibited surveillance, image-possession and disclosure offenses, and evidentiary treatment. Section 70-4-302 addresses intentional drone surveillance of hunters and anglers. No separate UAS-specific statewide trespass statute was located. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable source found | Sections 4-56-112 and 12-4-120 prohibit covered public bodies from acquiring drones made by manufacturers banned under federal NDAA Section 889 and make violating contracts void. Current procurement templates and solicitations may add requirements not inferred here. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific board source located | Tennessee engineering, land-surveying, and related professional-board materials were searched. No current UAS-specific board rule or opinion was located. Section 39-13-902 expressly recognizes land surveying by a duly registered surveyor and certified photogrammetric mapping, but it is a criminal-law provision rather than a board rule. |
| 11 | State preemption | Reviewed — no express UAS-specific state preemption provision located | Section 39-13-907 only states that the UAS-surveillance part does not permit conduct prohibited by other law. No express Tennessee statute broadly preempting municipal or county UAS regulation was located. Local ordinance research remains deferred under this project’s current state-level scope. |

## Open verification items

1. Confirm the exact property boundary and obtain written facility permission before work near correctional facilities, schools, ticketed venues, fireworks sites, or critical infrastructure; several provisions use different consent language and exceptions.
2. For state parks and natural areas, confirm whether an Authorized Flight Zone exists and obtain the current permit form, lead time, fee, insurance limit, operating conditions, and any separate specimen or artifact permit.
3. For University of Tennessee work, confirm that the contract expressly covers UAS operation and obtain the applicable campus procedure and UAS Coordinator conditions before mobilization.
4. For public-client procurement, recheck the current federal Section 889 manufacturer list and the client’s solicitation and contract terms; the Tennessee statutes incorporate federal restrictions by reference.
5. Confirm whether current agency or project contracts impose data-retention or deletion terms beyond the specific statutory limits for TEMA and law-enforcement records.
6. Recheck the codified 2026 version of § 39-13-903 when Tennessee publishes it online so the school, correctional, and critical-infrastructure subdivision numbers can be confirmed in the consolidated code.
