## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Tennessee affirmatively recognizes many common commercial AEC imaging activities, including mapping, registered land surveying, utility and pipeline inspection, FAA-authorized commercial service, certified photogrammetry, public-property imaging, and work for TDOT. That permission operates alongside criminal rules for surveillance and image use, large events, fireworks, correctional facilities, schools, and critical-infrastructure data. Separate public-client rules govern law-enforcement and emergency-management data and restricted public procurement. Commercial work in state parks or natural areas generally needs a written TDEC permit outside an Authorized Flight Zone, while University of Tennessee projects are governed by a property rule with a contract exclusion and campus procedures.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations
### TN-001 — Lawful capture of images — mapping, surveying, utilities, commercial services, transportation, and other stated purposes
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Section 39-13-902 makes UAS image capture lawful for enumerated purposes that include mapping; surveying by a duly registered land surveyor; utility operations, inspection, vegetation management, routing, and siting; owner or lawful-occupant consent; public-property imagery; pipeline work; FAA-authorized commercial services; TDOT planning, design, construction, maintenance, and operations; and certified photogrammetric mapping. Images may be used only for the lawful purpose for which they were captured. Incidental images of private persons or property from specified categories remain subject to § 39-13-905.

### TN-002 — Unlawful surveillance imagery; possession, distribution, and evidentiary treatment
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Subject to § 39-13-902, § 39-13-903 prohibits capturing an image of an individual or privately owned real property with intent to conduct surveillance and knowingly using an image for a prohibited purpose. Section 39-13-904 separately penalizes possession and disclosure or use of an unlawfully captured image, with each image a separate offense and prompt-cessation or destruction defenses. Section 39-13-905 generally makes unlawful or qualifying incidental imagery unavailable in proceedings, public-records disclosure, discovery, or subpoena, except when used to prove a violation of the UAS-surveillance part.

### TN-003 — Open-air ticketed events and fireworks sites
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Without the venue owner or operator’s consent, § 39-13-903 prohibits intentionally capturing an image of a person or event at, or dropping any item or substance into, an open-air venue where more than 100 people are gathered for a ticketed event. It also prohibits knowingly operating within or over a designated fireworks discharge site, display site, or fallout area during the event without the event owner or operator’s consent. Following the July 1, 2026 renumbering, these remain Class C misdemeanor provisions.

### TN-004 — Unmanned aircraft over correctional-facility grounds
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Tennessee prohibits knowingly using an unmanned aircraft over the grounds of a correctional facility. Public Chapter 868 inserted the school provision as a new subdivision (a)(5), so the correctional-facility rule is subdivision (a)(6) beginning July 1, 2026. The offense remains a Class C misdemeanor. Unlike adjacent event, fireworks, school, and critical-infrastructure provisions, the correctional-facility text does not state a general owner-consent, commercial-purpose, or FAA-authorization exception for an ordinary private contractor.

### TN-005 — Critical-infrastructure surveillance and data collection
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Without the business operator’s written consent, § 39-13-903 prohibits knowingly using a UAS within 250 feet of a listed critical-infrastructure perimeter to surveil, gather evidence or information, or record critical-infrastructure data. Covered facilities include electric systems, refineries, hazardous-substance manufacturing, chemical and rubber plants, petroleum or chemical storage, water and wastewater treatment, natural-gas or propane infrastructure, nonpublic railroad facilities, and communications facilities. The Class E felony provision does not prohibit commercial UAS operations conducted in compliance with FAA authorization. Public Chapter 868 renumbered it to subdivision (a)(7) on July 1, 2026.

### TN-006 — Freedom from Unwarranted Surveillance Act — law-enforcement drone use and data
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Section 39-13-609 treats covered law-enforcement drone collection as a search and requires FAA compliance plus a warrant or recognized exception, unless an enumerated path applies. Those paths include specified threats, imminent danger, fugitive or hostage operations, missing-person searches, public-area crash investigation, crimes on public property, fire investigation, consent-based event security, declared natural disasters, and crime-scene investigation. Collected data generally must be deleted within 15 business days unless directly relevant to the lawful mission, investigation, or prosecution. Violations can trigger exclusion, destruction, damages, injunctions, and attorney fees.

### TN-007 — Emergency-management and state-authority image capture and retention
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Section 39-13-902 authorizes state or local authorities and persons under contract with or acting for state authorities to capture imagery for catastrophe assessment, declared-emergency safety, property protection, damage or contamination assessment, and routine legally authorized air-quality monitoring. It separately authorizes TEMA emergency-management imagery, including disaster coordination and preliminary damage assessment. TEMA may retain damage-assessment images for no more than one year, or the FEMA-required period after a presidential major-disaster declaration, and may retain other images for no more than 15 business days. Incidental private-property imagery is governed by § 39-13-905.

### TN-008 — Unmanned aircraft surveillance over public K–12 school grounds
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Effective July 1, 2026, Public Chapter 868 makes it a Class C misdemeanor to knowingly use an unmanned aircraft over the grounds of a public school serving any K–12 grade with intent to conduct surveillance of the school grounds, without permission from a school administrator, the director of schools, or the director’s designee. The enacted provision does not apply to a law-enforcement officer acting within lawful duties or to a commercial-purpose operator authorized by the FAA to conduct operations in that airspace.

### TN-009 — Public-agency drone procurement restrictions
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Sections 4-56-112 and 12-4-120 prohibit state agencies and defined local or law-enforcement agencies from purchasing or acquiring a drone produced by a manufacturer banned under Section 889 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2019, as amended. The local-agency definition includes counties, municipalities, their branches or agencies, public utilities, utility districts, and interlocal entities. A contract or agreement for a purchase or acquisition that violates either section is void and unenforceable. The statutes regulate public acquisition and do not expressly prohibit every privately owned aircraft used by a consultant.

### TN-012 — Hunter Protection Act — drone surveillance of hunters and anglers
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** The Hunter Protection Act makes it a Class C misdemeanor to use a drone with intent to conduct video surveillance of private citizens who are lawfully hunting or fishing without first obtaining their written consent. The Act applies to individuals, firms, companies, partnerships, corporations, public or private organizations, institutions, and similar entities. Courts may enjoin prohibited conduct and award compensatory damages, punitive damages when appropriate, litigation costs, and reasonable attorney fees. The rule targets intentional surveillance rather than every incidental appearance of a hunter or angler in unrelated project imagery.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### TN-010 — Unmanned Aircraft Systems in park areas and natural areas
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Rule 0400-02-02-.33 prohibits UAS launch, landing, or operation in a Tennessee park area or natural area unless the entire activity stays inside an Authorized Flight Zone or the Commissioner first issues a signed UAS flight permit. An application must identify the pilot, times, locations, activity, impacts, purpose, FAA authority, liability acceptance, and requested additional information. Commercial applicants must state intended data or media use and may need liability insurance naming Tennessee as an additional insured. Permits are discretionary, location- and time-specific, revocable, and may carry fees and conditions. Official government contractors receive a stated exemption.

### TN-011 — Use of Unmanned Aircraft on University property
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Rule 1720-01-02-.06 restricts UAS use on University of Tennessee property. Among other limits, it prohibits operation inside buildings, on or above University-owned streets or sidewalks, above nonparticipants lacking cover, around home football games, outside official daylight hours, or contrary to federal or state law. The rule does not apply to a University unit or a person or entity with which the University has contracted to operate a UAS, although those operations must still comply with federal and state law. Campus procedures and the University UAS Coordinator may add project-specific controls.

### TN-013 — Big Game Drone Recovery — current operator requirements
*Official agency policy, permit, or guidance with operative requirements | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** TWRA permits drones only to recover deer reasonably believed mortally wounded, from 30 minutes after sunset through 3:00 a.m., over private property with full landowner permission. A quota elk hunter may use a drone on North Cumberland WMA with manager approval. Pilots need a current FAA Remote Pilot Certificate, and the aircraft must have thermal imaging; neither pilot nor hunter may possess a hunting weapon during use. Commercial recovery operators must complete a one-time no-fee ReadyOp registration, submit specified information before each attempt, and report afterward. Drones may not be used to hunt or create an unfair advantage.
