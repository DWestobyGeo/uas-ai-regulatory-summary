## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Mississippi regulates UAS through a criminal statute targeting critical-infrastructure and correctional-facility surveillance (the Mississippi Unmanned Aircraft Systems Protection Act of 2021, Miss. Code Ann. §§ 97-47-1 to 97-47-9), a separate 2023 statute that both authorizes recreational/commercial UAS operation and preempts most local UAS ordinances (the Uncrewed Aircraft Systems' Rights and Authorities Act, Miss. Code Ann. §§ 61-21-1 to 61-21-11), and a distinctive state-procurement statute requiring domestically manufactured small UAS (and banning Chinese-manufactured UAS) for state and political-subdivision purchases and state-contracted infrastructure-inspection services (Miss. Code Ann. § 31-7-67). Unlike Minnesota, Mississippi has affirmatively preempted local UAS ordinances (subject to enumerated exceptions for government-owned UAS, FAA-enforcement ordinances, generally applicable non-UAS-specific nuisance/trespass ordinances, and property-owner launch/landing control), and does not impose a state-level UAS registration or commercial-operator license beyond what the FAA requires. Substantive UAS restrictions exist for Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks (MDWFP)-managed Wildlife Management Areas (launch/landing prohibition) and state parks (aircraft-operation prohibition absent written Executive Director permission), and for alligator hunting (drone-assisted location/pursuit/take prohibition). No UAS-specific guidance from Mississippi's professional engineering/surveying licensing board, no MDOT Aeronautics Division UAS-specific policy, no gubernatorial executive order, no Attorney General opinion, and no state appellate court decision addressing UAS were located in this pass. A notable and recurring pattern in this research: several UAS-related bills — a 2023 bill (HB 839) that would have created much of the preemption/rights framework that a companion Senate bill (SB 2146) ultimately did enact, and 2024/2025 bills (HB 1370, HB 1494) that would have added UAS-specific language to Mississippi's general trespass statutes and to the licensed-surveyor land-entry statute — were reported in secondary sources in ways that could be mistaken for current law; all of the trespass/surveyor-entry bills specifically died in committee and are NOT currently enacted (see Section 5).

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Mississippi Unmanned Aircraft Systems Protection Act of 2021 (Miss. Code Ann. §§ 97-47-1 to 97-47-9)
`Binding statute (criminal) | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Section 97-47-5 makes it an offense to knowingly (a) use a UAS to conduct surveillance of, collect information or data from, or photographically/electronically record a "critical infrastructure" or "correctional facility" without the prior written consent of the owner or owner's designee, or (b) deliver or attempt to deliver contraband by UAS onto correctional-facility property or adjacent property. "Critical infrastructure" is defined narrowly at § 97-47-3(b) as a petroleum refinery or tank farm; an electrical power generation facility supporting the Mississippi power grid; a natural gas processing and terminal facility; a federal or state military installation; or an entity contracted by the Department of Defense or State Military Department to produce defense products — it does **not** extend to water/wastewater, telecommunications, ports, or rail/freight facilities. "Correctional facility" covers state, federal, municipal/county, and youth-detention confinement facilities. Section 97-47-9 makes a violation of (a) a misdemeanor (up to 1 year and/or a $1,000 fine) and a violation of (b) a felony (3-15 years and/or a $25,000 fine). Section 97-47-7 exempts law-enforcement UAS use for any lawful purpose, permits other public agencies/contractors to operate a UAS over such sites only with the facility owner's written consent and FAA-rule compliance, and provides that the chapter does not apply to UAS operating under an FAA Certificate of Waiver or Authorization.

### Uncrewed Aircraft Systems' Rights and Authorities Act (Miss. Code Ann. §§ 61-21-1 to 61-21-11)
`Binding statute | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Enacted in 2023 (Laws 2023, ch. 306, SB 2146), this chapter authorizes an individual, in compliance with federal law, to operate a UAS recreationally, and an individual or business entity doing business lawfully in Mississippi to operate/use a UAS commercially (§ 61-21-5), without any state registration requirement beyond what federal law requires. Section 61-21-7 makes a person liable for an act or offense committed with the aid of a UAS to the same extent as if performed directly, and confirms a UAS can be the instrumentality of a privacy-rights tort. Section 61-21-9 — Mississippi's preemption provision — bars a political subdivision from enacting or enforcing an ordinance relating to UAS ownership, operation, design, manufacture, testing, maintenance, licensing, registration, certification, airspace, altitude, flight paths, equipment, or pilot/operator/observer qualifications, subject to exceptions permitting a political subdivision to enforce FAA restrictions, regulate its own government-owned/operated UAS, enforce generally applicable (non-UAS-specific) nuisance/voyeurism/harassment/trespass/property-damage ordinances, and regulate UAS launch/landing/pilot-presence on its own property. The Act also protects UAS takeoff/landing for emergency or critical-infrastructure maintenance support, technical-malfunction landings, sworn public-safety-officer operations, and federal-government operations.

### State-Purchased Small UAS — Domestic Manufacturer, Collision-Avoidance, and Country-of-Origin Requirements (Miss. Code Ann. § 31-7-67)
`Binding statute | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Beginning January 1, 2025, all sUAS purchased by the State of Mississippi or any agency/political subdivision must be purchased exclusively from a "domestic manufacturer" (U.S.-incorporated and headquartered, majority American-owned, manufacturing in the U.S.), must possess a collision-avoidance system, and all maintenance/repair/technical services must be performed by a "domestic sUAS company"; all state-contracted infrastructure-inspection services requiring sUAS use must employ domestically manufactured sUAS. Mississippi-based domestic manufacturers and sUAS companies receive a 10% bid preference in Title 31, Chapter 7 procurement, and agencies must solicit at least one bid from a Mississippi-based sUAS manufacturer. An agency may not purchase or operate an sUAS manufactured in the People's Republic of China (including assembly outside China using more than a negligible amount of PRC-origin parts, software, components, or raw materials), subject to a research-institution exemption and a grandfather clause for PRC-manufactured sUAS purchased before January 1, 2025.

### Hunter/Angler Harassment Statute — No UAS-Specific Provision Currently Enacted (Miss. Code Ann. § 49-7-147)
`Binding statute | Current / in force (as to its existing, non-UAS text)`

**Objective Summary:** Section 49-7-147 currently prohibits intentionally (a) interfering with or attempting to prevent another's lawful taking of wildlife, (b) attempting to disturb wildlife or affect its behavior with intent to prevent another's lawful taking of it, or (c) harassing a person engaged in lawful taking of wildlife or preparation for such taking; a violation is a Class II violation under Miss. Code Ann. § 49-7-143. The currently codified text contains **no reference to drones, UAS, or unmanned aircraft.** A bill (House Bill 719) proposed amending this section specifically to prohibit using an unmanned system to conduct video surveillance of, or interfere with, a person engaged in lawful hunting or fishing; that amendment was not confirmed enacted, and the current codified section does not contain it.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### MDWFP Wildlife Management Area Rules — UAV Launch/Landing Prohibition (40 Miss. Code R. 2-1.1(GG))
`Binding administrative rule | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** The consolidated basic regulations applicable to all Mississippi Wildlife Management Areas (WMAs) provide, at subsection (GG): "Unmanned Aerial Vehicles may not be launched from or landed on MDWFP-Owned Wildlife Management Areas." The rule restricts only launch and landing, not overflight, and exists alongside numerous other WMA use restrictions (target shooting, ORV/vehicle use, hunter-orange requirements, trail-camera identification, etc.) in the same rule, issued under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 39-7-11, 49-1-29, 49-4-4, and 49-5-13.

### Alligator Hunting Season Regulations — Drone/UAV Prohibition (40 Miss. Code R. 2-5.2, 2-5.3, 2-5.4)
`Binding administrative rule | Current / in force (see Section 5 on successor-version currency)`

**Objective Summary:** Mississippi's public-waters, private-lands, and Pelahatchie Bay alligator-hunting-season rules each provide that "the use of drones, UAVs (unmanned aerial vehicles), or any remotely operated vehicle or vessel to assist in the location, pursuit, and/or take of alligators is prohibited." A violation is a Class III violation under Miss. Code Ann. § 49-7-101. The rules are issued under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 49-1-29, 49-4-4, and 49-7-47.

### General Rules and Regulations for State Parks — Aircraft Operation Restriction (40 Miss. Code R. 6-1.2(W)(18))
`Binding administrative rule | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** The rule provides: "Except in an extreme emergency, the operation of aircraft on land or water or the air delivery of any person or thing by parachute, helicopter, hot air balloon or other means, without the written permission of the Executive Director of the Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks, is prohibited." The rule text addresses "aircraft" generally rather than naming "drone" or "unmanned aircraft" specifically, but MDWFP's practice treats UAS as covered aircraft requiring the same written special-use-permit process. Issued under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 49-1-29, 49-4-4, and 55-3-33.

## 4. Non-Regulatory Context

*The items below are drawn from news and secondary reporting, not primary legal authority. They are provided for situational awareness only and are not part of the verified source register.*

- **Failed trespass/surveillance UAS amendments, Mississippi Legislature (HB 1370, 2024; HB 1494, 2025).** HB 1370 would have added UAS-specific surveillance language to Mississippi's general trespass statutes (Miss. Code Ann. §§ 97-17-85, 97-17-87, 97-17-93, 97-17-97) and to the licensed-surveyor land-entry statute (§ 73-13-103); it passed the House 108-7 on March 12, 2024, but died in Senate Judiciary Division A on April 2, 2024. A nearly identical bill, HB 1494, died in House Judiciary A on February 4, 2025. Neither is current law; AEC firms should not assume UAS-specific trespass liability or surveyor-entry UAS immunity exists in Mississippi beyond the statutes summarized in Sections 2-3 above. [LegiScan HB1370](https://legiscan.com/MS/bill/HB1370/2024); [LegiScan HB1494](https://legiscan.com/MS/bill/HB1494/2025)
- **Failed "Uncrewed Aircraft Systems Rights and Authorities Act" companion bill, Mississippi Legislature (HB 839, 2023).** A House bill containing much of the same preemption/rights framework ultimately enacted via Senate Bill 2146 (Miss. Code Ann. §§ 61-21-1 to 61-21-11) died on the calendar on February 9, 2023; the substance became law only through the separate Senate vehicle, illustrating that bill captions and House passage alone do not confirm enactment. [LegiScan/TrackBill HB839](https://trackbill.com/bill/mississippi-house-bill-839-the-uncrewed-aircraft-systems-rights-and-authorities-act-create/2316643/)
- **Domestic-manufacturer sUAS procurement law, Mississippi Legislature (2023 ch. 460; amended 2024 ch. 498).** Mississippi's state and political-subdivision sUAS procurement law (Miss. Code Ann. § 31-7-67, summarized in Section 2) was amended in 2024 to add the China-manufacture ban effective January 1, 2025, following earlier 2023 legislative attention to a "Mississippi Security Drone Act" concept; AEC firms delivering UAS-based infrastructure-inspection services under Mississippi public contracts should confirm current-list compliance for both the operator's own equipment (where a contract flows the requirement down) and any subcontracted sUAS services.

- **Alligator-season rule successor version.** The alligator-hunting drone prohibition (40 Miss. Code R. 2-5.3) was fetched in a version the publisher labels "effective until 7/1/2025." A successor version for the current (2025-2026) season was not independently re-fetched to confirm the drone/UAV prohibition is retained verbatim, though it has been a stable feature across many amendment cycles.
- **MDOT Aeronautics Division.** No public MDOT Aeronautics Division webpage with commercial-UAS-specific guidance, registration, or licensing content (comparable to some other states' DOT aeronautics offices) was located. This should be rechecked periodically in case MDOT publishes such guidance.
- **Hunter-harassment amendment currency (HB 719).** The exact legislative session and final disposition of HB 719 (the bill proposing UAS-specific language for Miss. Code Ann. § 49-7-147) could not be pinned down precisely in this pass; what is confirmed directly is that the current codified § 49-7-147 text contains no UAS-specific language.
- **State UAS procurement/manufacturer restriction scope for private consultants.** Miss. Code Ann. § 31-7-67 binds the state and its agencies/political subdivisions as purchasers and extends the domestic-sUAS requirement to state-contracted infrastructure-inspection services; whether a specific public contract flows this requirement down to a private AEC consultant's own fleet for non-inspection deliverables must be confirmed against the individual contract/RFP.
- **Executive orders, AG opinions, and court decisions.** No Mississippi gubernatorial executive order, Attorney General opinion, or state appellate/supreme court decision addressing UAS was located; this negative finding rests on targeted rather than exhaustive historical searches and should be rechecked in a future currency pass.
