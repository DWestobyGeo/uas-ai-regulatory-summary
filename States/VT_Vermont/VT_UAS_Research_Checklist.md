# Vermont UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Vermont (VT)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current Titles 13 and 20, the 2025–26 session, and enacted 2026 report legislation were reviewed. Chapter 205, the weaponized-drone offense, and current private-property provisions are included. Act 157 (2026) references the drone-use report only within broader report-management legislation and adds no operator-facing UAS requirement. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | The Fish and Wildlife Board's aerial-hunting rule and the Forests, Parks and Recreation state-lands rule/procedure are included. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | The current Vermont executive-order materials were searched. No current statewide operator-facing UAS executive order was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Vermont Supreme Court and Attorney General materials were searched. No controlling reported Vermont decision or formal Attorney General opinion specifically applying current state UAS law to ordinary commercial AEC operations was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | 20 V.S.A. § 4623 requires every drone use to comply with applicable FAA requirements and guidelines. No separate Vermont commercial UAS registration, pilot credential, or general state aviation permit was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | The January 29, 2025 FPR procedure requires written Commissioner approval through a Special Use Permit or License for covered launch and landing on state park and forest lands, including commercial operations. Fish and Wildlife Board Rule § 20 prohibits drone-assisted taking, locating, surveillance, driving, or harassment of wildlife. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | 20 V.S.A. §§ 4622, 4624, and 4625 regulate law-enforcement use and reporting and correctional-facility overflight. No separate UAS-specific critical-infrastructure offense was located. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | 20 V.S.A. § 4626, effective June 6, 2024, prohibits specified drone surveillance of private property or people without prior written consent and creates a privacy presumption. It also contains a recreational low-altitude provision, utility/law-enforcement exceptions, and seller notice duty. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Reviewed — no applicable current UAS-specific restriction located | No enacted Vermont manufacturer, country-of-origin, component, or cybersecurity restriction for UAS procurement was located. Section 4626(c) imposes a notice duty on businesses selling federally registrable drones, but it is not an approved-manufacturer or equipment-eligibility rule. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Vermont professional engineering and land-surveying board materials were searched; no UAS-specific rule, opinion, or bulletin was located. General professional-practice rules were excluded under the scope gate. |
| 11 | State preemption | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Chapter 205 contains no express statewide preemption provision, and no separate current Vermont UAS-preemption statute or controlling opinion was located. Local-ordinance research remains deferred. |

## Open verification items

1. For FPR property, confirm the current online application, responsible district or regional License Coordinator, insurance and payment requirements, expected review time, and whether the proposed use requires a Special Use Permit or longer-term/commercial License.
2. For private-property imaging, document the project purpose and owner/occupant authorization and evaluate whether the proposed capture could meet § 4626's intent, surveillance, and reasonable-expectation-of-privacy elements.
3. For correctional-facility work, confirm whether the commercial-purpose exception applies to the precise operation and whether the facility expects written consent or notice despite the statutory FAA-compliance exception.
4. Confirm whether Fish and Wildlife Department land rules impose a separate property-use approval on nonrecreational AEC missions; the official FPR procedure expressly addresses recreational use on those lands but does not publish a general commercial application route.
5. Recheck the next codified update for any 2026-session technical changes to § 4624 reporting and for any new procurement or preemption legislation.
