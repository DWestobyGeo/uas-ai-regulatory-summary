# New Jersey UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** New Jersey (NJ)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | P.L. 2017, c.315 and current N.J. Stat. §§ 2C:40-27 through -30 and 23:7A-2 were reviewed. They establish operating authority, criminal prohibitions, public-use protection, wildlife rules, and qualified inconsistent-local-law preemption. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | N.J.A.C. 7:2-1.4(b) supports State Park special-use approvals. N.J.A.C. 7:30 pesticide-control provisions regulate aerial applicator licensing, businesses, application conduct, notification, and records. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no separate current commercial-operator requirement located | Current gubernatorial executive-order materials and the 2024 drone-sighting response were searched. No enduring general commercial-UAS operating or procurement restriction was located in an executive order. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no controlling UAS-specific construction located | New Jersey court opinions and Attorney General directives were searched for interpretation of §§ 2C:40-27 through -30 and agency UAS surveillance requirements. No controlling published decision or statewide UAS-specific law-enforcement directive was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | NJDOT's UAS pages confirm no separate state registration requirement. QIA 066 imposes covered-foreign-UAS restrictions on FHWA-funded projects and grants. No additional general commercial NJDOT flight permit was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable sources found | State Park Service Policy 2.38 establishes the property prohibition and approval pathway. The 2017 act prohibits UAS-assisted taking of wildlife and UAS operation intended to hinder lawful taking. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Section 2C:40-28 governs correctional-facility and first-responder interference. Section 2C:40-27 allows a critical-infrastructure owner to pursue a federal restriction but creates no automatic state buffer. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable UAS-specific sources found | The correctional-surveillance, restraining-order, hunter-interference, and Rowan harassment provisions are recorded. General privacy, trespass, stalking, and harassment laws may apply but were not restated as UAS records without a direct UAS hook. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable project-specific sources found | NJDOT QIA 066 applies covered-UAS funding restrictions to FHWA work. NJDEP's manual imposes cybersecurity practices on Department and contracted pilots. No blanket state ban on private purchase or operation was located. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — pesticide specialty found; no UAS-specific engineering or surveying rule located | Engineering, land-surveying, mapping, and professional-board materials were searched. No separate UAS-specific licensure rule was located. Aerial pesticide Category 11 licensing is included because it directly governs treatment missions by aircraft. |
| 11 | State preemption and local authority | Applicable source found | Section 2C:40-29 preempts county or municipal enactments concerning private UAS use only when inconsistent with P.L. 2017, c.315. The qualifier does not establish blanket field preemption, and no court decision defining its reach was located. |
