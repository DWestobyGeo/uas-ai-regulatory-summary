## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Maryland's core statewide UAS authority is a 2015 preemption statute rather than a comprehensive operator-facing drone code. Md. Code Ann., Econ. Dev. §14-301, enacted by the Unmanned Aircraft Systems Research, Development, Regulation, and Privacy Act of 2015 (2015 Md. Laws, ch. 164, SB 370), provides that only the State may prohibit, restrict, or regulate the testing or operation of UAS, expressly preempting and superseding conflicting local ordinances; a companion section, §14-302, assigns the Department (Commerce) and MDOT MAA a duty to monitor FAA UAS rulemaking and report to the Governor and General Assembly, which they did in a December 2018 joint report with the Maryland Department of State Police. Beyond preemption, Maryland's UAS-specific statutory footprint is narrow: a 2024 statute (Md. Code Ann., Crim. Law §9-417.1) makes it a misdemeanor to use a drone to deliver contraband to a person confined in a correctional facility, and a long-standing generic "hunting from an aircraft" prohibition (Md. Code Ann., Nat. Res. §10-410(j)) is widely reported — though not independently confirmed in this pass at the COMAR level — to be applied by DNR to drone-assisted hunting. At the agency level, DNR's Maryland Park Service permits UAS operation on department-managed land (including state parks) subject to FAA compliance and prior Park Manager contact, a materially more permissive posture than some other states' blanket park-drone bans, while MDOT MAA publishes airspace-authorization guidance for the BWI Marshall and Martin State Airport vicinities that largely restates federal LAANC/DroneZone procedure. No Maryland court decision, Attorney General opinion, operative executive order, or state-agency UAS procurement/country-of-origin restriction was located. Notably, Maryland currently has **no statute governing law-enforcement UAS surveillance or "Drone as First Responder" programs** — a 2026 bill that would impose a warrant requirement (HB0471, previously HB0954 in 2025) remains pending and unenacted, a materially different posture from states like Maine that have a comprehensive law-enforcement-UAS statute.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### State Preemption of Local Unmanned Aircraft System Regulation — Md. Code Ann., Econ. Dev. §§14-301–14-302
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2015 Md. Laws, ch. 164 (SB 370); no subsequent amendment identified in the current codified text*

**Objective Summary:** §14-301 provides that only the State may enact a law or take other action to prohibit, restrict, or regulate the testing or operation of unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) in Maryland; subsection (c) expressly preempts county and municipal authority to do so and supersedes any existing local ordinance that prohibits, restricts, or regulates UAS testing or operation, without affecting federal preemption of state law. §14-302 requires the Department (Commerce) and MDOT MAA to monitor FAA UAS rulemaking, assess its state impact, consult with the University of Maryland's Mid-Atlantic Aviation Partnership and local governments, and report findings to the Governor and General Assembly — a duty that produced a joint MDSP/MDOT MAA report in December 2018.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Use the statewide rule as the regulatory baseline, while separately screening site-control rules and any local authority preserved by the preemption provision. Record that jurisdictional check in the project flight-planning package.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.

- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document the applicability analysis and any exception relied upon in the project file. Align the scope, client representations, and operator procedures with the verified authority, and seek state counsel if material facts fall outside its clear terms.
### Unauthorized Delivery of Contraband to a Place of Confinement Using an Unmanned Aircraft — Md. Code Ann., Crim. Law §9-417.1
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2024 Md. Laws, ch. 101 (SB 273), effective October 1, 2024*

**Objective Summary:** §9-417.1 makes it unlawful for a person to deliver contraband to a person detained or confined in a place of confinement using an unmanned aircraft. A violation is a misdemeanor punishable by imprisonment not exceeding 3 years, a fine not exceeding $1,000, or both. Every correctional facility must post exterior signage warning of the section's provisions in an area visible to the general public.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Screen the project map for protected facilities and active emergency operations before dispatch. Establish conservative stand-off and escalation points, brief the crew on the applicable restriction, and document any source-supported authorization before entering the affected area.

Preserve the facts supporting any exception or consent theory, allocate client and operator responsibilities clearly, and seek state counsel when project facts approach ambiguous or penal language.
### Restrictions on Hunting Wildlife Generally — Hunting from an Aircraft Prohibited — Md. Code Ann., Nat. Res. §10-410(j)
*Binding law | Current; drone-specific application not independently verified in this pass*

**Objective Summary:** §10-410(j) makes it unlawful for a person to hunt or attempt to hunt wild birds or mammals from an aircraft within Maryland; a violation is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine not exceeding $1,000, imprisonment not exceeding 6 months, or both. "Aircraft" is not separately defined within §10-410. Numerous secondary drone-law compilations describe additional DNR-published UAS-specific hunting restrictions (a bar on using a drone to locate, drive, or flush game; a 24-hour gap required between drone scouting and hunting the same property; and a wounded/expired-game recovery exception requiring Natural Resources Police notification), but this research pass could not independently open and verify the specific COMAR provision or DNR guide page stating those rules.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Track this authority before mobilization, but do not treat a proposal, repealed measure, or superseded source as a current operating rule. Base the flight plan on the verified law and agency policy then in force.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### Operation of Drones — Statewide Maryland Park Policy (DNR Maryland Park Service)
*Official agency policy | Current; no issue/revision date found within the document*

**Objective Summary:** DNR's "Statewide Maryland Park Policies" webpage states that UAS operated on DNR-owned or -managed land, including state parks, must comply with all FAA regulations and all State and federal law, including the Use of State Parks regulations, and that every drone operator must contact the Park Manager before flying in a State Park. The policy cites COMAR 08.07.06.13 (Plants, Rocks, Minerals, and Animals) and COMAR 08.07.06.20 (Disorderly Conduct) as the "applicable" regulations, though direct review of both confirms neither contains UAS-specific text. Individual parks set park-specific conditions; for example, Sandy Point State Park permits drone operation only before Memorial Day and after Labor Day, during normal operating hours, with restrictions near the adjacent Chesapeake Bay Bridge.

### Drones – Uncrewed Aircraft Systems (UAS) — MDOT MAA Community Relations Guidance
*Advisory guidance | Current; no issue/revision date found within the document*

**Objective Summary:** MDOT MAA's guidance page states that drone operators must obtain FAA airspace authorization to operate within 5 miles of Martin State Airport and within 7 miles of BWI Marshall Airport, via the FAA's LAANC system or DroneZone (not directly from the airport or control tower); most Part 107 and recreational requests are granted almost immediately, while flights above published LAANC altitudes require manual review (allow 72 hours). The page notes the control tower provides no separation services from drones, that operators must maintain visual line of sight, and that flight is prohibited during an active Temporary Flight Restriction (TFR).

### Professional Licensing — Maryland Land Surveying Statute (Md. Code Ann., Bus. Occ. & Prof. §15-101(k))
*Binding general regulation | No UAS-specific board guidance located*

**Objective Summary:** §15-101(k) defines "practice land surveying" to include, among other listed activities, "utilizing measurement devices or systems, such as aerial photogrammetry, global positioning systems, land information systems, geographic information systems, or similar technology" for evaluating or locating boundaries, easements, or rights-of-way, and measuring/platting features "in the air, on the surface of the earth, in underground work, and on the beds of bodies of water." No standalone State Board for Professional Land Surveyors rule, advisory opinion, or FAQ specifically addressing UAS-derived photogrammetric or LiDAR survey deliverables was located during this research pass.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **State preemption vs. local ordinances:** Md. Code Ann., Econ. Dev. §14-301 expressly preempts and supersedes conflicting local UAS ordinances, yet multiple Maryland counties reportedly continue to enforce their own drone permit/insurance requirements on county-owned land (e.g., parks), and the Maryland Attorney General's office has publicly declined to take a position on which law controls. AEC firms should not assume county-level drone permit requirements are unenforceable in practice merely because the state statute purports to preempt them; confirm the current posture with county counsel before relying solely on state preemption for a specific county engagement.
- **Correctional-facility statute scope:** Secondary sources describe Md. Code Ann., Crim. Law §9-417.1 as covering both unauthorized image recording and contraband delivery near correctional facilities and as carrying felony-level penalties with a defined no-fly buffer; the current codified text obtained directly in this pass shows only a contraband-delivery misdemeanor with no distance threshold or felony classification. This discrepancy should be resolved by reviewing the full chaptered 2024 session law (2024 Md. Laws, ch. 101) before relying on this citation for a specific correctional-facility-adjacent project.
- **Drone-specific hunting restrictions:** Widely and consistently reported DNR drone-hunting rules (locate/drive/flush prohibition, 24-hour scouting rule, wounded-game recovery exception with Natural Resources Police notification) could not be independently verified against a specific COMAR provision or the DNR's own Guide to Hunting and Trapping page in this pass. Confirm directly with DNR Wildlife and Heritage Service before relying on these specific parameters for a land-management or forestry UAS survey near active hunting areas.
- **State park drone policy's COMAR citation:** DNR's own webpage cites COMAR 08.07.06.13 and .20 as the "applicable" Use of State Parks regulations for drones, but neither provision contains UAS-specific text on its face. The controlling authority for DNR's drone policy therefore appears to be agency policy/practice rather than a codified UAS-specific regulation; confirm current park-specific conditions directly with the relevant Park Manager before a commercial engagement.
- **Law-enforcement UAS "gray area":** Maryland has no enacted statute governing law-enforcement UAS surveillance or "Drone as First Responder" programs. HB0471 (2026), which would impose a warrant requirement, remains pending and has not advanced in either the 2025 or 2026 session as of this research date; AEC firms partnering with Maryland public-safety agencies on UAS work should monitor this bill's status.
- **Land-surveying licensure and UAS-derived deliverables:** No Board rule or guidance specifically addressing UAS-derived (as opposed to manned-aircraft) photogrammetric or LiDAR survey products was located, despite §15-101(k)'s explicit reference to "aerial photogrammetry." Confirm directly with the State Board for Professional Land Surveyors before relying on this citation for a specific project's licensure determination.
