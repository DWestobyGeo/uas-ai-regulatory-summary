## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Florida regulates commercial UAS primarily through two statutes — the Unmanned Aircraft Systems Act (§330.41) and the Freedom from Unwarranted Surveillance Act (§934.50) — both substantially amended for 2025. The state vests UAS regulatory authority centrally and preempts most local UAS-specific ordinances, while local governments retain authority over takeoff/landing on their own property and generally applicable nuisance/park rules. Penalties for critical-infrastructure and school violations were sharply increased in the 2025 legislative session. Multiple commercial/FAA-compliance carve-outs support routine AEC operations, but the elevated penalties make documentation discipline important.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### Procurement — Florida Department of Management Services
*Official agency policy | Binding on governmental agencies only*

**Objective Summary:** DMS publishes and maintains the approved-manufacturer list implementing §934.50(7). Agencies submitted discontinuation plans for non-approved drones by July 1, 2022, and fully discontinued non-approved-manufacturer drones (effectively excluding DJI, Autel, and other Chinese-manufactured drones from government use) by January 1, 2023.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** If your fleet is DJI-based, budget separately for DMS-approved airframes before bidding on FDOT, county, or municipal UAS work — don't assume your standard private-sector kit qualifies, and check the current list early since procurement lead times for approved manufacturers can run long.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Require current vendor documentation for aircraft, critical components, installed software, ownership, origin, and support lifecycle before purchase. Compare the proposed configuration with the controlling authority and preserve the evidence supporting the acquisition decision.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Confirm whether a public-agency RFP or contract incorporates the approved-manufacturer requirement as a flow-down term; if so, build equipment-compliance representations into the proposal and retain documentation of equipment used on each task order.

### Fish and Wildlife — Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission
*Binding statute + official agency guidance*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits using a drone to take, harass, or assist in taking fish or wildlife (Fla. Stat. §379.401 and accompanying FWC rules); FWC guidance flags that low-altitude flights near nesting shorebirds/wading birds can constitute harassment. §330.41(7) separately prohibits drone contact with state wildlife management lands made with intent to harass.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** For environmental-monitoring and corridor/wetland mapping flights, plan altitude and flight paths to maintain standoff from known rookeries and nesting colonies during breeding season, and coordinate with the regional FWC office before any Wildlife Management Area project.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document the environmental/monitoring purpose of flights near wildlife concentrations and any FWC coordination in the project file — the harassment standard is judgment-based, so contemporaneous documentation of flight purpose and altitude is the best defense if a complaint arises.
### Corrections — Fla. Stat. §944.47 and Critical-Infrastructure Overlap
*Binding law | Current*

**Objective Summary:** §944.47 criminalizes introducing contraband into a state correctional institution, including via drone (2nd/3rd-degree felony depending on contraband/facility). State correctional institutions, contractor-operated correctional facilities, county detention facilities, and juvenile secure-detention facilities are separately enumerated as "critical infrastructure facilities" under §330.41(2)(b)17.–19., meaning ordinary overflight is restricted absent the commercial/FAA exemption.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Identify correctional/detention facilities near any project site during pre-flight planning — route flight paths around them by default rather than relying on the commercial exemption unless the project specifically requires flying there.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** For any project genuinely requiring inspection/survey work at or adjacent to a correctional facility, obtain written facility-operator coordination in advance and retain FAA authorization/commercial-purpose documentation given the felony stakes.
### Professional Licensing — Florida Board of Professional Surveyors and Mappers
*Binding general licensing statute | No UAS-specific board rule located*

**Objective Summary:** Florida's "practice of surveying and mapping" definition under Ch. 472, Fla. Stat., already contemplates photogrammetric procedures as an accepted measurement method; a Florida-licensed Professional Surveyor and Mapper (PSM) must certify boundary surveys and official mapping deliverables regardless of collection method. No standalone UAS-specific board guidance document was located.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Route drone-derived photogrammetry/LiDAR/orthophoto products through a Florida-licensed PSM for review before delivery on any boundary or official survey deliverable — the pilot flying the mission does not need to be a PSM, but sign-off does.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Before relying on an unlicensed or exempt delivery path, give Florida Board of Professional Surveyors and Mappers / Dept. of Agriculture and Consumer Services a written description of the proposed UAS-derived product, its stated accuracy, and whether it will establish or certify boundaries, elevations, or authoritative locations. Ask the board to confirm the applicable license, responsible-charge, certification, or exemption path and retain the response with the project quality plan; the research packet does not establish a UAS-specific turnaround time.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Absent UAS-specific board guidance, rely on the general Ch. 472 licensure requirement and document PSM involvement in the QA/QC workflow for every certified deliverable; consider a formal inquiry to the Board if a project raises a novel certification question.
