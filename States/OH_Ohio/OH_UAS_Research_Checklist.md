# Ohio UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Ohio (OH)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current Chapters 4561 and 5501, incorporated critical-infrastructure definitions, H.B. 77, and the enacted H.B. 251 were reviewed. H.B. 77 is current; the H.B. 251 law-enforcement sections become effective October 6, 2026, and the procurement section becomes effective October 6, 2030. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | Current UAS-specific rules were located for state parks, nature preserves and natural areas, Division of Forestry lands, hunting, and multiple state universities. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific operator requirement located | Current gubernatorial executive-order materials were searched. No separate current executive order imposing a commercial UAS operating or procurement requirement was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Advisory source found; no controlling UAS decision located | The Ohio Attorney General’s 2018 law-enforcement UAS model-policy report was reviewed as nonbinding background. No controlling Ohio appellate decision or formal Attorney General opinion construing the current UAS-specific statutes was located. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable source found | H.B. 77 requires the ODOT Office of Aviation to publish public, commercial, and recreational UAS information and critical-facility examples. ODOT’s advanced-air-mobility and UAS materials were reviewed; no separate statewide commercial operator permit was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | Rules 1501:46-13-11, 1501:17-11-03, 1501:3-2-16, and 1501:31-15-02 establish distinct state-park, preserve, forestry, and hunting restrictions. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | Sections 4561.50-.51 protect specified critical facilities and on-duty responders. Sections 4561.60-.64 create a future-effective law-enforcement surveillance and data framework. No separate emergency-management commercial-flight permit was located. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | Section 4561.51 addresses responder interference and criminal-purpose critical-facility imaging; § 4561.15 addresses endangerment and disregard of rights or safety. Current and future law-enforcement surveillance provisions and agency-property recording and harassment rules were also reviewed. No separate generally applicable UAS voyeurism statute was located in this pass. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable future-effective source found | Enacted § 5501.84 will restrict public-entity acquisition, state funding, and operation of systems manufactured or assembled by a listed foreign adversary beginning October 6, 2030. No present generally applicable private-fleet manufacturer ban was located. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific source located | Ohio engineering and surveying statutes, Board rules, and professional-practice materials were searched. Surveying definitions can encompass measurements and mapping from the air, but no UAS-specific Board rule, opinion, or bulletin was located, so general licensing law was not converted into a UAS record. |
| 11 | State preemption and local authority | Applicable source found | Section 4561.52 expressly authorizes specified local governments to regulate their own fleets and exclusively recreational UAS use in or above their parks or other public property. It does not use express blanket-preemption language, so the register preserves that limitation rather than overstating the provision. |

## Open verification items

1. Confirm the current ODOT Office of Aviation critical-facility examples and any successor guidance before work near a facility that may fall within the incorporated definition.
2. Obtain written property-manager instructions for the exact ODNR unit; the parks, preserves, and forestry rules use different scopes, lead times, and approval language.
3. For a law-enforcement client or partner, determine whether an October 2026 flight is a surveillance flight taken “on behalf of” the agency and agree on responsibility for flight-data documentation and retention.
4. Before a public-sector fleet or state-funded contract extends into October 2030, check the Secretary of State registry and the final federal-law exception analysis under § 5501.84.
5. For work on a public-university campus, verify the current policy version, request form, lead time, insurance terms, venue restrictions, and approver; requirements differ substantially by institution.
6. Confirm whether any additional Ohio public university has adopted an active UAS-specific policy outside the codified rules and official policy materials located in this pass.
