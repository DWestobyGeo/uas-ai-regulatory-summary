## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Ohio’s principal operator-facing framework is relatively new. H.B. 77 took effect April 9, 2025 and added state enforcement of federal UAS restrictions, emergency-responder interference offenses, criminal-purpose protections for a broad group of critical facilities, and a general prohibition on knowing endangerment or purposeful disregard of rights or safety. Ohio does not create a general commercial UAS license or statewide mission permit. Its local-authority statute expressly identifies regulation of local-government fleets and exclusively recreational use over local parks or other locally owned public property, but does not call itself blanket preemption.

State-managed property is a major operational layer. State parks, nature preserves and natural areas, and Division of Forestry properties use different permission language and geographic reach. Ohio’s public universities also maintain institution-specific UAS policies; the active policies located in this pass commonly require advance approval, and several impose lead times, insurance, standoff, privacy, contract, or institutional-purpose conditions.

Two enacted frameworks are not yet effective. On October 6, 2026, H.B. 251’s law-enforcement warrant, use, and data provisions take effect. On October 6, 2030, its public-entity foreign-adversary acquisition, funding, and operation restrictions take effect. Their future status is stated explicitly below.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations
### OH-001 — UAV operation and prohibitions; critical-facility definitions

**Authority:** Ohio Rev. Code §§ 4561.50-.51; incorporated definition at § 2911.21(F)(4)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Ohio Rev. Code § 4561.51 makes federally prohibited UAV operations a state offense unless the operator complies with an FAA authorization. It separately prohibits operations that disrupt, interrupt, or impair on-duty law-enforcement, fire, or emergency-medical personnel. Purposeful photography, recording, or loitering over or near a critical facility is prohibited when done to further a violent or threatening offense or to destroy or tamper with the facility. The incorporated facility definitions cover courts, police and correctional sites, military facilities, air-ambulance hospitals, commercial distribution centers, and a long list of fenced, posted, railroad, electronic, and industrial infrastructure. Penalties range from misdemeanors to a third-degree felony.

### OH-002 — Unsafe operation of aircraft or UAV

**Authority:** Ohio Rev. Code § 4561.15(A)(5), (C)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Ohio Rev. Code § 4561.15(A)(5) prohibits operating an unmanned aerial vehicle on land or water or in the airspace over Ohio in a manner that knowingly endangers any person or property or purposely disregards the rights or safety of others. Under division (C), a violation may be punished by a fine of not more than $500, imprisonment for not more than six months, or both. The UAS provision was added by H.B. 77 and is distinct from the separate careless-or-reckless standard for manned aircraft in the section.

### OH-003 — Local use and operation of UAVs

**Authority:** Ohio Rev. Code § 4561.52  
**Status:** Current / in force

Subject to Ohio Rev. Code §§ 4561.50-.53, § 4561.52 authorizes a municipal corporation, township, Chapter 1545 park district, or county to regulate two categories: UAVs owned and operated by that governmental entity, and UAVs operated exclusively for hobby or recreational purposes in or above a park or other public property the entity owns. The section does not expressly label itself a preemption statute or independently authorize local regulation of ordinary commercial UAS operations. It nevertheless defines the UAS-specific local powers affirmatively granted by H.B. 77 and should be read with other generally applicable property and police powers.

### OH-007 — General hunting and trapping provisions

**Authority:** Ohio Admin. Code 1501:31-15-02(B)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Ohio Admin. Code 1501:31-15-02(B) prohibits hunting, shooting, killing, taking, or attempting to take a wild bird or wild quadruped by means, aid, or use of an aircraft, expressly including aerial drones. A narrow exception allows a drone to aid recovery of a deer after the taking has occurred, provided no one involved in the recovery is employing a hunting device. If the deer is found alive, drone use must stop before hunting resumes; the operator may communicate the last location where the drone observed the deer.

### OH-008 — Law-enforcement UAV surveillance, use, and data requirements

**Authority:** Ohio Rev. Code §§ 4561.60-.64; H.B. 251, 136th General Assembly  
**Status:** Enacted / future effective

Effective October 6, 2026, Ohio Rev. Code §§ 4561.60-.64 require a warrant when a peace officer uses UAS to enter or observe a house or place in circumstances where physical entry would require one, subject to enumerated warrantless uses such as exigencies, disasters, crash scenes, crime scenes, nonintrusive public observation, event threat assessment, and collaborative research or training. Agencies may not use lethal-weapon UAS or authorize surveillance by one private individual of another without specified consent. Agencies must document and verify flight data for each surveillance flight taken by or on their behalf, retain data relevant to proceedings, and generally treat flight and surveillance data as public records.

### OH-009 — Public-entity UAS foreign-adversary purchasing and operation restriction

**Authority:** Ohio Rev. Code § 5501.84; H.B. 251, 136th General Assembly  
**Status:** Enacted / future effective

Beginning October 6, 2030, Ohio Rev. Code § 5501.84 prohibits a public entity from purchasing or otherwise acquiring a UAS manufactured or assembled by a foreign adversary, prohibits use of state funds in connection with such systems purchased on or after that date—including through contracts, grants, and cooperative agreements—and prohibits public entities from operating such post-effective-date systems. A federal-law or federal-regulation allowance for continued purchase and operation supplies an exception. The Secretary of State must publish and update at least semiannually a registry derived from specified federal lists.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### OH-004 — Aviation

**Authority:** Ohio Admin. Code 1501:46-13-11(C)-(D)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Ohio Admin. Code 1501:46-13-11 prohibits unmanned-aerial-craft operation within state-park boundaries without permission from the division chief or an authorized agent, except at an airfield or landing zone maintained for such craft. Separate permission is required to photograph, film, or record public activities, historical or cultural features, natural formations, division facilities, or other aspects of managed lands and waters. The rule also bars operation over open-air assemblies, beaches, boats, or roadways in division areas, requires FAA compliance, prohibits harassment of persons, and limits wildlife-harassment use to nuisance-control permittees who also have division permission.

**Authority:** Ohio Admin. Code 1501:17-11-03  
**Status:** Current / in force

Ohio Admin. Code 1501:17-11-03 prohibits launching, landing, or otherwise operating an unmanned aerial craft in the airspace over or within a state nature preserve or natural area unless written permission is obtained from the division chief or an authorized agent. The rule separately prohibits using unmanned craft to photograph, film, or record public activities, cultural or historical features, natural formations, division facilities, or other aspects of managed lands and waters without that permission, and strictly forbids using them to harass persons or wildlife.

**Authority:** Ohio Admin. Code 1501:3-2-16  
**Status:** Current / in force

### OH-010 — Use of Unmanned Aircraft Systems (UAS)

**Authority:** Ohio State University interim policy, issued 2015-11-20 and revised 2025-11-19  
**Status:** Current university policy

Ohio State treats all university property, including regional campuses and reasonable airspace above it, as a no-drone-fly zone unless the remote pilot first receives university approval and complies with imposed conditions. The process includes a UAS request form and operations agreement, with review by Public Safety and potentially communications, export-control, venue, and other offices. Current flight-request guidance calls for submission at least 14 days before the event, prohibits flight over people, and adds venue-specific controls. The policy also governs university-sponsored activities off property and requires accident and adverse-event reporting.

### OH-012 — Administrative policy regarding unmanned aircraft systems

**Authority:** Ohio Admin. Code 3342-5-12.16  
**Status:** Current / in force

Kent State is a no-drone-fly zone under Ohio Admin. Code 3342-5-12.16. All operations, including commercial contractor flights, require UAS Review Committee approval through a request submitted at least two weeks in advance. A continuous-operation approval may cover multiple flights for up to six months. Approved operators must stay in designated areas, avoid the Kent State airport, minimize ground and aviation risks, and ordinarily remain at least 50 feet from walkways, 200 feet from buildings, and 100 feet from parking lots or roads. Privacy, sensitive-data, marks, notification, and incident-reporting restrictions also apply.

### OH-016 — Unmanned Aircraft (Drone) Policy

**Authority:** University of Cincinnati Policy 6.3.2  
**Status:** Current university policy

University of Cincinnati Policy 6.3.2 strictly prohibits UAS use on university property unless the flight is solely for research, education, or university operations; the university has authorized it; all external approvals have been obtained; and the mission avoids monitoring locations where people have a reasonable expectation of privacy. Recreational and personal-imaging flights are prohibited. The policy applies to third parties and contractors as well as university personnel, makes operators responsible for fines or damages, and permits law enforcement to ground noncompliant or dangerous operations. The university maintains a current online drone-flight approval request process.

### OH-017 — UAS (Drone) Use Approval Process

**Authority:** University of Akron UAS (Drone) Use Approval Process  
**Status:** Current university procedure

The University of Akron requires a contracted UAS vendor and the contracting department to seek Department of Safety approval at least two weeks before flight, submit a flight plan, and carry the approved application during operations. The vendor must provide FAA authority, and the contracting department must have a university-approved contract. That contract must require at least $1 million in general, aviation, or equivalent liability coverage that includes UAS and names the university as an additional insured. Operations over assemblies, stadiums, or populated areas need specific approval, and airport or heliport notification documentation may also be required.

## 4. Non-Regulatory Context

The Ohio Attorney General’s 2018 advisory-group report provides a model policy and recommendations for law-enforcement UAS programs, but it is educational guidance rather than binding operator authority. Because H.B. 251 establishes a statutory law-enforcement framework effective October 6, 2026, agencies and their contractors should use the enacted code—not the older model alone—as the current legal baseline after that date.
