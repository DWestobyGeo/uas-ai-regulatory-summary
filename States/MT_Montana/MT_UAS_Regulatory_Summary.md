## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Montana regulates UAS primarily through a series of discrete criminal and evidentiary statutes rather than a single consolidated UAS act. A 2013 law-enforcement statute (Mont. Code Ann. § 46-5-109) bars using UAV-derived information as evidence or as the basis for a search-warrant affidavit absent a warrant or recognized exception. A newly enacted 2025 statute (Mont. Code Ann. § 45-6-210, SB 493) creates a criminal trespass offense for flying a UAV at or below 200 feet over another's property or residence without authorization — already the subject of active legislative debate over its application to the "corner-crossing" public-land-access controversy. A 2017 statute (Mont. Code Ann. § 76-13-214), paired with a narrow, single-subject local-ordinance preemption (§ 7-1-111(20)), prohibits UAV interference with aerial wildfire suppression. Montana's hunting code (Mont. Code Ann. § 87-6-208, with "aircraft" statutorily defined at § 87-6-101(3) to expressly include unmanned aerial vehicles) restricts drone-assisted game scouting, spotting, herding, and hunt interference. A narrower procurement-related statute (§ 7-32-401) bars local law-enforcement agencies from acquiring armored or weaponized drones through federal military-surplus programs. A significant negative/corrective finding from this pass: the administrative rule long cited by secondary sources as governing state-park drone use (former ARM 12.8.816) has in fact been **repealed**, and no UAS-specific successor rule was located in the current consolidated state-park public-use rule (ARM 12.8.2). No Montana statute restricts UAS surveillance of critical infrastructure or correctional facilities comparable to other states' UAS Protection Acts, no state-level UAS manufacturer/country-of-origin procurement restriction was located, no MDT Aeronautics Division UAS-specific guidance was located, no Attorney General opinion or state appellate decision addressing UAS was located, and a 2019 executive order creating a Montana UAS Council (EO 11-2019) has expired by its own terms.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Limitations on Unmanned Aerial Vehicles — Law Enforcement Evidentiary and Warrant Restriction (Mont. Code Ann. § 46-5-109)
`Binding statute (evidentiary/criminal procedure) | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** In any prosecution or proceeding within Montana, information from an unmanned aerial vehicle is not admissible as evidence unless obtained (a) pursuant to a search warrant, (b) under a judicially recognized exception to the warrant requirement, or (c) during investigation of a motor-vehicle crash scene on or involving a public roadway. Subsection (2) separately bars use of UAV-obtained information in a probable-cause affidavit unless obtained under one of those same three circumstances. Subsection (3) defines "unmanned aerial vehicle" as an aircraft operated without direct human intervention from on or within the aircraft, expressly excluding satellites. Originally enacted in 2013 (Ch. 377) and amended in 2019 (Ch. 178) to add the motor-vehicle-crash exception.

### Criminal Trespass by Unmanned Aerial Vehicle — Exceptions (Mont. Code Ann. § 45-6-210)
`Binding statute (criminal) | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Enacted in 2025 (Laws 2025, ch. 410, SB 493), this statute makes it a criminal trespass for a person to knowingly cause a UAV to fly 200 feet or lower over the property or residence of another without the property owner's or resident's authorization; a violation is fined $500. Subsection (3) exempts: UAS operations by a federal, state, tribal, or local government unit/agency (including contractors) for public safety, firefighting, law enforcement, or emergency response; peace-officer operations conducted under § 46-5-109; operations within an easement with the dominant-tenement owner's permission; utility or broadband-service-provider operations to assess damage or inspect property/infrastructure (including cell towers and wireless infrastructure); and operations by an FAA-licensed owner/operator conducting legitimate business purposes consistent with FAA rules, licenses, and exemptions. "Unmanned aerial vehicle" takes the meaning given in § 76-13-102.

### Obstruction of Aerial Wildfire Suppression Activities; Related Local-Ordinance Preemption (Mont. Code Ann. § 76-13-214; § 7-1-111(20))
`Binding statute (criminal); binding limitation on local ordinance power | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Section 76-13-214 prohibits a person from obstructing, impeding, preventing, or otherwise interfering with lawful aerial wildfire suppression activities by any means, including by use of an unmanned aerial vehicle system; a violation is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $1,500, and the fine does not limit other remedies for damages or recovery of wildfire suppression costs. Subsection (3)(a) exempts UAS operations conducted by a federal, state, tribal, or local government unit/agency (including contractors) for public safety and welfare purposes, including firefighting, law enforcement, or emergency response. Enacted concurrently in 2017 (HB 644) was a companion amendment, now codified at § 7-1-111(20), which bars a local government unit with self-government powers from enacting any ordinance governing the private use of a UAV in relation to a wildfire — a narrow, subject-specific preemption rather than a general preemption of local UAS ordinances.

### Unlawful Use of Aircraft — Hunting-Related Drone/UAV Restrictions (Mont. Code Ann. § 87-6-208; definition at § 87-6-101(3))
`Binding statute (criminal) | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Section 87-6-208 prohibits using an "aircraft" — statutorily defined at § 87-6-101(3) as "any manned or unmanned aerial vehicle or device that is used or intended to be used for flight in the air," expressly reaching drones/UAS — to kill or take game from the aircraft; to spot or locate a game animal for hunting purposes within 24 hours of use; to concentrate, pursue, drive, rally, or stir up game birds, migratory birds, game animals, or fur-bearing animals; to intentionally interfere with a hunt, including by disturbing animal behavior; to land in violation of state/federal land-use travel plans or without written permission on private property while pursuing game; or to spot/locate an animal and communicate its location to a hunter while airborne or within 24 hours afterward. Exceptions apply for emergencies, search and rescue, Department of Livestock predator control, and support of an agricultural operation. Penalties range from a $300–$1,000 fine and up to 6 months' imprisonment for a general violation, up to a $500–$2,000 fine, up to 6 months' imprisonment, and 30-month loss of hunting/fishing/trapping privileges for using an aircraft to kill/take specified big game.

### Limitations on Excess Property Provided to Local Law Enforcement — Armored/Weaponized Drones (Mont. Code Ann. § 7-32-401)
`Binding statute | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Section 7-32-401(1)(a) bars a local-government law-enforcement agency from receiving drones that are armored, weaponized, or both, from a military-equipment surplus program operated by the federal government; the same subsection separately bars receipt of combat-configured/coded aircraft, grenades/explosives and launchers, silencers, and militarized armored vehicles. Subsection (2) requires that any property a law-enforcement agency does purchase from such a surplus program be paid for with state or local funds only, not federal funds. "Law enforcement agency" is defined by cross-reference to the local-government law-enforcement services authorized under Title 7, chapter 32. This provision binds local-government law-enforcement agencies only, not the state itself, other state agencies, or private AEC consultants.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### State Parks — Former Unmanned Aircraft Systems Rule Repealed; No UAS-Specific Successor Rule Located (formerly ARM 12.8.816)
`Repealed, expired, or superseded authority | Repealed — no current UAS-specific successor rule identified`

**Objective Summary:** Former ARM 12.8.816 provided that launching or operating a UAS, drone, or model aircraft from a Montana state park was prohibited unless authorized by a commercial-use or special-use permit, or occurring within an area specifically designated for such use by the park manager (originally adopted 2016 MAR p. 825, eff. 5/7/16). Justia's official Administrative Rules of Montana mirror (current through Mont. Admin. Register Vol. 11, June 6, 2025) shows the entire former subchapter 12.8.8 ("State Parks Public Use Regulations"), including Rule 12.8.816, as **repealed**. The successor consolidated public-use subchapter, ARM 12.8.2 ("Public Use Regulations," rules 12.8.201–12.8.219, also current through the same June 2025 register), was reviewed in full and contains **no UAS-, drone-, or unmanned-aircraft-specific rule** among its current provisions (covering weapons, animals, vehicles, camping, fires, property disturbance, disorderly conduct, restricted areas, sanitation, boating/swimming, and emergency-closure rules).

### Executive Order Creating the Montana Unmanned Aerial Systems Council (Executive Order No. 11-2019)
`Executive order | Expired`

**Objective Summary:** Executive Order 11-2019 (Aug. 29, 2019, Gov. Steve Bullock) created the Montana Unmanned Aerial Systems Council, administratively attached to MDT, comprising representatives of DEQ, DNRC, FWP, MDT, Department of Livestock, Department of Justice, the Montana University System, and stakeholders. The order directed the Council to issue draft and final statewide UAS procurement procedures (Nov. 15, 2019 / Jan. 1, 2020) and a draft and final "Montana Unmanned Aerial Systems Plan" (Apr. 1, 2020 / Aug. 1, 2020) addressing centralized state-agency UAS oversight, operator training/certification standards, interagency coordination, and citizen-privacy considerations regarding state UAS use over private property. By its own terms, the Council "shall complete its work and cease to exist by September 1, 2020, unless rescinded or renewed by subsequent executive order"; no renewal was located.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **State-park UAS policy after ARM 12.8.816's repeal.** The former dedicated state-park drone rule has been repealed and no UAS-specific successor rule was located in the current consolidated public-use subchapter (ARM 12.8.2). It is unresolved whether FWP applies an unwritten practice equivalent to the former permit/designated-area requirement, or whether state-park UAS use is now governed only by general public-use rules (e.g., restricted areas, disorderly conduct) plus statewide statutes. AEC firms should confirm current UAS policy directly with individual park managers or FWP's State Parks Division before flying in a Montana state park.
- **MDT Aeronautics Division and Title 67 aircraft registration.** Title 67's general "aircraft" definition (§ 67-1-101(4): "a contrivance used or designed for navigation of or flight in the air") does not expressly include or exclude unmanned aircraft, unlike the Title 87 hunting-code definition, which does. MDT's public aircraft-registration materials (tail numbers, ELTs, VFR flight plans, N-numbers) are oriented entirely toward manned aircraft, and no MDT Aeronautics UAS-specific registration or policy page was located. Whether Title 67 registration is understood by MDT to apply to UAS was not resolved in this pass and should be confirmed directly with MDT Aeronautics.
- **Status of the Montana Unmanned Aerial Systems Council's work product.** Executive Order 11-2019 expired by its own terms in September 2020, but a 2022 Legislative Audit Division committee document titled "The Montana Unmanned Aircraft Systems Plan" suggests state-agency UAS planning work continued in some form afterward. The relationship between this later document and the original Council structure was not independently verified in this pass.
- **§ 45-6-210 effective date.** The precise statutory effective-date clause for the 2025 drone-trespass law (as opposed to Montana's general default effective date) was not independently confirmed in this pass.
- **Local UAS ordinances outside the wildfire-preemption scope.** Because Montana's only confirmed UAS-related preemption is limited to wildfire-related ordinances (§ 7-1-111(20)), local ordinances on other UAS subjects (e.g., a municipal park drone restriction) may exist and remain enforceable; researching those local ordinances is deferred under the current program scope but should be checked project-by-project.
- **Executive orders, AG opinions, and court decisions.** No additional Montana gubernatorial executive order, Attorney General opinion, or state appellate/supreme court decision addressing UAS was located; this negative finding rests on targeted rather than exhaustive historical searches and should be rechecked in a future currency pass.
