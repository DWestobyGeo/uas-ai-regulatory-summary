# Nevada UAS Research Checklist

- **State:** Nevada (NV)
- **Research date:** 2026-08-02
- **Phase:** Phase 1 — Objective Research
- **Scope:** Statewide and state-agency UAS authorities only. Municipal, county, tribal, federal, and site-specific local layers are outside this pass.

| # | Coverage category | Status | Research note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State statutes and amendments | Applicable source found | Current NRS Chapters 493 and 503 were reviewed, together with 2023 S.B. 11 and the two 2025 amendments affecting NRS 493.118. A.B. 439 created the current data-residency procurement exception; S.B. 12 made organizational terminology changes. |
| 2 | Administrative rules and regulations | Applicable source found | NAC Chapters 407, 493, 503, and 555 and approved LCB File R111-23 were reviewed for parks, public-agency operations, wildlife, and pesticide application. |
| 3 | Executive orders | Reviewed — no applicable UAS-specific operator requirement located | Current and archived gubernatorial executive-order materials were searched. No separate current executive order imposing a commercial UAS operating or procurement requirement was located. |
| 4 | Court decisions and attorney-general materials | Reviewed — no controlling UAS-specific authority located | Nevada appellate-court and Attorney General materials were searched for construction of the UAS-specific provisions. No controlling published decision or formal Attorney General opinion was located in this pass. |
| 5 | Aviation and transportation agencies | Applicable statutory source found; no separate general commercial permit located | NRS 493.109 creates Nevada’s airport-proximity rule. NDOT permit materials were reviewed; temporary right-of-way occupancy may be required for ground use, filming, or traffic control, but no UAS-specific statewide NDOT flight permit was located. |
| 6 | Parks, public lands, forestry, fish, wildlife, and natural resources | Applicable source found | State Parks prohibits drone use except in a supervisor-designated area or under a special-use permit. NRS 503.010 and NAC 503.148 restrict hunting or trapping assistance using UAVs. No separate general UAS rule for all Division of State Lands holdings was located. |
| 7 | Corrections, public safety, emergency management, and critical infrastructure | Applicable source found | NRS 493.109 protects defined critical facilities; NRS 493.112-.118 govern law-enforcement and public-agency operations, registration, authorized purposes, and procurement. |
| 8 | Privacy, surveillance, harassment, trespass, and interference | Applicable source found | NRS 493.050 addresses low-altitude interference, NRS 493.103 creates the repeat-overflight trespass action and exceptions, and NRS 493.112 governs law-enforcement surveillance. General tort and privacy law was not converted into UAS records without a direct UAS hook. |
| 9 | Procurement, equipment, cybersecurity, and manufacturer restrictions | Applicable source found | NRS 493.112, 493.115, and 493.118, approved R111-23, and 2025 A.B. 439 establish current public- and law-enforcement-agency vendor, equipment, service, data-residency, federal-compliance, and grandfathering rules. |
| 10 | Professional licensing-board materials | Reviewed — no separate UAS-specific board rule located | Nevada engineering and land-surveying board materials were searched. NRS 493.103 expressly addresses land surveyors and registered businesses, but no separate active UAS-specific professional-board rule or opinion was located. |
| 11 | State preemption and local authority | Reviewed — no express blanket preemption located | NRS 493.120 directs uniform and federally harmonized interpretation but does not expressly void all local UAS enactments. No blanket state preemption record was created. |

## Open verification items

1. Obtain the written critical-facility-owner consent or airport/FAA authorization applicable to the exact site and carry the required documentation during flight.
2. Confirm Nevada business registration and document how the mission satisfies every element of the NRS 493.103 commercial or surveying exception when relying on it.
3. For public-sector work, resolve in writing whether the agency will register contractor aircraft and operators with DPS and how the agency maps the mission to an authorized public purpose.
4. Check the current DPS and Nevada technology-office restriction lists, vendor data policies, federal-funding terms, and product lineage before offering equipment or services to a public agency.
5. Obtain current park or University approval forms, insurance language, lead times, location limits, and any separate filming or occupancy permit before mobilization.
6. For pesticide application, confirm the exact Nevada pest-control business license, applicator field/category, operator qualification, product label, and any federal agricultural-aircraft requirement.
