# North Dakota UAS Regulatory Summary

## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

North Dakota combines generally applicable aircraft rules with several targeted UAS provisions. Broad aircraft definitions support application of low-flight, unsafe-operation, impairment, landing-consent, liability, and wildlife protections to drones. The UAS surveillance chapter primarily controls law enforcement and court-authorized surveillance rather than routine private mapping. Commercial project teams also face drone-inclusive harassment and stalking laws, Parks and Recreation authorization, hunter-interference limits, a detailed unmanned aerial-applicator licensing system, NDDOT survey standards, and institutional controls on public-university property. Ordinary UAS do not require separate state registration unless used for aerial application, according to the Aeronautics Commission.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### ND-001 — Lawfulness of aircraft flight and landing; liability for damage

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. §§ 2-03-01, 2-03-04, 2-03-05  
**Status:** Current / in force

Chapter 2-03 defines aircraft broadly as any contrivance used or designed for flight. Flight is lawful unless it is so low that it interferes with the owner's existing use of land, water, or the space above it, or is imminently dangerous to persons or property below. Landing on another's land or water without consent is unlawful except for a forced landing. Aircraft owners and pilots can be liable under state tort rules for injuries or property damage caused by ascent, descent, flight, or falling objects.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Plan flight altitude, route, and contingencies so the operation does not interfere with the existing use of land or create imminent danger below. Obtain landowner consent before any planned landing outside the controlled project site, and document preflight condition, incident response, and insurance because state tort liability remains possible for flight or falling-object damage.
- **Agency Practitioner:** N/A — no agency process involved
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Preserve site-access rights, planned launch and landing areas, landowner consent, flight logs, maintenance and payload records, and any incident evidence. Contract terms should allocate responsibility for property damage without implying that FAA compliance eliminates North Dakota nuisance, trespass, or tort exposure.

### ND-002 — Reckless or intoxicated aircraft operation and aircraft tampering

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. §§ 2-03-01, 2-03-10  
**Status:** Current / in force

North Dakota makes careless aircraft operation in willful disregard of others, or operation without due caution in a manner that endangers or is likely to endanger persons or property, a class A misdemeanor. Operating an aircraft while intoxicated or under the influence is also a class A misdemeanor, and a person may not act as crew or start an aircraft engine within eight hours after consuming alcohol. Unauthorized operation, control manipulation, part removal, or other intentional tampering with a civil aircraft is separately prohibited.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Brief the crew that North Dakota separately prohibits careless or impaired aircraft operation and applies an eight-hour alcohol rule to aircraft crew. Use fit-for-duty checks and controlled custody of the aircraft, controller, batteries, and configuration so unauthorized operation or tampering is detected before launch.
- **Agency Practitioner:** N/A — no agency process involved
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Make impairment, reckless operation, unauthorized control, and aircraft tampering immediate stop-work and incident-escalation events. Preserve crew-status, custody, maintenance, and flight records if conduct could implicate the state offenses rather than relying only on a generic safety report.

### ND-003 — Admissibility and warrant requirements for UAS surveillance

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. §§ 29-29.4-01 through 29-29.4-03  
**Status:** Current / in force

UAS information is inadmissible in a North Dakota prosecution or proceeding unless obtained under a search warrant or a warrant exception, and generally may not support probable cause unless obtained on that basis or through monitoring public lands or international borders. A surveillance warrant must identify authorizers, operating locations, maximum flight duration, whether individual data will be collected, and how data and derived conclusions will be used, disclosed, retained, handled, and destroyed. These controls govern governmental evidentiary use rather than ordinary private project imagery.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** An ordinary privately directed AEC collection is not governmental surveillance merely because a public client will receive a deliverable. If law enforcement directs the collection or intends evidentiary use, require written agency direction identifying the warrant or exception, collection area and duration, authorized uses, and data-handling responsibilities before flight.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Route the warrant or exception determination through the agency's supervisory and legal process; a consultant cannot create that authority. The warrant package and mission order should identify the statutory authorizers, operating locations, maximum duration, individual data expected, and rules for use, disclosure, retention, and destruction.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Define in writing whether the consultant is performing ordinary project documentation or acting at law-enforcement direction, because the evidentiary and warrant rules turn on governmental surveillance. For covered collection, retain the warrant or exception determination, mission limits, chain of custody, disclosures, retention, and destruction evidence.

### ND-004 — Statutory exceptions for specified UAS surveillance

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. § 29-29.4-04  
**Status:** Current / in force

Chapter 29-29.4 does not prohibit specified border patrol, law-enforcement exigency, or state and local authority use during environmental or weather catastrophes to protect safety or property and assess damage, erosion, flooding, or contamination. It also preserves research, education, training, testing, and development undertaken by or with a North Dakota school or higher-education institution and public or private collaborators in mutually supported efforts. The exception is not a general commercial-survey exemption and does not replace property, airspace, contract, or institutional approvals.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Do not treat the catastrophe and institutional-research exceptions as a general commercial surveying exemption. For a qualifying public-safety or mutually supported education/research mission, document the responsible authority or institution, exact statutory purpose, collaborators, operating boundary, and separate property, airspace, and contract approvals.
- **Agency Practitioner:** The responsible authority or educational institution should issue a written mission determination identifying the applicable exception, purpose, participants, geographic and temporal limits, and data controls. For collaborative research, preserve evidence that the effort is genuinely undertaken by or with the North Dakota institution and is mutually supported rather than merely vendor work relabeled as research.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Read each exception narrowly and retain the facts that satisfy it; catastrophe response, exigency, border patrol, and institutional research have different predicates. State expressly that the exception addresses chapter 29-29.4 and does not displace property permission, aviation rules, procurement terms, or institutional approval.

### ND-005 — Prohibited governmental authorization of armed UAS and private-person surveillance

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. § 29-29.4-05  
**Status:** Current / in force

A law-enforcement agency may not authorize a UAS armed with a lethal weapon. It also may not authorize, including by permit, a private person to use a UAS or robot to surveil another private person without that person's express informed consent or the consent of the owner of the real property where the person is present. Surveillance of lawful constitutional activity is prohibited unless otherwise allowed by the chapter. These provisions are tied to law-enforcement authorization and should not be overstated as a general private-flight consent statute.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Keep lethal weapons out of any law-enforcement-directed aircraft configuration and do not treat a public client's request as authority to surveil a private person. Where the statute permits reliance on consent, obtain express informed consent from the person or qualifying property owner and constrain the sensor footprint to the documented purpose.
- **Agency Practitioner:** A law-enforcement agency may not authorize a lethal-weapon-equipped UAS and should reject any mission configured that way. Before authorizing private-person surveillance, document the chapter authority and the required express informed consent, and separately screen collection involving lawful constitutional activity.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** For law-enforcement programs, configuration control and acceptance testing should demonstrate that the fielded UAS cannot carry or deploy a lethal weapon under the approved configuration. Maintain an approved-payload inventory and change-control record so later attachments cannot silently defeat that limitation.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Do not overstate this as a universal private-flight consent rule; it addresses law-enforcement authorization and specified surveillance. Retain the agency authorization, configuration record, consent language, consenting person's basis, collection limits, and handling plan before relying on a statutory consent path.

### ND-006 — Documentation, retention, and access rules for authorized UAS surveillance

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. § 29-29.4-06  
**Status:** Current / in force

Authorized surveillance must be documented with flight duration, path, and mission objectives, verified by the supervising authorized person, and retained for five years. Lawfully obtained imaging or other data not tied to reasonable and articulable suspicion, an ongoing investigation, or trial may not be retained longer than 90 days. Accused persons may obtain covered information through criminal discovery, and other interested persons may request required documentation under North Dakota's open-records chapter, subject to the statute's operational and security limitations.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** When supporting covered governmental surveillance, configure the mission record to capture flight duration, path, objectives, supervising verifier, and the data classification that controls retention. Separate unrelated imagery promptly so the agency can meet the 90-day destruction rule while preserving material tied to suspicion, investigation, or trial.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Use a documented workflow for supervisory verification, five-year flight-record retention, 90-day deletion of unrelated imaging, criminal discovery, and open-records requests subject to operational and security limits. Assign the agency—not the field contractor—to approve retention classifications, disclosures, legal holds, and destruction.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Select capture and processing systems that support role-based access, exportable audit logs, configurable retention and deletion, defensible redaction, and delivery in the agency's required format. Confirm cloud hosting, backup replication, account ownership, and verifiable vendor deletion before approving the platform for covered data.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Allocate ownership, supervision, discovery response, public-records handling, retention, legal holds, and certified destruction between the agency and consultant. Preserve the authorization, verified flight record, access and disclosure logs, classification decision, and deletion evidence for each covered mission.

### ND-007 — Drone-inclusive harassment and stalking offenses

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. §§ 12.1-17-07, 12.1-17-07.1  
**Status:** Current / in force

North Dakota's harassment and stalking statutes define a robot to include remotely piloted aircraft. Using one, with intent to frighten or harass, for offensive conduct with no legitimate purpose can be harassment. Drone acts can form a stalking course of conduct, and unauthorized tracking of a person's movements or location by robot, GPS, or electronic means can qualify when it would cause a reasonable person fear, intimidation, or harassment and serves no legitimate purpose. Documented project purpose and disciplined collection boundaries are therefore important.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Use a mission-specific collection plan for Statewide that limits camera angle, dwell time, audio, zoom, thermal capture, and retention to the contracted purpose. Brief the crew on aborting or redirecting collection when people, residences, or unrelated activity enter the sensor footprint.
- **Agency Practitioner:** N/A — no agency process involved
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document the legitimate project purpose, collection boundary, route, sensor settings, notices or access coordination, and response to complaints. Repeated targeting, unauthorized tracking, or conduct intended to frighten or harass should trigger immediate stop-work and preservation of flight and communications records.

### ND-008 — UAS operation on Parks and Recreation Department lands

**Authority:** N.D. Admin. Code §§ 58-02-08-02(14), 58-02-08-16.1  
**Status:** Current / in force

North Dakota prohibits operation of any UAS for hobby, recreation, or commercial purposes within land owned, leased, or managed by the Parks and Recreation Department unless the director or deputy director authorizes it. A violation is a class 2 noncriminal offense. The rule is broader than a launch-and-landing-only restriction because it uses operation 'within' covered lands; project teams should identify the property boundary and secure written authorization before relying on an off-property launch location for work over or inside a park unit.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Treat property authorization for All land owned, leased, or managed by the Department as a pre-mobilization gate and obtain conditions that cover launch, landing, route, dates, aircraft, crew, and sensor purpose. Confirm whether the approval is site-wide or location-specific and carry the written authorization and field contact with the crew.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Direct the operator to the Parks and Recreation Department's current authorization route and obtain director or deputy-director approval before commercial operation within covered lands. The written decision should identify the park unit and boundary, dates, aircraft, pilots, sensor purpose, conditions, and field contact.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Retain the signed director or deputy-director authorization, submitted materials, property map, operating conditions, amendments, and closeout correspondence. Because the rule prohibits operation within covered lands, do not assume an off-property launch avoids the approval requirement for a route entering the unit.

### ND-009 — Intentional killing, chasing, or harassment of wildlife from aircraft

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. §§ 2-03-01, 20.1-01-11  
**Status:** Current / in force

A person operating or controlling any aircraft in North Dakota may not intentionally kill, chase, or harass a wild animal or wild bird, whether protected or unprotected, except as the wildlife title allows or when necessary to protect life or property under the stated exceptions. Chapter 2-03 broadly defines aircraft as any contrivance used or designed for flight, supporting application to UAS. Environmental and wildlife surveys should use altitude, stand-off distance, abort criteria, and seasonal planning that avoid intentional pursuit or harassment.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Screen the mission for active hunting and wildlife sensitivity within Statewide; plan altitude, stand-off distance, route, observers, and abort criteria to avoid pursuit, harassment, surveillance of participants, or assistance to a taking. Document the project's environmental or infrastructure purpose and pause if animals materially react or the flight would disrupt lawful hunting or fishing.
- **Agency Practitioner:** N/A — no agency process involved
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Preserve the legitimate mission purpose, planned route and stand-off distances, wildlife briefing, observations of animal response, and any stop or route change. Escalate if the mission design involves intentional pursuit, hazing, or other conduct near the statute's limited life-or-property exceptions.

### ND-010 — UAS interference with lawful hunting

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. § 20.1-01-31  
**Status:** Current / in force

North Dakota expressly prohibits using an aerial vehicle that does not carry a human operator on public or private land to intentionally interfere with another person's lawful taking of wildlife or to intentionally harass, drive, or disturb wildlife for the purpose of disrupting a lawful hunt. Incidental interference from lawful public or private land use is excluded. A commercial project near active hunting should document its legitimate purpose, coordinate site access, and avoid flight behavior or scheduling that could be understood as intentional disruption.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Screen the mission for active hunting and wildlife sensitivity within Public and private land statewide; plan altitude, stand-off distance, route, observers, and abort criteria to avoid pursuit, harassment, surveillance of participants, or assistance to a taking. Document the project's environmental or infrastructure purpose and pause if animals materially react or the flight would disrupt lawful hunting or fishing.
- **Agency Practitioner:** N/A — no agency process involved
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document the project's legitimate land-use purpose, active-hunting screening, schedule or access coordination, route, wildlife response, and any complaint. Intentional disruption is the key risk, so preserve facts separating incidental effects of lawful project work from conduct aimed at a hunter or taking.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### ND-011 — Unmanned aerial applicator licensing, pilot, aircraft, and training requirements

**Authority:** N.D.C.C. § 2-05-18; N.D. Admin. Code ch. 6-02-02  
**Status:** Current / in force

Before any drone spraying in North Dakota, including on private land, the owner or operator must obtain the annual NDAC unmanned aerial applicator license and list every pilot and registered aircraft. Current agency requirements include FAA Part 137 authority, Part 107 credentials for each pilot, North Dakota pesticide certification, an identified qualified chief pilot, FAA and state aircraft registration, and a current FAA airworthiness certificate. Pilots need approved training or ten hours of supervised solo flight at operational loads, with written records. The application fee is $200; violations can be class B misdemeanors.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Treat Unmanned aerial applicator licensing, pilot, aircraft, and training requirements as a separate mobilization track from ordinary imaging: verify the operator, every pilot, each aircraft, payload, product, and mission approval before scheduling field work. Build extra lead time for licensing, aircraft configuration, training records, label review, and sensitive-site coordination.
- **Agency Practitioner:** The North Dakota Aeronautics Commission should verify the annual unmanned aerial applicator application, $200 fee, chief pilot, each remote pilot, each registered aircraft, FAA Part 137 authority, Part 107 credentials, pesticide certification, airworthiness evidence, and training records before licensing. Record changes to pilots or aircraft against the issued license rather than treating fleet substitutions as automatically covered.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Buy and configure an application system as a regulated aircraft-payload combination: verify operational weight, chemical compatibility, containment and shutoff, calibration, approved loads, registration identity, airworthiness basis, and support for required training records. Fleet planning should account for annual licensing and whether an aircraft or payload substitution changes the approved configuration.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Put spraying on a separate compliance track from imaging and retain NDAC licensing, aircraft registration, FAA and pesticide credentials, pilot and chief-pilot records, training evidence, product labels, calibration, and mission logs. Contract language should identify who is the licensed applicator and prohibit unapproved pilots, aircraft substitutions, products, or off-label work.

### ND-012 — Minimum guidelines for UAS surveys on NDDOT construction projects

**Authority:** NDDOT Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) Guidelines  
**Status:** Current / project-specific

NDDOT's minimum UAS survey guidelines require the appropriate State Plane coordinate system, survey-grade ground control and checkpoints, a mechanical shutter, at least 75 percent forward and side overlap, suitable weather and lighting, and accuracy checking after processing. Processing reports, control and checkpoint data, and accuracy reports must be submitted; imagery and outputs are maintained through final acceptance and provided to NDDOT on request. The document gives different minimum control layouts for stockpiles, borrow sites, RTK systems, and longitudinal roadway surveys.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Translate Minimum guidelines for UAS surveys on NDDOT construction projects into the project flight plan and QA/QC checklist before mobilization, including the required control, collection, accuracy, retention, and deliverable items that apply to the work. Resolve any conflict between the agency guidance and the executed scope before collection so a technically successful flight does not produce an unacceptable survey deliverable.
- **Agency Practitioner:** NDDOT project staff should confirm the survey plan and required State Plane system, control and checkpoint layout, overlap, mechanical-shutter configuration, accuracy checks, submittals, and retention before collection. Review the processing report and accuracy evidence against the contract before accepting quantities or deliverables; the guidance is not a separate flight permit.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Specify aircraft, positioning, camera, payload, processing software, and storage that can produce the cited accuracy evidence and deliverables without proprietary-format lock-in. Acceptance testing should verify the complete workflow—control, capture, processing, export, and archive—not only nominal aircraft or sensor specifications.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Identify the guideline version incorporated into the contract and resolve conflicts among the scope, survey control, accuracy, quantity, deliverable, and retention terms before flight. Preserve agency acceptance and any approved deviation so technical guidance is not later treated as an unstated warranty or a substitute for licensed-professional obligations.

### ND-013 — Approval procedure for non-university UAS activity on NDSU property

**Authority:** NDSU Policy 723 and Procedure for sUAS Flights on NDSU Property Unrelated to NDSU Research or Education  
**Status:** Current / institution-specific

NDSU policy requires compliance with aviation and other applicable law for UAS operations over university property or for university activity. For flights on NDSU property unrelated to university research or education, the linked procedure requires a Facility/Grounds Use Agreement and Flight Plan Form, prior approval, and submission at least five business days before the proposed flight. The procedure also flags the main campus's controlled-airspace setting. A consultant should confirm the current forms, reviewer, insurance terms, airspace authorization, and whether the mission instead follows NDSU's research process.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Treat property authorization for NDSU property as a pre-mobilization gate and obtain conditions that cover launch, landing, route, dates, aircraft, crew, and sensor purpose. Confirm whether the approval is site-wide or location-specific and carry the written authorization and field contact with the crew.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Route a non-research or non-education flight through NDSU's current Facility/Grounds Use Agreement and Flight Plan process at least five business days in advance. The approval record should address the operating area, controlled-airspace authorization, pilots, aircraft, insurance, dates, purpose, conditions, and campus field contact.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** N/A — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Retain the executed facility-use agreement, approved flight plan, insurance, airspace evidence, conditions, amendments, and campus communications. Confirm whether the mission belongs under the outside-operator procedure or NDSU's separate research process before relying on the five-business-day route.

### ND-014 — State UAS replacement program for federal security compliance

**Authority:** 2025 N.D. Sess. Laws ch. 50, § 14 (S.B. 2018)  
**Status:** Active program / public-agency context

North Dakota appropriated $9 million for the Department of Commerce to coordinate replacement of state-owned UAS that do not comply with the federal National Defense Authorization Act and American Security Drone Act. The program includes state agencies and higher-education institutions and is a public-fleet transition measure, not a blanket prohibition on private ownership or operation. However, government solicitations and federally funded projects may impose manufacturer or equipment eligibility conditions, so a consultant should verify the funding source and current client specifications before proposing a platform.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** This replacement appropriation governs state-owned fleets, not ordinary private ownership or operation. For government or federally funded work, confirm during proposal and again before aircraft assignment whether the solicitation or funding terms impose NDAA, American Security Drone Act, manufacturer, component, or data-security eligibility on consultant-furnished equipment.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Inventory each state-owned UAS, manufacturer, components, serial number, software, funding source, and federal-compliance basis, then coordinate eligible replacement through the Department of Commerce program. Preserve prioritization, disposition, replacement, configuration, and funding records so a noncompliant aircraft is not returned to covered service.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Treat the program as a fleet-transition and lifecycle task: validate federal eligibility at purchase and delivery, retain manufacturer and component evidence, test the approved configuration, and plan secure disposition and data migration for replaced systems. Do not infer that a model eligible for this program automatically satisfies every project-specific or later federal requirement.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Do not describe the appropriation as a statewide private-UAS ban; its direct subjects are state agencies and higher-education fleets. For consultant work, make any federal-security flowdown explicit in the solicitation or contract and retain the funding, eligibility, and incorporated-standard basis for rejecting or accepting equipment.

## 4. Non-Regulatory Context

North Dakota operates a mature public UAS ecosystem through the Northern Plains UAS Test Site and the Vantis beyond-visual-line-of-sight network. These programs may create research, testing, and advanced-operation opportunities, but participation or geographic proximity does not itself authorize a commercial mission. The State's 2025-2027 UAS replacement program is aimed at government-fleet compliance with federal equipment restrictions; it is not a general ban on privately owned foreign-manufactured drones. The Aeronautics Commission also recommends proactive coordination near public and private airstrips for agricultural UAS work even where a separate state coordination mandate may not apply.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

1. Confirm launch, landing, access, and low-altitude overflight consent for the actual project parcel and adjacent staging areas.
2. Determine whether any public-agency mission is surveillance under chapter 29-29.4 and, if so, allocate warrant, flight-log, data-retention, deletion, discovery, and records-request duties.
3. For Parks and Recreation land, obtain current written director or deputy-director authorization and all property-specific conditions.
4. Check active hunting seasons and wildlife sensitivities, and document stand-off, altitude, route, observer, and abort controls that avoid pursuit, harassment, or hunt interference.
5. For dispensing or spraying, verify the annual NDAC license, every aircraft and pilot listing, chief-pilot qualifications, training records, Part 137 status, pesticide credentials, airworthiness, insurance, product registration, label, and sensitive-area requirements.
6. For NDDOT work, confirm the contract's controlling survey specification, coordinate system, accuracy class, control/checkpoint layout, required supplemental survey, processing report, file format, retention period, and ownership of imagery and derived products.
7. For NDSU or another public institution, identify the correct institutional workflow, current forms, lead time, insurance, equipment eligibility, research/privacy review, and controlled-airspace authorization.
8. For a state or federally funded client, confirm whether the proposed aircraft and components satisfy the solicitation's current manufacturer, cybersecurity, data-hosting, and supply-chain conditions.
