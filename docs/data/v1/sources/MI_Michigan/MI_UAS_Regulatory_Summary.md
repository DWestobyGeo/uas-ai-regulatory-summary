## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Michigan's core UAS framework is the Unmanned Aircraft Systems Act (2016 PA 436, MCL 259.301–259.331), which combines a state preemption clause with a set of direct operator prohibitions. MCL 259.305 bars a "political subdivision" (county, city, village, township, or similar public corporation) from enacting or enforcing an ordinance regulating UAS ownership or operation, subject to narrow carve-outs (a political subdivision's own UAS fleet; a limited horse-safety ordinance where nonemergency motor vehicles are already banned). A December 26, 2025 Michigan Court of Claims decision, *Michigan Coalition of Drone Operators, Inc v Regents of Univ of Mich*, held that this preemption does not extend to Michigan's constitutional public universities, because a university is not a "political subdivision" under the Act — meaning the University of Michigan's own campus drone ordinance survived a state- and federal-preemption challenge, and, by the same reasoning, Michigan State University's and Wayne State University's ordinances likely would as well. Beyond preemption, the Act separately prohibits UAS-based interference with law enforcement, fire, EMS, search-and-rescue, and corrections personnel (MCL 259.321); UAS-based harassment, protective-order violations, and privacy invasions (MCL 259.322); and unauthorized state-agency (and contractor) UAS surveillance of permitted facilities (MCL 259.307). Michigan's Natural Resources and Environmental Protection Act separately prohibits using an unmanned vehicle or device to take game or fish (MCL 324.40111c) or to hinder another person's lawful taking of game or fish (MCL 324.40112) — a narrower pairing than some secondary sources describe. The DNR's Land Use Orders of the Director impose location-specific UAS restrictions and a commercial-use written-permission requirement on state parks, recreation areas, state forest campgrounds, and boating access sites. MDOT has separately imposed a 2026 Design Advisory prohibiting Chinese-manufactured or foreign-adversary-controlled UAS components on any MDOT-let design or construction-engineering contract — a procurement restriction that expressly reaches AEC consultants, not just MDOT employees. No UAS-specific guidance from Michigan's Board of Professional Surveyors was located. A reported 15-bill "SHIELD" legislative package addressing critical-infrastructure no-fly zones, a state drone registry, and expanded foreign-manufacturer procurement bans remains largely pending, with only two of its bills (HB 5329, HB 5331) having passed the House as of this research date.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Unmanned Aircraft Systems Act — Political Subdivision Preemption — MCL 259.305
*Binding statute | Current*

**Objective Summary:** MCL 259.305(1) provides that, except as expressly authorized by statute, a political subdivision "shall not enact or enforce an ordinance or resolution that regulates the ownership or operation of unmanned aircraft or otherwise engage in the regulation of the ownership or operation of unmanned aircraft." A narrow carve-out (subsection (2)) allows a political subdivision that already bans nonemergency motor vehicles to adopt an ordinance addressing UAS interference with horse-drawn commercial activity, subject to required exceptions and an FAA fixed-site-facility petition requirement. Subsection (3) preserves a political subdivision's authority to regulate its own UAS fleet. Subsection (5) provides that if the Act conflicts with the NREPA hunting/anti-harassment provisions (MCL 324.40111c or 324.40112), those sections control. "Political subdivision" is defined at MCL 259.303(d) as "a county, city, village, township, or other political subdivision, public corporation, authority, or district in this state."

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Plan environmental and mapping flights to avoid conduct that could be characterized as locating, pursuing, disturbing, or harassing wildlife. Coordinate mission timing and stand-off distances with the land manager when project work overlaps sensitive habitat or hunting activity.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document the applicability analysis and any exception relied upon in the project file. Align the scope, client representations, and operator procedures with the verified authority, and seek state counsel if material facts fall outside its clear terms.
### Restriction on State-Agency UAS Surveillance of Permitted Facilities — MCL 259.307
*Binding statute | Current*

**Objective Summary:** MCL 259.307(1) bars a Michigan department, agency, board, or commission, or a person under contract with or acting at the direction of or on behalf of one, from using a UAS to surveil, inspect, gather evidence about, or collect information concerning a facility subject to a permit, license, order, or decree issued or governed by that entity, unless: the facility owner/operator gives express consent; the entity acts under a valid search warrant; the entity reasonably believes there is an imminent threat to public health, safety, property, or natural resources; or the entity is inspecting a roadway, highway, airport, airport approach corridor, port, or similar infrastructure. Data collected under an exception must be furnished to the facility owner/operator on request and is rebuttably presumed exempt from Michigan's Freedom of Information Act. The section does not apply to a law enforcement agency operating a UAS as part of a law enforcement investigation.

### Prohibition on UAS Interference with Law Enforcement, Emergency, and Corrections Personnel — MCL 259.321
*Binding statute | Current*

**Objective Summary:** MCL 259.321 prohibits an individual from knowingly and intentionally operating a UAS in a manner that interferes with the official duties of a law enforcement official, a firefighter, EMS personnel, search-and-rescue personnel, a state correctional officer, or a local corrections officer. MCL 259.323(1) makes a violation a misdemeanor punishable by up to 90 days' imprisonment, a fine of up to $500, or both.

### Prohibition on UAS Harassment, Protective-Order Violation, and Invasion of Privacy — MCL 259.322
*Binding statute | Current*

**Objective Summary:** MCL 259.322 prohibits knowingly and intentionally operating a UAS to: (1) subject an individual to "harassment" as defined in MCL 750.411h or 750.411i; (2) come within a distance of an individual that, if done in person rather than by remote UAS operation, would violate a restraining or other judicial order; (3) violate Michigan's surveillance statute (MCL 750.539j) or otherwise capture images/audio of an individual in a manner invading a reasonable expectation of privacy; or (4), for a registered sex offender, knowingly and intentionally follow, contact, or capture images of another individual where the offender's criminal sentence would otherwise prohibit that conduct. A violation is a misdemeanor under MCL 259.323 (up to 90 days, a $500 fine, or both).

### Hunting/Fishing UAS Restriction and Anti-Hunter-Harassment Provision — MCL 324.40111c; MCL 324.40112
*Binding statute | Current*

**Objective Summary:** MCL 324.40111c(2) provides that "an individual shall not take game or fish using an unmanned vehicle or unmanned device that uses aerodynamic forces to achieve flight or using an unmanned vehicle or unmanned device that operates on the surface of water or underwater." Separately, MCL 324.40112(2)(c) makes it unlawful to use such a device, among other listed methods, "to affect animal or fish behavior in order to hinder or prevent the lawful taking of an animal or a fish" by another person. A violation of MCL 324.40112 is a misdemeanor (up to 93 days and a $500–$1,000 fine for a first offense, with enhanced penalties for repeat violations, plus mandatory revocation of any department-issued hunting/fishing permit or license).

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Keep flights within the documented project purpose and avoid lingering over neighboring property, people, livestock, or agricultural operations. Brief crews on intent-based harassment risk and retain the mission plan and client scope supporting the legitimate AEC purpose.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### Land Use Orders of the Director — UAS Restrictions and Commercial-Use Permission Requirement on DNR-Managed Land
*Official agency policy (administrative order under NREPA § 504, MCL 324.504) | Current*

**Objective Summary:** The DNR's official State Parks Rules FAQ page states that, "in accordance with the Unmanned Aircraft System Act (PA 436 of 2016) and rule 5.1(5)," drone operators must not fly in a state park or recreation area: in a manner that knowingly and intentionally interferes with department employees or their designees performing official duties or search-and-rescue operations; within 100 yards of a cultural or historical site or structure; upon or over the viewing platforms at Tahquamenon Falls or the platform at Palms Book State Park; over an occupied beach area, equestrian facility, campground, or restroom/open-air changing court; over an area subject to an aerial right-of-way; or for a commercial purpose without first obtaining written permission from an authorized DNR representative under Mich Admin Code R 299.922(dd). The Land Use Orders of the Director contain materially identical UAS provisions for state-operated public boating access sites/harbors (Order 2.103(g)) and state forest campgrounds (Order 4.4(4)), each independently verified in this pass.

### Design Advisory DA 2026-04 — American Security Drone Act of 2023 (Prohibited Foreign-Manufactured UAS Components on MDOT-Let Projects)
*Official agency policy | Current (issued April 24, 2026)*

**Objective Summary:** MDOT Design Advisory DA 2026-04 states that, in accordance with the American Security Drone Act of 2023 (ASDA) and OMB Memorandum M-26-02, MDOT "prohibits the use of any aircraft, flight controller, payloads, ground control station, software and firmware supplied by a manufacturer that is based in, or subject to the control of a foreign adversary, specifically the People's Republic of China," on all MDOT-let projects and all phases, "including but not limited to Design and Construction Engineering." Only UAS vetted by the U.S. government and listed as NDAA-compliant may be associated with an MDOT-let project, and no costs associated with a prohibited drone are allowable to be billed to MDOT. MDOT stated further guidance on the treatment of direct and indirect UAS costs would follow in a future Consultant Advisory. This is a Design Division advisory, not a MDOT Office of Aeronautics document; no separate MDOT Aeronautics-specific internal UAS operations policy was located in this pass.

### Michigan Coalition of Drone Operators, Inc v Regents of University of Michigan — Preemption Scope for Public Universities
*Court decision (Michigan Court of Claims) | Current; final order, appellate status not independently confirmed*

**Objective Summary:** In *Michigan Coalition of Drone Operators, Inc v Regents of Univ of Mich*, No. 24-000079-MZ (Mich Ct Claims, Dec. 26, 2025), the Court of Claims (Swartzle, C.J.) granted summary disposition for the University of Michigan, rejecting a facial challenge to the Regents' drone ordinance (Article XVI), which generally bans outdoor UAS takeoff, landing, or operation on University of Michigan property (Ann Arbor, Dearborn, and Flint campuses) absent a written waiver or a listed exemption (FAA-compliant law-enforcement use, or indoor use under university safety policy). The court held the University is not a "political subdivision" under MCL 259.303(d) because it is a constitutional corporation under Const 1963, art 8, § 5 with authority coordinate with the Legislature, so MCL 259.305 does not expressly, field-, or conflict-preempt the ordinance. The court separately held the ordinance is not federally preempted because it targets privacy and campus-security objectives distinct from aviation safety/airspace efficiency, does not conflict with a specific FAA regulation, and contains its own savings clause deferring to federal law in case of conflict.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **Preemption gap for public universities:** *Michigan Coalition of Drone Operators v Regents of Univ of Mich* (Dec. 2025) confirmed MCL 259.305 does not preempt a Michigan constitutional public university's own drone ordinance. AEC firms should independently confirm the current drone policy and waiver process at each University of Michigan campus, and should not assume Michigan State University's or Wayne State University's ordinances (if any) are preempted by the state Act, even though this decision addressed only the University of Michigan directly. Whether this decision has been or will be appealed was not confirmed in this pass.
- **Order 5.1's primary text not independently opened:** The DNR's UAS restrictions for state parks and recreation areas were verified through the DNR's own official FAQ page (which quotes "rule 5.1(5)" directly) and through materially identical sibling provisions in the Land Use Orders of the Director for boating access sites/harbors and state forest campgrounds, but Order 5.1's own primary chapter was not located within the specific combined LUOD PDF retrieved in this pass. Confirm the complete, current Order 5.1 text directly with DNR before relying on this citation for a specific state-park project.
- **MDOT Aeronautics-specific UAS policy:** No internal MDOT Office of Aeronautics UAS operations policy (as distinct from the Design Division's DA 2026-04 procurement advisory) was located in this pass. AEC firms should confirm with their MDOT contract administrator whether any additional Aeronautics-specific requirements apply to a given project.
- **MDOT's forthcoming Consultant Advisory on UAS cost treatment:** DA 2026-04 states that MDOT will issue further guidance addressing direct and indirect cost treatment for UAS usage in a future Consultant Advisory, which had not been identified or reviewed as of this research date.
- **Pending "SHIELD" package status:** Only 2 of 15 reported SHIELD bills (HB 5329, HB 5331) had passed the Michigan House as of the most recent secondary reporting reviewed (May 14, 2026); none had passed the Senate or been signed into law. This status could not be independently re-confirmed against each bill's own official legislature.mi.gov page in this pass due to a tool-availability interruption and should be rechecked before relying on any pending-bill status stated in this report.
- **Land-surveying licensure and UAS-derived deliverables:** No Board rule or guidance specifically addressing UAS-derived (as opposed to manned-aircraft) photogrammetric or LiDAR survey products was located, and the LARA Surveyor FAQ PDF did not yield extractable UAS-specific text in this pass. Confirm directly with LARA's Board of Professional Surveyors before relying on this citation for a specific project's licensure determination.
