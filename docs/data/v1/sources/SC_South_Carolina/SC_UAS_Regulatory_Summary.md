## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

South Carolina is in a statutory transition. Through December 31, 2026, operations near state correctional and local detention facilities are governed by 500-foot horizontal and 250-foot vertical limits, with a detailed notification exception for registered commercial infrastructure work. On January 1, 2027, Act 150 replaces those two restrictions with a 1,500-foot horizontal rule for correctional, detention, and military property and adds state offenses involving federal compliance, registration, malicious facility data collection, contraband, and weaponization. Separate state-agency layers include professional-board treatment of UAV photogrammetry, state-park filming approval and insurance, and covered-foreign-UAS restrictions on federally funded SCDOT work.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### SC-001 — Current correctional and local detention facility UAS buffers and infrastructure exception
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force through December 31, 2026*

**Objective Summary:** Through December 31, 2026, South Carolina prohibits UAS operation within 500 feet horizontally or 250 feet vertically of a Department of Corrections facility or local detention facility without written consent from the Corrections Director or jail administrator. A federally registered commercial operator is excepted when monitoring, operating, maintaining, enhancing, or assessing repairs to electric, communications, water-conveyance, or transportation infrastructure, but must notify the appropriate official separately for each operation no more than five days and no less than two hours beforehand and provide the FAA registration number. Violations may lead to misdemeanor penalties and aircraft confiscation.

### SC-002 — Correctional facility boundary and FAA fixed-site publication duties
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** The Department of Corrections must petition the FAA to designate qualifying detention and correctional facilities as fixed-site facilities, and the South Carolina Aeronautics Commission must publish FAA designations. Corrections and local detention facilities also must provide site lists and electronic UAS-boundary data to the Commission for publication in its GIS. The Commission currently provides an SCDOC UAV No Fly Zone Map and identifies SCDOC as the data owner. These mapping duties support flight planning but do not replace the operator obligation to apply the governing statutory distance, obtain required permission or notice, and check current FAA restrictions.

### SC-003 — Act 150 federal compliance, registration, and controlled-airspace offenses
*Binding statute or regulation | Enacted / not yet effective*

**Objective Summary:** Beginning January 1, 2027, Act 150 requires compliance with applicable FAA registration, operating, airspace, waiver, exemption, and authorization requirements and states that South Carolina does not supersede federal airspace authority. It creates state misdemeanors for intentionally or knowingly operating, taking off, or landing in FAA-designated controlled airspace without required authorization and for specified knowing registration violations, including unregistered commercial operation. Penalties escalate for repeat offenses, and each violated subsection or provision is a separate offense. The act defines a commercial operator by business-purpose operation in compliance with FAA rules but does not create a separate state commercial license.

### SC-004 — Act 150 correctional, detention, and military facility 1,500-foot restrictions
*Binding statute or regulation | Enacted / not yet effective*

**Objective Summary:** On January 1, 2027, Act 150 replaces the current correctional statutes with a prohibition on intentionally or knowingly operating, taking off, or landing directly above or within 1,500 feet horizontally of a known federal, state, county, or municipal correctional or detention facility unless the appropriate corrections or governing authority gives express written authorization or consent. The same 1,500-foot rule applies to a known military installation without prior express written consent from the Department of Defense or installation commander. The act excepts facility or military personnel acting within official duties, but does not carry forward the current commercial infrastructure notification exception.

### SC-005 — Act 150 contraband, malicious facility surveillance, weaponization, and seizure provisions
*Binding statute or regulation | Enacted / not yet effective*

**Objective Summary:** Beginning January 1, 2027, Act 150 creates felony offenses for operating, taking off, landing, or possessing a drone with intent to deliver contraband to a correctional or detention facility; collect visual, photographic, video, geospatial, thermal, or other sensory information about a military or correctional facility to identify vulnerabilities or for another unlawful purpose; or weaponize the drone or threaten or harm persons or property. Aircraft involved may be seized, retained, transferred, or destroyed after proceedings, subject to an innocent-owner process. The data-collection offense requires the stated malicious or unlawful intent; it is not a general ban on every map or image.

### SC-006 — Act 150 state-local regulatory boundary for public-property operations
*Binding statute or regulation | Enacted / not yet effective*

**Objective Summary:** Effective January 1, 2027, a South Carolina municipality, county, or other political subdivision may not enact an ordinance that conflicts with Article 3 or FAA regulations. The act nevertheless preserves local authority to impose reasonable restrictions on drone takeoff and landing sites and on operation on or above public property within the jurisdiction when the subject is not otherwise covered by the article. This is conflict preemption rather than a complete displacement of local UAS rules. Local public-property permission and operating restrictions may therefore remain material even after the statewide act becomes effective.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### SC-007 — Professional board policy on UAV scanning, LiDAR, and photogrammetric surveying
*Official agency policy | Current board policy*

**Objective Summary:** The South Carolina engineering and surveying board states that it regulates professional practice, not drones or other tools, and recognizes scanning, LiDAR, and similar UAV methods as acceptable techniques for Tier A or Tier B surveying. In later official minutes, the Board distinguished taking a photograph from photogrammetry and explained that creating measured, overlapping-image products such as contours constitutes licensed photogrammetric practice. The operator credential and the professional credential therefore address different functions: using a drone does not itself require a Board license, but offering or producing regulated survey or photogrammetric work must be performed within the applicable professional-licensure framework.

### SC-008 — State park drone coordination and commercial filming permit requirements
*Permit or property-use requirement | Current agency process*

**Objective Summary:** South Carolina State Parks directs anyone seeking to bring a drone to a state park to contact that park directly. The South Carolina Film Commission identifies a permit contact for filming within state-owned parks and expressly reminds commercial aerial-drone operators of Part 107 requirements. Its production permit guidance requires an original certificate of insurance, at least one million dollars in liability coverage, naming the State and its agents as additional insured, and indemnification of the State. Because the public FAQ does not publish a uniform drone approval form or processing time, location-specific authorization and operating conditions must be confirmed before mobilization.

### SC-009 — SCDOT federally funded project restriction on covered foreign UAS
*Official agency policy | Current contract requirement*

**Objective Summary:** SCDOT Local Public Agency required provisions implement the American Security Drone Act and OMB Memorandum M-26-02 for federally funded work. The special provision directs contractors to refrain from purchasing or using UAS manufactured by covered foreign entities within existing fleets for operations, maintenance, or data processing on any federally funded project or grant. It points to the Federal Acquisition Security Council list on SAM.gov and a cleared-drone list. This is a project-funding and contract rule rather than a statewide ban on private commercial ownership, but it can disqualify aircraft, associated services, or processing workflows proposed for covered transportation work.
