## 1. State overview

Virginia preempts most political-subdivision regulation of privately owned UAS, but permits takeoff-and-landing rules on subdivision-owned property under Virginia Aviation Board procedures and exceptions. State criminal provisions address operations near dwellings after notice or for harassment, federal UAS security restrictions, correctional facilities, utilities and critical infrastructure, contracted defense facilities, privacy-sensitive peeping, and specified protected persons. State law also limits investigative UAS use by public bodies while preserving non-law-enforcement government missions and private commercial use.

Property-use rules are important for field planning. Virginia State Parks prohibit voluntary landing or unlawful operation within a park. Department of Wildlife Resources rules prohibit unauthorized use on department-owned lands as well as drone hunting and wildlife harassment. Unmanned aircraft are exempt from Virginia aircraft registration. No enacted general manufacturer or country-of-origin restriction was located; the current UAS procurement limitation is confined to weaponized aircraft acquired by specified public agencies.

## 2. Statewide laws

### VA-001 — Residential proximity, harassment, and notice to desist

**Authority:** Va. Code § 18.2-121.3(A)(i), (C)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 18.2-121.3 makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to knowingly and intentionally cause a UAS to enter another's property and come within 50 feet of a dwelling house either to coerce, intimidate, or harass another person, or after actual notice to desist for any other reason. Subsection C states exceptions involving consent, federally authorized and otherwise lawful operation consistent with federal regulations, employee official business, and specified utility or facility employees. The provision requires the stated intent, proximity, property-entry, or notice elements; it is not written as a general statewide setback from every dwelling.

### VA-002 — Federal UAS security restrictions and correctional facilities

**Status:** Current / in force

### VA-004 — Contracted defense facilities and controlled technical information

**Authority:** Va. Code § 18.2-121.3(D)  
**Status:** Current / in force; effective July 1, 2025

Subsection D makes it a Class 4 felony to knowingly, intentionally, and without authorization cause a UAS to enter a contracted defense facility's property and obtain or attempt to obtain imagery containing or revealing controlled technical information. This rule applies notwithstanding subsections A–C. The statute defines a contracted defense facility by specified Department of Defense contract work and defines controlled technical information by reference to DFARS clause 252.204-7012, excluding lawfully public information. It also gives qualifying facility owners, operators, and employees limited immunity for certain defensive actions that do not injure a person.

### VA-005 — UAS peeping or spying into dwellings

**Authority:** Va. Code § 18.2-130.1(B)–(C)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 18.2-130.1 makes it unlawful to knowingly and intentionally cause a UAS to secretly or furtively peep or spy, or attempt to peep or spy, into or through an opening of a building, structure, or enclosure occupied or intended as a dwelling. The conduct must be without just cause and occur under circumstances violating the occupant's reasonable expectation of privacy. A violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. The section does not apply to a lawful criminal investigation.

### VA-006 — Status-specific following, contact, and imaging restrictions

**Authority:** Va. Code § 18.2-324.2  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 18.2-324.2 prohibits a person required to register under § 9.1-901 from knowingly and intentionally using a UAS to follow or contact another person without permission, or to capture recognizable images of another person without permission. It separately prohibits a respondent under specified protective orders from knowingly and intentionally using a UAS to follow, contact, or capture images of the petitioner or another individual named in the order. Either violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. These are status-specific operator restrictions rather than general commercial UAS rules.

### VA-007 — Public-body investigative use and weaponized public UAS

**Authority:** Va. Code § 19.2-60.1  
**Status:** Current / in force; amended in 2026

Section 19.2-60.1 generally requires a search, administrative, or inspection warrant when covered state or local bodies use UAS for criminal-law or regulatory investigations. It enumerates warrantless exceptions, including alerts, immediate danger, crash reconstruction, consent, certain arrest operations, investigation of UAS around government property or public critical infrastructure, and specified DEQ enforcement. Non-law-enforcement government support such as damage, traffic, flood, and wildfire assessment is outside the warrant requirement, and the section does not prohibit private commercial use. Public deployment or facilitation of weaponized UAS is generally prohibited, subject to stated exceptions.

### VA-008 — Local preemption and subdivision-owned property

**Authority:** Va. Code § 15.2-926.3  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 15.2-926.3 prohibits a political subdivision from regulating privately owned UAS use within its boundaries. A subdivision may regulate UAS takeoff and landing on property it owns, but the measure must follow Department of Aviation rules, be reported to the department, and be published through the statutory process. The section preserves access controls and enumerates protected operations, including federally compliant commercial operations, certain emergency or maintenance support, technical-malfunction landings, public-safety operations, and specified federal-government work.

**Practical Interpretation**

### VA-009 — State aircraft-registration exemption

**Authority:** Va. Code § 5.1-5(A)  
**Status:** Current / in force; amended in 2025

Virginia generally requires specified resident, based, aerial-application, and intrastate for-hire civil aircraft to register with the Department of Aviation before operation. Section 5.1-5(A), however, expressly states that an owner of an unmanned aircraft as defined in § 19.2-60.1 is not required to register that aircraft. This is an exemption from Virginia's aircraft-registration program, not an exemption from applicable federal registration, FAA operating requirements, property-use restrictions, or the other state UAS provisions summarized here.

### VA-010 — Acquisition and use of weaponized UAVs by state agencies

### VA-011 — Attorney General interpretation of local zoning authority

**Authority:** Virginia Attorney General Opinion 22-046 (Jan. 6, 2023)  
**Status:** Current persuasive official interpretation

Opinion 22-046 concludes that Virginia law limits local authority over privately owned drones to regulation of takeoffs and landings on property owned by political subdivisions. It states that a locality therefore may not use zoning regulations to prohibit privately owned drone takeoffs and landings on private property or subject that activity to conditional- or special-use permit requirements. The opinion interprets § 15.2-926.3 and does not eliminate independent state law, federal aviation requirements, private property rights, or the subdivision-owned-property rules authorized by that section.

## 3. State agency requirements and guidance

### VA-012 — Political-subdivision takeoff and landing regulations

**Authority:** 24VAC5-20-410 through 24VAC5-20-450  
**Status:** Current / in force; effective October 25, 2023

Virginia Aviation Board Part VIII permits political subdivisions to regulate UAS takeoff and landing on property they own. Rules may establish operating times and designate mapped properties presenting specific public-safety or natural-resource risks, where operators can be asked to demonstrate purpose and risk controls. Adoption requires Department of Aviation consistency review, public notice and hearing, reporting, and department publication before effectiveness. The regulation limits permits in designated-use areas, restricts rules for sub-0.55-pound aircraft, and preserves commercial, emergency, public-safety, governmental, and other stated exceptions.

### VA-013 — Virginia State Parks aviation rule

**Authority:** 4VAC5-30-400  
**Status:** Current / in force

Virginia's State Parks aviation rule provides that no person may voluntarily land or unlawfully operate within or upon a park an airplane, remote-control model aircraft, helicopter, unmanned aerial system, drone, balloon, parachute, or other aviation apparatus. “Voluntarily” means anything other than a forced landing. Rescue and evacuation aircraft are exempt for emergencies and approved training exercises. The rule does not state a general commercial UAS permit or waiver process, and DCR's current public guidance describes drones as not flyable in state parks under this provision.

### VA-014 — Wildlife and Department of Wildlife Resources lands

**Authority:** 4VAC15-20-240  
**Status:** Current / in force

Except as authorized by the DWR director or designee, 4VAC15-20-240 prohibits using a drone or unmanned aircraft to hunt, take, kill, drive, or herd wild animals for hunting or trapping; to harass wildlife by significantly disrupting normal behavior; or on department-owned lands. It also prohibits hunting or assisting a hunt on the same calendar day on property after using a drone to locate or surveil wildlife during an open season. The rule preserves department-authorized wildlife management and specified federal or animal-control activities, and allows certain department employees, contractors, or agents acting for approved needs.

## 4. Non-Regulatory Context

The 2025 General Assembly considered HB 2038, which would have restricted public-body procurement and use of defined insecure UAS and directed development of state cybersecurity certification and approved-model lists. The bill was left in House General Laws and did not become current law. It is included here only as a future-update signal, not as an operative requirement.
