## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Washington requires mandatory, fee-based state UAS registration (RCW 47.68.250) layered on top of FAA registration — a firm-wide compliance item to build into equipment onboarding. State law does not broadly preempt local UAS ordinances; local-ordinance research is deferred to a later phase per current research instructions and is not covered in this document. A general criminal voyeurism statute and a wildlife-harassment administrative rule fill some of the role that a dedicated UAS privacy statute would otherwise play. A widely repeated piece of third-party misinformation about a state drone-labeling/consent law is addressed explicitly below because it appears across many secondary sources and could otherwise be mistakenly relied upon.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### RCW 47.68.250 — Registration of aircraft
*Binding law | Mandatory since April 1, 2022*

**Objective Summary:** Every aircraft, including commercial unpiloted aircraft systems, must be registered with WSDOT for each calendar year operated or based in Washington. $15/drone/year, renews annually in January, in addition to FAA registration. A later-effective version of the statute is queued for January 1, 2027.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Add WSDOT registration and its January renewal to the standard equipment-onboarding and annual-compliance checklist for every drone that will fly in Washington — it's easy to miss because it sits outside the FAA registration workflow most crews already know.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Registration lapses are a straightforward compliance gap to catch in an internal audit; require proof of current WSDOT registration before dispatching any airframe on a Washington job, the same way you'd confirm current insurance and FAA Part 107 currency.

### RCW 9A.44.115 — Voyeurism
*Binding law (general, not UAS-specific)*

**Objective Summary:** First-degree voyeurism is a Class C felony, second-degree a gross misdemeanor, covering non-consensual photography/filming of a person with a reasonable expectation of privacy. Not drone-specific, but is the operative privacy-law risk for aerial photography/videography near residences, worksite trailers, or occupied structures.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Set standard flight paths and camera angles to avoid lingering over residential windows or private yards during construction-site or corridor mapping flights — basic flight-planning hygiene that also keeps the crew clear of this statute.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Train crews that intent matters less than appearance here — even incidental capture of private areas on video can generate a complaint; retain flight-path logs and camera-gimbal settings as a defense record for any residential-adjacent job.

### Drone labeling / overflight-consent claim — not current law
*Repealed, expired, or superseded authority (never enacted)*

**Objective Summary:** Many third-party drone-law aggregator sites assert Washington has a codified requirement that drones be labeled with owner name/phone number and that operators must obtain landowner consent before overflying private property, often mis-citing "RCW 47.68.380." Verified directly: RCW 47.68.380 is actually titled "Aerial search and rescue — Liability — Definition" and is unrelated. The labeling/consent language originates from House Bill 1049 (2017-18), which stalled in the House Technology & Economic Development Committee and was never enacted.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Don't build a labeling or per-overflight-consent step into Washington field procedures based on this claim — it isn't required by state law. Rely on the actual applicable rules (registration, park rules, privacy statute) instead.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Correct this specific point if it appears in any client-facing compliance document or internal training material sourced from a drone-law aggregator site; citing a nonexistent statute undermines the credibility of the whole compliance package.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### DOT / Aviation — Washington State Department of Transportation, Aviation Division
*Official agency policy*

**Objective Summary:** WSDOT administers the RCW 47.68.250 registration program. Exemptions requiring no action: hobby/recreation-only use, government-owned non-commercial aircraft, certain academic exemption operations, foreign-registered aircraft. Exemptions requiring a request form: temporarily unairworthy WA-based aircraft, nonresident-owned aircraft in WA less than 90 days, manufacturer test/demo aircraft. State UAS Coordinator: Eddy Hensley (eddy.hensley@wsdot.wa.gov).

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Confirm none of the exemptions apply before assuming a project drone is exempt — most standard commercial fleet equipment based or operated in Washington will need registration, so build the WSDOT process into new-equipment intake rather than treating it as an edge case.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* If claiming a nonresident/short-term exemption for an out-of-state crew flying briefly in Washington, track the 90-day clock carefully and file the exemption request form in advance rather than relying on the exemption applying automatically.

### Parks and Public Lands — Washington State Parks and Recreation Commission
*Binding administrative regulation*

**Objective Summary:** WAC 352-32-130: UAS flight in a WA state park is permitted only within a director-designated flying area under an approved management plan, or under a specific director/designee permit requiring at least 60 days' advance application (waivable in extenuating circumstances) for staff review and SEPA compliance.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Build a 60-day lead time into project scheduling for any job requiring flight on or launched from Washington State Parks land — identify park-sited work early in the proposal stage.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Submit the permit application with enough buffer to absorb SEPA review delays, and retain the approved permit in the project file; do not rely on the extenuating-circumstances waiver as a planning assumption.

### Fish and Wildlife — Washington Fish and Wildlife Commission
*Binding administrative regulation*

**Objective Summary:** WAC 220-413-070: unlawful to use a vehicle, aircraft, unmanned aircraft, or motor-propelled boat to pursue, concentrate, or harass wild animals/birds, except as authorized by WDFW for wildlife-conflict purposes. Also unlawful to use aircraft (including UAS) to spot/locate/report wildlife location for hunting, and to hunt game animals/birds/migratory birds on the same day one has operated a UAS.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Avoid low-altitude pursuit-like maneuvers near wildlife concentrations on environmental-monitoring and corridor-mapping flights — steady, predictable flight paths at planned survey altitude are unlikely to implicate this rule, but circling or diving near animals could.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Document flight altitude and purpose for survey work near known wildlife concentrations; the hunting-day restriction is not directly relevant to commercial survey operations but is worth a one-line mention in field-crew orientation.

### Corrections — 2016 Senate Bill 6437 (status uncertain)
*Proposed or pending authority — enactment not independently confirmed*

**Objective Summary:** SB 6437 (2016), as introduced, would add a new RCW 72.09 section requiring DOC to acquire/utilize drone detection and deterrence technology, and a new RCW 9A.76 section making it a Class C felony to knowingly operate a drone over or within 1,000 ft of a correctional facility perimeter without DOC authorization. The bill was reported out of the Senate Law & Justice Committee as a substitute bill in 2016; whether it was ultimately enacted and its current codified RCW section were not independently confirmed in this research pass.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Until this is confirmed one way or the other, plan any flight near a Washington correctional facility as if the 1,000 ft/felony rule is in force — the operational cost of extra standoff distance is trivial compared to the cost of guessing wrong on a felony statute.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* This is a high-priority open item: before authorizing any flight near a WA correctional facility, independently confirm at app.leg.wa.gov whether this bill or a successor was codified, and get the current section number and standoff distance in writing. Do not rely on this entry as a final answer.

### Professional Licensing — Washington State Board of Registration for Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors
*Binding general licensing statute | No UAS-specific board rule located*

**Objective Summary:** Ch. 18.43 RCW requires official survey products to be prepared/certified by a licensed Professional Land Surveyor. Industry secondary sources indicate producing certain UAS-derived outputs without a certified photogrammetrist or licensed land surveyor is "against the law" under this framework, though no standalone Board-issued UAS-specific guidance document was located.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Route drone-derived photogrammetry/LiDAR products through a WA-licensed PLS or certified photogrammetrist for sign-off before delivery, the same as any other official survey product — treat the absence of a UAS-specific brochure as a gap to ask the Board about directly, not as evidence the requirement doesn't apply.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Absent published UAS-specific guidance, apply the general Ch. 18.43 RCW licensure requirement and document PLS/photogrammetrist review in the deliverable QA/QC record; consider requesting a written interpretation from the Board for novel certification questions.

### Procurement / Equipment Restrictions
*Category reviewed — no state-specific restriction located*

**Objective Summary:** No Washington state law or executive order specifically restricting government purchase/use of drones by manufacturer or country of origin was located. Washington public-safety agencies are subject to the federal FCC "Covered List" restrictions on new foreign-made drones/components, which is a federal action.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* No state-level equipment restriction currently constrains fleet choice for private commercial work in Washington.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* No state compliance obligation to build into private contracts; note the federal covered-list overlay if contracting with a Washington public-safety agency.
