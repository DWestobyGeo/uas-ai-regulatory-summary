## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Hawaii regulates UAS primarily through its criminal code: a 2023 trespass/harassment statute (§ 711-1114) targeting UAS flown within 50 ft of a dwelling to coerce, intimidate, or harass, or after notice to desist; a 2024 graduated three-tier felony framework (Act 161, §§ 711-1121–1123) covering weaponization, Remote ID/anti-collision lighting defeat, property damage, and lesser misuse; and existing privacy statutes (§§ 711-1111, 711-1110.9) covering drone-based surveillance of a private place. DLNR separately restricts aircraft (including, per secondary sources, drones) landing or launching within the Hawaii State Park System absent written board permission. Notably, Hawaii has not enacted any statewide UAS preemption statute, leaving each county free to regulate UAS on county property — a materially different landscape than most states researched so far.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Haw. Rev. Stat. § 711-1114 — Trespass with an Unmanned Aircraft System
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2023 (Act 58)*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it a misdemeanor to intentionally cause a UAS to cross the property line of another and come within 50 ft of a dwelling to coerce, intimidate, or harass another person, or — after having been given actual notice to desist — for any other reason at all; or to take off or land a UAS in violation of current FAA special security instructions or UAS security-sensitive airspace restrictions. Does not apply where consent to entry was given by a person with legal authority to consent (or a person lawfully present on the property), or where the operator was authorized by federal regulations and operated the UAS lawfully and consistent with those regulations.

### Haw. Rev. Stat. §§ 711-1121, 711-1122, 711-1123 — Misuse of Uncrewed Aircraft (First, Second, and Third Degree)
*Binding law | Current, effective July 2, 2024 (Act 161)*

**Objective Summary:** Creates a graduated three-tier felony framework for UAS misuse. First degree (Class A felony): arming a UAS with a firearm, explosive, electric gun, or weapon of mass destruction; interfering with a manned aircraft; delivering prison contraband; or causing serious bodily injury. Second degree (Class B felony): disabling Remote ID transmission, defeating or failing to operate required anti-collision lighting, causing substantial bodily injury, or causing more than $20,000 in property damage. Third degree (Class C felony): tampering with a registration number, obstructing a police officer or firefighter, causing bodily injury, causing more than $750 in damage, or operating while impaired.

### Haw. Rev. Stat. §§ 711-1111, 711-1110.9 — Violation of Privacy (Second and First Degree)
*Binding law | Current*

**Objective Summary:** Section 711-1111 (second degree) makes it unlawful to install or use a device — including a drone — to observe, record, or broadcast a person in a "private place" without consent; a basic violation is a misdemeanor (up to 1 year imprisonment, up to a $2,000 fine). Where the recording shows the person nude or engaged in sexual activity, § 711-1110.9 (first degree) applies instead, a Class C felony punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment and a $10,000 fine.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### DLNR Division of State Parks — Aircraft Landing/Launching Restriction
*Binding administrative regulation | Current, rule compiled June 1999*

**Objective Summary:** Haw. Admin. R. § 13-146-9 provides that no person shall land or launch aircraft — as defined by cross-reference to Haw. Rev. Stat. § 261-1, including airplanes, airships, balloons, gliders, hang gliders, helicopters, parachutes, dirigibles, and other similar means of conveyance — within the Hawaii State Park System except with the written permission of the DLNR Board of Land and Natural Resources or its authorized representative, or in an emergency. The rule's text does not separately name UAS/drones, relying on the cross-referenced statutory "aircraft" definition; DLNR's Division of State Parks is reported by secondary sources to treat drones as covered and to require a special-use permit for any drone launch/landing/operation in the state park system.

### No State Preemption of Local UAS Ordinances; County Ordinance Patchwork
*Research result — no enacted statewide preemption statute located*

**Objective Summary:** Hawaii has not enacted a comprehensive statewide UAS-preemption statute. Secondary sources describe each of Hawaii's four counties (City and County of Honolulu, and Maui, Hawaii, and Kauai counties) as free to regulate UAS takeoff/landing on county-owned property (though not airspace, which remains federally regulated), with county drone ordinances commonly targeting beach and park access.
