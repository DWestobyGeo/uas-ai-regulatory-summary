# New Hampshire UAS Regulatory Summary

## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

New Hampshire now has a substantial UAS-specific framework. RSA 644:23 converts specified Part 107 violations into state offenses and separately prohibits privacy violations, emergency-response interference, interference with crewed aircraft or airports, certain correctional-facility operations, and weaponized or projectile-capable small UAS. Fish and Game rules regulate wildlife-taking, surveillance of hunters, and operations on Department lands; DNCR requires a permit or contract to launch or land on its reservations. Aerial pesticide work, UNH property, and government procurement add mission- or client-specific controls. The general aircraft-registration statute also appears by its definitions to reach federally registered UAS, but NHDOT publishes no UAS-specific implementation guidance, so that point requires direct agency confirmation.

### NH-002 — Operation of an aircraft while impaired

**Authority:** RSA 422:3, VI, XXII, XXVII-a, XXVII-b, XXX; RSA 422:28(VIII)  
**Status:** Current / in force

RSA 422:28(VIII) prohibits operating or attempting to operate an aircraft on the ground, on public waters, or in the air while under the influence of intoxicating liquor or a controlled drug that affects safe operation, or with a blood-alcohol concentration of 0.04 percent or more. Because RSA 422 defines aircraft by federal registration and separately incorporates unmanned-aircraft definitions, a federally registered UAS operation can fall within this independent state prohibition. This is stricter and more specific than a general workplace fitness policy and should be treated as a no-operation condition.

### NH-004 — UAS operation that violates New Hampshire's privacy statute

**Authority:** RSA 644:23(III); RSA 644:9  
**Status:** Current / in force

A person commits a misdemeanor under RSA 644:23(III) by operating a small UAS in a manner that violates RSA 644:9. The incorporated privacy statute prohibits specified device-enabled observation, photography, recording, amplification, broadcasting, or transmission in a private place and from outside a private place when the information would not ordinarily be visible, audible, or comprehensible outside. A private place includes dwelling interiors and other locations where a person reasonably expects freedom from surveillance. Limited law-enforcement and investigation exceptions do not create a general commercial mapping exemption, so sensor geometry, windows, audio, people, and data retention require deliberate controls.

### NH-005 — Interference with law-enforcement, firefighting, or emergency response operations

Operating a small UAS that interferes with law enforcement, firefighting, or other emergency response is a misdemeanor. Ordinary commercial AEC work does not qualify merely because it documents an incident; a mission should stop and deconflict whenever emergency activity arises.

### NH-006 — Small-UAS interference with human-occupied aircraft or airport operations

**Authority:** RSA 644:23(IV)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Negligently operating a small UAS so as to interfere with or disrupt the flight of a human-occupied aircraft, or to impede the normal operation of an international, regional, municipal, or general-aviation airport, is a class A misdemeanor. The offense escalates to a class B felony if the operation damages a human-occupied aircraft in flight and to a class A felony if it causes a crash resulting in death or serious bodily injury. Airport-adjacent work therefore requires more than airspace authorization: site layout, traffic patterns, lost-link behavior, observers, and immediate yielding to crewed aircraft must prevent operational interference.

### NH-007 — Correctional-facility UAS offense

### NH-009 — Use of drones to take, locate, surveil, communicate wildlife location, drive, or harass wildlife

**Authority:** N.H. Admin. R. Fis 312.01-.02; Fis 301.07(g)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Fis 312.02 prohibits taking or attempting to take wildlife with a UAS and using one to locate or surveil wildlife for taking, communicate wildlife location to a person on the ground for taking, or drive, harass, aid, or assist in taking wildlife. Law-enforcement and Fish and Game personnel acting in official duties are excepted. The moose rule separately bars UAV or aircraft location and communication between September 1 and December 31 or during the open season. An environmental survey is not hunting by default, but clients, deliverables, data sharing, season, flight behavior, and wildlife response must not convert the work into prohibited assistance or harassment.

### NH-015 — Prohibition on government acquisition of certain UAS

**Authority:** 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 95; RSA ch. 162-V  
**Status:** Current / in force; principal prohibitions prospective

## 4. Non-Regulatory Context

NHDOT operates an established UAS program and completed a statewide UAS research report, but program descriptions and policy recommendations are not treated here as obligations on private operators. The 2026 General Court also considered a new civil and criminal private-property surveillance bill and a critical-infrastructure bill; those proposals did not become current law in the form reviewed. New Hampshire's online RSA compilation states that it includes updates only through December 2025, so 2026 chapter laws must be checked alongside the codified pages until the next update.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

1. **State aircraft registration:** Does NHDOT currently require a state registration certificate, decal or other identifier, and $100 operating fee for the specific FAA-registered UAS, and what form or waiver process should be used?
2. **Private-place capture:** Will any optical, thermal, audio, LiDAR, or other sensor capture information from a private place that would not ordinarily be visible, audible, or comprehensible from outside, and whose consent or other lawful basis is documented?
3. **Emergency and airport deconfliction:** Are responders, crewed-aircraft routes, airport operations, or incident-command restrictions present, and what immediate yield, abort, notification, and lost-link controls apply?
4. **Corrections and payloads:** Does the mission approach a covered correctional facility or use any dispensing, release, tethered, impact, or other payload that could implicate RSA 644:23(V) or (VI)?
5. **Wildlife and recreation:** Is the site within active habitat or hunting, trapping, or fishing activity, and could the flight, imagery, deliverable, or data sharing assist taking, disturb wildlife, surveil participants, or interfere with lawful activity?
6. **State-managed property:** Is launch or landing on a Fish and Game or DNCR reservation, and have the current property list, application, lead time, insurance, fees, operating area, resource review, and permit conditions been confirmed?
7. **Pesticide mission:** Does the aircraft dispense pesticide, and if so does the agricultural, certificate-or-permit, and 20-foot exception apply, or are prior Division approval and occupied-area notice required?
8. **University or government client:** Does UNH permission apply, and will a state or local client acquire the system or service in a manner affected by the July 2026 procurement law or forthcoming 2028 restrictions?
