## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Delaware consolidates most of its UAS-specific law into a single, comprehensive statute, 11 Del. C. § 1334, which prohibits flying a UAS over large gatherings, critical infrastructure, active first-responder incidents, or so as to harass or invade a person's privacy — but then broadly exempts any UAS "used for a commercial or other purpose if the operator is authorized by the Federal Aviation Administration," a notably wide carve-out for properly authorized commercial operators. The same statute preempts all county/municipal UAS regulation. Separately, a 2019 amendment makes drone-delivered prison contraband a felony, and DNREC's Division of Parks and Recreation requires a detailed Special Use Permit — with insurance, FAA documentation, and (for non-recreational work like infrastructure inspection or GIS) an FAA Certificate of Authorization — for any drone use on state park land.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### 11 Del. C. § 1334 — Unlawful Use of an Unmanned Aircraft System
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2018, last amended 2024*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it unlawful to knowingly operate, direct, or program a UAS to fly: over an event with more than 1,500 people in attendance; over "critical infrastructure" (a defined list including petroleum/chemical/fuel storage and processing, electric generation/substation facilities, military facilities, port/harbor/rail-yard facilities, drinking-water infrastructure, correctional facilities, government buildings, and public-safety buildings); over an active first-responder incident; so as to harass a person on private property; so as to invade a person's privacy on private property (per specified paragraphs of § 1335); or so as to violate a protective order. Exemptions: law enforcement use; flight with the property owner/occupier's written permission; higher-education institutional use for educational purposes; and any UAS used for a commercial or other purpose where the operator is FAA-authorized. Penalties range from an unclassified misdemeanor to a Class A misdemeanor if injury or property damage results. Subsection (e) preempts all county/municipal UAS regulation.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### DNREC Division of Parks and Recreation — Special Use Permit for Drones
*Permit requirement | Current, 7 DE Admin. Code 9201, § 13.0*

**Objective Summary:** A Special Use Permit is required for any drone/UAV operation on DNREC Division of Parks and Recreation land or water. Drones may not be flown around unprotected persons, in parking lots, near protected wildlife/nesting areas, in Nature Preserves, or within 100 ft of vulnerable property. Application (licensure, insurance, FAA documentation, flight plan) is submitted to the park superintendent at least 10 days in advance; a $75 fee applies (plus $35/hour if supervision is required); required insurance is $1,000,000 per occurrence / $3,000,000 aggregate specifically covering drone use. Non-recreational uses — explicitly including infrastructure inspection and GIS — additionally require a pre-approved FAA Part 107 exemption and issued FAA Certificate of Authorization (COA), plus Division authorization.
