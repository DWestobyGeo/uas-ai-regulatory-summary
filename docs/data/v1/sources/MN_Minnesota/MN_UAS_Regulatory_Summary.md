## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Minnesota regulates UAS through a combination of criminal statutes, a distinct state aircraft-registration and commercial-licensing regime administered by the Minnesota Department of Transportation (MnDOT) Office of Aeronautics, and agency-specific rules and policies from the Department of Natural Resources (DNR) and public universities. Unlike some states, Minnesota has not enacted a statewide statute preempting local UAS ordinances, so municipalities, counties, and public entities such as the University of Minnesota remain free to — and in a number of documented cases do — impose their own additional UAS restrictions; MnDOT itself maintains a running (non-exhaustive) list of known local ordinances. At the state level, Minnesota's most distinctive AEC-relevant feature is that commercial UAS operation is subject to a state aircraft-registration requirement (Minn. Stat. § 360.55, Subd. 9-10) and, per MnDOT's own guidance, a separate state Commercial Operations License for operators whose flight-derived work product is provided to a third party — a licensing regime broader than the FAA Part 107 baseline that most AEC firms will need to affirmatively address before flying commercially in the state. Substantive UAS restrictions exist for correctional facilities (Minn. Stat. § 243.552), law enforcement use (Minn. Stat. § 626.19), DNR-managed state parks/recreation areas/waysides (Minn. R. 6100.2200 and DNR policy), hunting-related equipment use (Minn. Stat. §§ 97B.081, .085, .086, as applied to drones by DNR's own enforcement guidance), and university property (University of Minnesota administrative policy). No Minnesota-specific UAS procurement, manufacturer, or country-of-origin restriction, executive order, attorney general opinion, or state appellate court decision was located in this research pass.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Minn. Stat. § 626.19 — Use of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (Law Enforcement)
`Binding statute | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Section 626.19 requires a law enforcement agency to obtain a search warrant before using a UAV, unless one of 11 enumerated exceptions in Subd. 3 applies — including emergencies involving risk of death or bodily harm, imminent risk of evidence destruction, heightened-risk public events, credible terrorist-attack intelligence, disaster response, pre-event threat assessment, reasonable-suspicion public-area monitoring, crash reconstruction, training/public-relations use with consent, a government entity's written request, and an active missing-person search. Subd. 4 separately bars deploying facial recognition or other biometric-matching technology without a warrant, bars equipping a UAV with weapons, and restricts collecting data on public protests or demonstrations absent a warrant or applicable exception. The statute imposes documentation requirements tied to each deployment (Subd. 5), classifies UAV data as generally private/nonpublic with a default seven-day deletion requirement absent an active investigation (Subd. 6), excludes unlawfully obtained UAV evidence from criminal, administrative, or civil proceedings (Subd. 7), creates a civil remedy for an aggrieved party (Subd. 8), requires public comment and a written, publicly posted use policy before an agency's first UAV operation (Subd. 9-10), imposes sealed-warrant notice procedures (Subd. 11), and requires annual reporting by law enforcement agencies to the commissioner of public safety and by courts to the state court administrator (Subd. 12).

### Minn. Stat. § 243.552 — Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Prohibition (Correctional Facilities)
`Binding statute (criminal) | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Section 243.552, Subd. 2(a) makes it a misdemeanor for any person to knowingly fly a UAV in the airspace over a Minnesota state correctional facility, or over grounds belonging to or land controlled by the facility, without the written consent of the commissioner of corrections or a designee. Subd. 2(b) elevates the offense to a gross misdemeanor where the person additionally uses the UAV to record images (video or photographs) of the facility or its grounds, or to introduce or attempt to introduce non-contraband items as defined at Minn. Stat. § 243.55. The statute applies to any individual, not only to commercial operators, and contains no exception for newsgathering, mapping, or inspection work.

### Minn. Stat. § 360.55, Subd. 9-10 — Small Unmanned Aircraft System Registration
`Binding statute | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Subd. 9(a) requires a small UAS (unmanned aircraft weighing less than 55 pounds at takeoff, including payload) to be registered with the state for an annual $25 fee, unless the system is owned and operated solely for recreational purposes, in which case registration and the fee are not required. Subd. 9(b) exempts a qualifying system from the separate ad valorem aircraft-registration tax that otherwise applies under §§ 360.511-360.67. Subd. 10, added in the 2025 session, separately requires an operator planning a "coordinated unmanned aircraft system fleet" event to register the fleet at least 15 days in advance with the commissioner, providing organizer contact information, event date/time/location, the number of aircraft, proof of liability insurance, a copy of the small-UAS pilot's license, and a copy of the commercial operator's license, subject to a $2-per-aircraft daily fee in lieu of the Subd. 9 fee.

### MnDOT Commercial Operations License (Minn. Stat. § 360.075, as cited by MnDOT)
`Official agency policy interpreting a statutory licensing requirement | Current (as posted)`

**Objective Summary:** MnDOT's official commercial-UAS-operator guidance states that a Minnesota Commercial Operations License (in addition to the Subd. 9 aircraft registration) is required whenever a person or business advertises or holds itself out as providing a service involving aircraft, including drones, and the flight's output is delivered to a third party — regardless of whether a separate charge is made for the drone work itself. MnDOT's own examples: a drone flight whose photos are delivered to a wedding client requires both registration and the license; a drone flight used only to test sightlines, where the client-facing deliverable is produced by other means (e.g., a lift truck), requires registration but not licensing. The license is renewed annually for $30 through MnDOT's licensing portal, with drone-specific application instructions (e.g., entering "Private-UAV" for the operating-airport field, leaving the Unicom-frequency field blank, and not selecting "Flight School" even when instruction is offered).

**Citation note:** MnDOT's own webpage cites Minn. Stat. § 360.075 as the source of this licensing requirement. On direct reading, however, § 360.075 is a criminal violations/penalties section (covering unauthorized aircraft use, discharging weapons from aircraft, low flying, acrobatic flying without a parachute, etc.) and does not itself establish a licensing requirement. The substantive commercial/flight-operator licensing authority appears to sit elsewhere in Chapter 360 (a "flight operator's license" is referenced in passing at Minn. Stat. § 360.63, Subd. 1, and Minnesota Rules Chapter 8800 likely implements the licensing rules in more detail), but the precise enabling section was not independently confirmed in this research pass. This discrepancy is noted so a subsequent verification pass can pin down the exact citation; it does not appear to affect the substance of MnDOT's described licensing practice.

### Hunting-Related Drone Restrictions (Minn. Stat. §§ 97B.081, 97B.085, 97B.086, as applied to UAS by DNR guidance)
`Binding statute, as applied to UAS by official agency guidance | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Minn. Stat. § 97B.085, Subd. 1 prohibits using "radio equipment" to take big or small game. Minn. Stat. § 97B.081 prohibits casting artificial light (spotlight, headlight, or similar) to spot, locate, or take a wild animal, subject to enumerated exceptions. Minn. Stat. § 97B.086 prohibits possessing night-vision or thermal-imaging equipment while taking wild animals or while possessing an implement capable of taking one, subject to exceptions for peace officers/military personnel and certain coyote/fox hunters outside the firearms deer season. None of the three statutes mentions "drone" or "unmanned aircraft" in its own text. The DNR's own official enforcement guidance document, *Drone use while hunting* (files.dnr.state.mn.us), states directly that "it is not legal to use drones to take big or small game, which includes using drones to recover dead or wounded game," and cites these three statutes as the applicable authority for that position.

### Absence of Statewide Preemption of Local UAS Ordinances
`Research result (negative finding) | Current`

**Objective Summary:** No Minnesota statute or administrative rule preempting local UAS regulation was located in this pass (contrast, e.g., Michigan's MCL 259.305). MnDOT's own "Local Ordinances for UAS Operators" webpage confirms this posture, acknowledging that "some communities in Minnesota have enacted ordinances or rules related to the operation of drones and model aircraft," and lists known examples (Champlin, Dakota County, Eagan, Isanti, Minneapolis, Ramsey County, St. Bonifacius, Three Rivers Park District, University of Minnesota, Washington County, Wright County) while expressly disclaiming completeness. MnDOT describes its own role as the "state-level regulatory agency for aircraft in Minnesota" and offers to assist communities drafting a local ordinance, but this is an advisory/coordination role, not a statutory preemption power.

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### Minn. R. 6100.2200 and DNR State Park/Recreation Area/Wayside UAS Policy
`Binding administrative rule (landing prohibition); official agency policy (permit practice) | Current / in force`

**Objective Summary:** Minn. R. 6100.2200 makes it unlawful to land an aircraft on land or water totally within the boundaries of a state park, except by permit, at a designated airport/landing strip, in an emergency, during rescue operations, or while conducting official federal/state/county business. The DNR's own official state park rules webpage states that unmanned aircraft/drones are "aircraft" under the FAA's definition and that, because landing is prohibited, operating a UAS in these areas "is not practical"; the Parks and Trails Division accordingly "discourages" (but does not flatly ban overflight of) UAS use in state parks, recreation areas, and waysides, citing wildlife, infrastructure, and visitor safety/privacy/experience concerns. The same page separately requires a film/photography permit — reviewed by the park/area supervisor, or by the DNR public relations supervisor specifically for aircraft/drone projects, with review taking up to 30 days — whenever a project "involves the use of an aircraft, including unmanned aircraft (drone)," regardless of whether any other permit trigger applies.

### University of Minnesota — Use of Unmanned Aircraft Systems (Drones) Administrative Policy
`Official agency (university) policy | Current / in force (effective November 2016)`

**Objective Summary:** The University of Minnesota's system-wide administrative policy requires anyone operating a UAS on University property or in airspace over University property to obtain approval from the University's UAS Safety Board (UASSB) via a program plan submitted at least five business days before the activity, except for use entirely inside an enclosed classroom or research lab. A request to use UAS for public relations, communications, or commercial purposes on the University's behalf requires additional review and approval by University Marketing Communications, University Services, and the relevant campus law enforcement agency, and commercial operators must have completed all required federal, state, and local registration/licensure requirements. Recreational and other non-mission-related UAS use is prohibited on University property outright. A stadium-specific blackout applies for three hours before, during, and after main-bowl events at Huntington Bank Stadium and Malosky Stadium, extending to all East Bank, West Bank, Saint Paul, and Duluth campus property and airspace. All UAS on University property must be registered with both the FAA and the MnDOT Office of Aeronautics regardless of ownership; the University holds a blanket FAA Certificate of Waiver or Authorization, and the more restrictive of University policy or FAA regulation governs in case of conflict.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **MnDOT commercial-license citation.** MnDOT's own webpage cites Minn. Stat. § 360.075 as authority for the Commercial Operations License requirement, but that section is a criminal-penalties provision, not a licensing statute. The precise enabling statute/rule (likely elsewhere in Chapter 360 or in Minnesota Rules Chapter 8800) should be confirmed directly with MnDOT's Office of Aeronautics before treating this citation as legally definitive.
- **Wildlife Management Area (WMA) drone rule.** Secondary sources describe WMAs as banning drone operations that "chase, herd, scare, or disturb" wildlife absent an area-manager permit. This could not be traced to a specific codified Minnesota Rules part in this pass and is not included in the source register; it should be independently verified against Minnesota Rules Chapters 6230/6232 (or successor DNR wildlife-area rules) in a future pass.
- **Insurance requirement detail.** MnDOT's commercial-operator guidance references a Minnesota aircraft insurance requirement at Minn. Stat. § 360.59, Subd. 10, but this subdivision was not independently fetched and verified in this pass.
- **State UAS procurement/manufacturer restrictions.** No Minnesota-specific UAS procurement, approved-manufacturer, or country-of-origin restriction applicable to state-agency purchases was located; this should be rechecked given active national attention to this issue, since a future state policy could directly affect AEC firms delivering UAS services under public contracts.
- **Currency of pending legislation.** Multiple UAS-related bills (e.g., MN HF1301, HF1396, HF2406) were identified in bill-tracking searches; none were confirmed as enacted in this pass. Their status should be rechecked in a subsequent currency review.
