## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Louisiana regulates UAS chiefly through a single, frequently amended criminal statute rather than a comprehensive drone act, paired with an explicit statewide preemption of local UAS ordinances and two state-agency land-management rules. The core statute, La. R.S. 14:337, was created in 2014 and has been amended in nearly every legislative session since (most recently by 2025 Act 170, HB 261, the "We Will Act" Act, effective August 1, 2025): it bars using a UAS to surveil or record an enumerated set of "targeted facilities" — petroleum/alumina refineries, chemical/rubber plants, nuclear generation facilities, schools, grain elevators, and "critical infrastructure" as defined in R.S. 14:61(B) — without the owner's written consent; bars UAS overflight of the governor's mansion or a correctional facility without the facility head's consent; bars UAS surveillance or interference at a military installation without the commander's consent; and, as of the 2025 amendment, affirmatively authorizes state and local law enforcement to detect, track, and disable (via jamming, hacking, or physical capture) a UAS operating in a "nefarious manner" — making Louisiana the first state to grant non-federal law enforcement direct UAS-mitigation authority. Notably, R.S. 14:61(B)'s incorporated critical-infrastructure definition expressly extends to active construction/improvement sites of covered infrastructure, a detail with direct bearing on AEC construction-monitoring UAS work in Louisiana's petrochemical corridor. Separately, R.S. 2:2 gives the state exclusive jurisdiction to regulate UAS and preempts local ordinances — a notably different posture from many other states in this project. At the agency level, LDWF's administrative code flatly bans drones on all department-administered wildlife refuges, WMAs, and habitat conservation areas, and separately bans drone-assisted recovery of wounded deer/bear (though a pending, not-yet-finally-adopted rule change would relax the latter for the 2026-27 season). The Louisiana Office of State Parks has an internal 2018 drone policy directing FAA-rule compliance, though its public-facing permit process (if any) could not be confirmed in this pass. DOTD's Aviation Division functions mainly as an FAA-information clearinghouse and hosts a statutory, multi-stakeholder Advanced Aviation and Drone Advisory Committee (which includes a designated Louisiana Society of Professional Surveyors seat). No Louisiana-specific UAS court decision, Attorney General opinion, or executive order was located, and a 2024 bill that would have banned state procurement of Chinese-manufactured UAS (HB 915) died in House committee and was never enacted — a finding worth flagging because several secondary sources describe its provisions as if they were current law.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Unlawful Use of an Unmanned Aircraft System; Law-Enforcement Mitigation Authority — La. R.S. 14:337
<span class="news-anchor" data-record-id="LA-001" hidden></span>
*Binding law | Current, as amended by 2025 La. Acts No. 170 (HB 261, the "We Will Act" Act) and No. 23, effective August 1, 2025*

**Objective Summary:** La. R.S. 14:337(A) makes it unlawful to intentionally use a UAS to conduct surveillance of, gather evidence or information about, or photographically/electronically record a "targeted facility" without the owner's prior written consent (escalating misdemeanor/felony-range penalties and mandatory forfeiture on repeat or aggravated violations under Subsection E); to fly a UAS over the governor's mansion or a state/local correctional facility without the facility head's written consent; or to surveil, interfere with, or record a federal or state military installation without the commander's written consent (up to 5 years at hard labor and a $2,000-5,000 fine). "Targeted facility" (Subsection B(6)) means petroleum/alumina refineries, chemical/rubber manufacturing facilities, nuclear power generation facilities, schools (R.S. 14:40.6(B)), grain elevators/storage, and "critical infrastructure" as defined in R.S. 14:61(B) — a cross-referenced definition covering chemical/manufacturing/refinery, electrical generation/transmission, water intake/treatment, natural-gas/LNG/pipeline, port/rail/trucking transportation, water-control, and communications/data facilities, and which expressly extends to "any site where the construction or improvement of any facility or structure referenced in this Section is occurring." Subsection C exempts a property owner (and retained third persons) on the owner's own property or property held under a lease/servitude/right-of-way/permit/license. Subsection D exempts FAA-compliant UAS operators, agricultural commercial operations (R.S. 3:41), and university-sanctioned research/teaching use. Subsection F exempts authorized film/TV production. A 2025 amendment added Subsection G, authorizing law enforcement to take "reasonable and necessary mitigation measures" — detection, tracking, identification, and interception/disabling via jamming, hacking, or physical capture — against a UAS operating in Louisiana in a "nefarious manner," where there is reasonable suspicion of criminal activity, an imminent public-safety threat, or another law violation.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Screen the project map for protected facilities and active emergency operations before dispatch. Establish conservative stand-off and escalation points, brief the crew on the applicable restriction, and document any source-supported authorization before entering the affected area.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Contact the protected facility's owner, operator, administrator, commander, or other official identified by the authority before mobilization and ask whether the proposed AEC mission qualifies for written approval or an employment/contractor exception. Provide the site, purpose, dates, flight area, crew and aircraft details the facility requests, and retain the signed authorization and security conditions; do not assume ordinary site access or a client work order is sufficient.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.

Preserve the facts supporting any exception or consent theory, allocate client and operator responsibilities clearly, and seek state counsel when project facts approach ambiguous or penal language.
### Regulation of Unmanned Aerial Systems and Unmanned Aircraft Systems; Preemption — La. R.S. 2:2
*Binding law | Current, created 2017 La. Acts No. 238, amended 2021 La. Acts No. 328*

**Objective Summary:** La. R.S. 2:2(A) provides that, subject to Subsection C and except as otherwise provided by law, the state has exclusive jurisdiction to regulate all unmanned aircraft systems and unmanned aerial systems, and that state law supersedes and preempts any rule, regulation, code, or ordinance of any political subdivision or other unit of local government. The section expressly disclaims any effect on the federal government's exclusive sovereignty over U.S. airspace under 49 U.S.C. 40103, and states that any interpretation contradicting federal UAS regulatory authority is null. Subsection B's UAS definitions exclude satellites and UAS used by federal/state government contractors, local law-enforcement/fire agencies, and utility/cable/telecom/rail entities regulated by the Public Service Commission, a local franchising authority, the FCC, or the Federal Railroad Administration while performing facility operation/repair/maintenance work. Subsection C nullifies any provision preempted by federal law.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Use the statewide rule as the regulatory baseline, while separately screening site-control rules and any local authority preserved by the preemption provision. Record that jurisdictional check in the project flight-planning package.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.

### Prohibited Activities — Drones on LDWF-Administered Lands — LAC 76:III.113
*Binding regulation | Current through Louisiana Register Vol. 51, No. 06, June 20, 2025*

**Objective Summary:** LAC 76:III.113(A) provides that "operation of drones or unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV) is prohibited on department-administered lands including wildlife refuges and wildlife management and habitat conservation areas." Subsection B separately prohibits airboats, aircraft, personal watercraft, "mud crawling vessels," and hovercraft on the same lands. The rule is promulgated under the LDWF Secretary's general property-management authority (R.S. 56:5) and the Wildlife and Fisheries Commission's rulemaking authority (R.S. 56:109), and contains no stated exception, permit process, or research/law-enforcement carve-out within this specific section.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** This is an outright ban on department-administered lands (wildlife refuges, WMAs, and habitat conservation areas), with no stated permit or approval exception in the rule text -- do not plan a flight assuming LDWF can authorize an exception on request. Confirm the specific parcel's administering agency and boundary before mobilizing.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Treat this as a hard no-fly rule for the described land categories, not a permit gate -- the rule text contains no exception process. Confirm the parcel's exact status and boundary with LDWF directly before advising that any flight is permissible there.
### Wounded-Game Drone Recovery — LAC 76:XIX.111(D)(14) — REVERSED for the 2026-2027 season
*Binding regulation | Current — the prior prohibition was reversed effective the 2026-2027 hunting season*

**Objective Summary:** This record previously described D.14 as an outright prohibition on using a drone to recover a wounded deer or bear. That has changed: on February 5, 2026, the Louisiana Wildlife and Fisheries Commission passed a Notice of Intent reversing the prohibition, and LDWF's official 2026-2027 Hunting Regulations (published July 2026) confirm "Use of a drone to recover a mortally wounded deer or bear is permitted" as one of the season's major rule changes. To stay inside the federal Airborne Hunting Act of 1956 (which bans using aircraft to hunt or harass wildlife), the rule requires the drone to operate outside official hunting hours, carry no weapon, use lights/infrared only to determine whether an animal is dead or alive, and leave immediately without disclosing location if the animal is alive and healthy; the drone pilot must hold FAA Part 107 remote pilot certification. D.5 (the separate, non-UAS-specific prohibition on taking game from an aircraft) is unaffected and remains in force. The exact final codified administrative-code paragraph text was not independently fetched and quoted in this pass — this record relies on LDWF's own regulations-pamphlet summary and commission meeting record, so confidence is held at Moderate rather than High.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Not applicable to a typical AEC UAS program — this is a hunting-recovery rule specific to licensed hunters and their drone operators, not a general property, infrastructure, or mapping restriction.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** If advising a hunting-adjacent client, confirm the drone pilot holds current FAA Part 107 certification and that the operation stays outside official hunting hours with no weapon attached, and reconfirm the final codified rule text directly with LDWF before relying on it for a specific engagement.

*Official agency policy | Field Memo dated March 5, 2018; no later revision identified*

**Objective Summary:** The Louisiana Office of State Parks' Field Memo 2018-001 ("Drone Use Guidelines") states that UAS operation on Office of State Parks property is regulated by the FAA and relevant state and local laws, and that drone users shall adhere to the policy and comply with FAA regulations, state and federal law, and other applicable local laws/policies. ### UAS Information Page and Louisiana Advanced Aviation and Drone Advisory Committee — DOTD / La. R.S. 2:2.1
*Official agency policy / advisory guidance, with a binding statutory advisory-committee structure | Current*

**Objective Summary:** DOTD's Office of Multimodal Commerce, Aviation Division states its UAS-related goal is to ensure drones operate safely in Louisiana airspace; its public webpage primarily links to FAA drone registration, "Know Before You Fly," and FAA UAS resources rather than stating independent state operating rules. La. R.S. 2:2.1 creates the Louisiana Advanced Aviation and Drone Advisory Committee within DOTD, a fifteen-member body with designated seats including the Louisiana Farm Bureau Federation, DRONERESPONDERS Public Safety Alliance, the **Louisiana Society of Professional Surveyors**, the Louisiana Mid-Continent Oil and Gas Association, the Pelican Chapter of AUVSI, two academic institutions, and the Houma-Terrebonne Airport Commission. ### Professional Licensing — Louisiana Land Surveying Statute (La. R.S. 37:682)
*Binding general regulation | No UAS-specific board guidance located*

**Objective Summary:** La. R.S. 37:682 defines "practice of land surveying" to include measuring areas, land surfaces, streams, and bodies of water for boundary determination and description; establishing/reestablishing/describing land boundaries, corners, divisions, distances, and directions; plotting and monumenting land divisions; and "mapping and topographical work." Unlike Kentucky's analogous statute, this definition contains no express reference to "photogrammetric" control or any other sensor/method-specific term. No standalone LAPELS rule, advisory opinion, or FAQ specifically addressing UAS-derived photogrammetric or LiDAR survey products was located during this research pass; LAPELS's full posted Board Rules (Title 46, Chapter 27) were not reviewed section-by-section.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **Wounded-game drone-recovery rule — exact codified text:** LDWF's own 2026-2027 Hunting Regulations pamphlet confirms drone recovery of a mortally wounded deer or bear is now permitted (subject to the FAA-certification, hours, and no-weapon conditions), reversing the prior D.14 prohibition. This retrofit relied on LDWF's regulations-pamphlet summary and commission meeting record rather than a directly quoted final Louisiana Register/administrative-code paragraph (the PDF could not be parsed as text in this pass) — confirm the exact codified LAC 76:XIX.111(D)(14) language directly with LDWF before relying on it for a specific hunting-lease or land-management engagement.
- **R.S. 14:337 Subsection G mitigation-authority scope for compliant commercial operators:** Subsection G authorizes law enforcement to disable a UAS operating in a "nefarious manner," triggered by reasonable suspicion of criminal activity, an imminent public-safety threat, or another law violation. Unlike Subsection D's FAA-compliance exemption to the Subsection A prohibitions, the statute does not expressly exempt a compliant commercial Part 107 flight from Subsection G's mitigation trigger. AEC firms operating near "targeted facility" sites (common given Louisiana's petrochemical corridor) should confirm flight plans and, where practicable, pre-coordinate with facility owners/operators and local law enforcement to reduce the risk of being mistaken for a nefarious-manner operation.
- **R.S. 14:61(B) construction-site extension:** Because "critical infrastructure" under R.S. 14:61(B) — incorporated into R.S. 14:337's "targeted facility" definition — expressly includes "any site where the construction or improvement of any facility or structure referenced in this Section is occurring," a UAS-based construction-monitoring engagement on a qualifying facility (e.g., a new pipeline, refinery expansion, or electrical substation) may itself be a "targeted facility" requiring owner consent under R.S. 14:337(A), independent of whether the completed facility would qualify. Confirm the specific project's consent/exemption basis (owner consent, FAA-compliance exemption, or another R.S. 14:337(D) exception) before flying.
- **Office of State Parks public permit process:** This project could not confirm whether Louisiana Office of State Parks maintains a public-facing UAS/commercial-filming permit application analogous to Kentucky's Department of Parks "Location Contract," nor could it fully retrieve and parse the 2018 Field Memo's complete text. Confirm current requirements directly with the specific park or historic site before any commercial UAS flight.
- **Preemption's reach over local-government-owned-property rules:** R.S. 2:2 preempts local *regulation* of UAS, but its text does not expressly address whether a parish or municipality may still impose generally applicable rules of conduct for its own publicly owned property (e.g., a city park's launch/landing rules). Secondary commentary draws this distinction, but it was not confirmed against the statute's own text or any Louisiana court/AG interpretation in this pass.
- **State-agency UAS procurement:** No enacted Louisiana statute currently restricts state-agency procurement of foreign-manufactured UAS; 2024's HB 915 died in committee. AEC firms working for Louisiana state agencies should not assume a state-level country-of-origin procurement restriction applies absent independent confirmation of subsequent legislation or agency policy.
