## 1. State overview

Utah requires state registration for commercial UAS operations that require federal certification. UDOT currently directs commercial and government operators to pre-register while it completes the payment and certificate portal. Utah also independently states visual-line-of-sight, controlled-airspace, airport, notice-to-airmen, altitude, public-transit, and surface-critical-infrastructure rules, with civil liability and escalating penalties. The state broadly preempts local UAS regulation, except for stated airport authority, and limits local licensing of Part 107 and Part 135 businesses to a single Utah political-subdivision license recognized statewide.

Mission-specific statutes address correctional facilities, wildfire scenes, livestock, private-property trespass and surveillance, law-enforcement data, and armed aircraft. Of particular importance to public-sector infrastructure work, Utah restricts covered-foreign-entity UAS used by public entities and their direct contractors for critical-infrastructure inspection, unless the public entity ensures specified offline operation, data-removal, and video-software controls. Commercial use of Utah State Parks property generally requires advance approval, and individual parks publish additional drone forms, seasonal closures, fees, and operating conditions.

### UT-001 — Commercial UAS state registration and UDOT pre-registration process

**Authority:** Utah Code §§ 72-10-109(4), 72-10-110(4); UDOT Aircraft Registration — Drone Registration  
**Status:** Current / in force; official portal pending

### UT-002 — Preemption of local UAS ordinances and statewide recognition of business licenses

**Authority:** Utah Code § 72-10-701  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 72-10-701 generally bars Utah political subdivisions from regulating private UAS use unless Chapter 10 authorizes the rule or an airport operator governs operations within airport boundaries or takeoff and landing at its airport. A commercial Part 107 or Part 135 UAS business may be required to obtain one local business license if it lacks a current license in good standing elsewhere in Utah, but other subdivisions must recognize that license. Fees are limited to actual processing cost, and multiple subdivision licenses or per-employee licenses are prohibited.

### UT-003 — Law-enforcement use of UAS data

**Authority:** Utah Code §§ 72-10-801, 72-10-802  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 72-10-802 prohibits a law-enforcement agency or officer from obtaining, receiving, or using UAS-acquired data unless it is obtained under a warrant, a judicially recognized warrant exception, qualifying circumstances involving a nongovernment actor, a lost-person search where no reasonable expectation of privacy exists, or another method that does not violate a reasonable expectation of privacy. Nongovernment data may be used for law-enforcement purposes only when it appears crime-related or supports a good-faith imminent or ongoing emergency. Covered data must be destroyed as soon as reasonably possible, subject to applicable retention law.

### UT-004 — Law-enforcement UAS data retention and incidental collection

**Authority:** Utah Code § 72-10-803  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 72-10-803 generally bars a law-enforcement agency from using, copying, or disclosing UAS data about a person, structure, or area that was not a mission target and requires destruction as soon as reasonably possible under applicable law. Exceptions cover inseparable target data, court-ordered or nongovernment-actor data, inadvertent collection that appears crime-related, emergency information whose use or disclosure would assist the response, and data collected over public lands outside municipal boundaries. The duty applies to the agency, but a contractor handling agency data may need to support the agency's retention and deletion controls.

### UT-005 — Weapon attached to an unmanned aircraft

**Authority:** Utah Code §§ 72-10-901, 72-10-902  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 72-10-902 makes flying an unmanned aircraft that carries or has an attached weapon a class B misdemeanor. The statutory weapon definition includes a firearm and an object capable, in its use or intended use, of causing death, bodily injury, or property damage under listed contextual factors. Exceptions apply when the operator follows an FAA certificate of authorization or other written approval, performs under a state or federal contract that permits the armed operation, or operates with Department of Defense permission in DOD-controlled airspace.

### UT-006 — Unlawful operation of a UAS near a correctional facility

**Status:** Current / in force

Section 72-10-903 prohibits operating a UAS to carry or drop any item to or inside correctional-facility property, to remove any item from inside that property, or to interfere with facility operations or security. Carrying, dropping, or removing an item is a third-degree felony; operational or security interference is a class B misdemeanor. The 2026 amendment preserves a narrow exception for an employee or contractor working for a mosquito-abatement district while acting within the scope of employment. ### UT-009 — Covered-foreign-entity UAS restriction for public critical-infrastructure inspection

**Authority:** Utah Code §§ 72-10-1201, 72-10-1202  
**Status:** Current / in force

Sections 72-10-1201 and 1202 prohibit a public entity or a contractor working directly for one from purchasing or operating, for critical-infrastructure inspection, a UAS manufactured or assembled by a covered foreign entity. ### UT-010 — Unlawful privacy violation and UAS commercial-purpose defense

**Authority:** Utah Code § 76-12-302  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 76-12-302 makes specified unauthorized surveillance of a private place, and certain nonconsensual ground-penetrating collection about property, a class B misdemeanor with possible destruction of data and civil remedies. A UAS operator has a defense when the flight serves a legitimate commercial or educational purpose, follows applicable FAA authority, and any otherwise prohibited conduct is solely incidental to that lawful use. A land surveyor performing a bona fide contract receives a separate ground-penetrating-data exception only if irrelevant data about noncontract property is not distributed and is destroyed after the contract.

### UT-011 — Criminal trespass involving unmanned aircraft

**Status:** Current / in force

Section 76-6-206 defines entry to include an entire unmanned aircraft and treats a UAS as remaining unlawfully over private property when the property is not open to the public and the operator lacks authorization. ### UT-012 — Wildland fire and unmanned aircraft

**Authority:** Utah Code § 65A-3-2.5  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 65A-3-2.5 prohibits UAS flight within an FAA wildfire temporary flight restriction or an area officially designated as a wildland-fire scene unless the incident commander grants permission and the operator follows imposed restrictions. Reckless violations carry escalating misdemeanor or felony penalties based on interference, contact, or crash consequences, plus possible restitution. The incident commander must grant reasonable, safely conditioned access within three miles to listed sanctioned entities, which include utilities, pipeline operators, insurers, resource-extraction entities, news media, and certain FAA-authorized operators. Officials may neutralize a violating aircraft when reasonably necessary.

### UT-013 — Harassment of livestock using a UAS

**Authority:** Utah Code § 76-13-214  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 76-13-214 prohibits intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly chasing livestock with intent to cause distress, or harming livestock, through use of a UAS. A qualifying violation is generally a class B misdemeanor but becomes a class A misdemeanor for a repeat offense, serious injury or death, or livestock or property damage over $1,000 including recovery cost. Exceptions cover the livestock owner, an employee or agent acting with owner direction or permission, emergency action to protect livestock or property, government personnel acting officially, and generally accepted animal-husbandry practices.

### UT-015 — Commission of an offense with the aid of an unmanned aircraft

**Authority:** Utah Code § 76-2-106  
**Status:** Current / in force

Section 76-2-106 provides that an actor may be found guilty of an offense when the actor commits that offense with the aid of an unmanned aircraft and the associated UAS is under the actor's control at the time. The section uses the UAS definitions in Utah Code § 72-10-102. It does not create a standalone operating permit or identify a separate offense level; instead, it establishes how controlled UAS use can support criminal responsibility for another underlying offense, such as conduct addressed by Utah's trespass, privacy, or interference provisions.
