## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Florida regulates commercial UAS primarily through two statutes — the Unmanned Aircraft Systems Act (§330.41) and the Freedom from Unwarranted Surveillance Act (§934.50) — both substantially amended for 2025. The state vests UAS regulatory authority centrally and preempts most local UAS-specific ordinances, while local governments retain authority over takeoff/landing on their own property and generally applicable nuisance/park rules. Penalties for critical-infrastructure and school violations were sharply increased in the 2025 legislative session. Multiple commercial/FAA-compliance carve-outs support routine AEC operations, but the elevated penalties make documentation discipline important.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Fla. Stat. §330.41 — Unmanned Aircraft Systems Act
*Binding law | Current, rewritten effective October 1, 2025*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits knowingly/willfully operating over, contacting, or interfering with an enumerated list of 20 "critical infrastructure facility" types (power/water/gas facilities, seaports, airports, correctional facilities, military installations, dams, communications facilities, etc.) — 3rd-degree felony — unless the operation is commercial and FAA-authorized (§330.41(4)). Bans drone overflight/contact with any pre-K–12 school at all times (§330.41(5)), with penalties escalating to felony for repeat, recorded violations. Bans operation on classified agricultural land without owner consent (§330.41(6), new 2025). Bans drone contact with private property, state wildlife management lands, or shooting ranges made with intent to harass (§330.41(7), new 2025). Vests UAS regulatory authority in the state (§330.41(3)), preempting local operational ordinances except nuisance/harassment rules and takeoff/landing authority over locally owned property.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Build the commercial/FAA-authorization basis into flight-planning checklists for any job near critical infrastructure. Treat pre-K-12 campuses as a hard no-fly without prior written coordination — there's no construction-project exception. Flag any site on classified agricultural land for owner-consent documentation before tasking a crew, and keep standard standoff/no-loiter discipline near residences and wildlife-management land.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* The felony-level penalty for critical-infrastructure violations means flight authorization documentation (FAA certificate, commercial purpose, client contract) should be retained in the project file as an affirmative-defense record, not a courtesy. For school-adjacent work, obtain written principal or district consent in advance — verbal coordination is not adequate given the criminal exposure. The 2025 agricultural-lands and harassment-based property provisions are new and largely untested; build consent documentation into standard operating procedure rather than relying on interpretation of "intent to harass."

### Fla. Stat. §934.50 — Freedom from Unwarranted Surveillance Act
*Binding law | Current, amended through 2025*

**Objective Summary:** Bars law enforcement from using a drone to gather evidence without a warrant or listed exception (§934.50(3)(a)), and bars any person, state agency, or political subdivision from using an imaging-equipped drone to surveil private property or persons in violation of a reasonable expectation of privacy without written consent (§934.50(3)(b)). Eighteen exemptions are listed in §934.50(4), including aerial mapping in FAA compliance (k); utility operations, maintenance, inspection, routing, and environmental monitoring (j); property-appraiser ad valorem assessment (i); communications-facility work (n); state/local disaster-damage assessment and public-land vegetation/wildlife management (g); and FWC/Florida Forest Service invasive-species and wildfire work (o). Subsection (7) requires the Department of Management Services (DMS) to maintain an approved-manufacturer list; governmental agencies had to discontinue non-approved-manufacturer drones by January 1, 2023.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Aerial mapping, corridor survey, and utility-inspection flights fall squarely within the (4)(k) and (4)(j) exemptions — tag each flight log with the specific exemption relied on so it's easy to produce for a client or regulator later. For public-works contract work, confirm any agency-owned equipment you fly is on the DMS approved-manufacturer list.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Because the privacy prohibition in (3)(b) carries civil remedies including punitive damages and attorney's fees for the prevailing party, require every commercial flight over or near private property to be tied in writing to a specific §934.50(4) exemption — not just a general belief that the work is "mapping." For public-agency contracts, confirm in the scope of work whether the DMS manufacturer requirement is imposed as a flow-down term.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### Procurement — Florida Department of Management Services
*Official agency policy | Binding on governmental agencies only*

**Objective Summary:** DMS publishes and maintains the approved-manufacturer list implementing §934.50(7). Agencies submitted discontinuation plans for non-approved drones by July 1, 2022, and fully discontinued non-approved-manufacturer drones (effectively excluding DJI, Autel, and other Chinese-manufactured drones from government use) by January 1, 2023.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* If your fleet is DJI-based, budget separately for DMS-approved airframes before bidding on FDOT, county, or municipal UAS work — don't assume your standard private-sector kit qualifies, and check the current list early since procurement lead times for approved manufacturers can run long.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Confirm whether a public-agency RFP or contract incorporates the approved-manufacturer requirement as a flow-down term; if so, build equipment-compliance representations into the proposal and retain documentation of equipment used on each task order.

### Aviation / DOT — FDOT Aviation and Spaceports Office
*Advisory guidance | Nonbinding*

**Objective Summary:** FDOT maintains a UAS reference webpage pointing to §§330.41, 330.411, and 934.50; confirms no separate FDOT UAS registration or commercial permit scheme exists.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Useful as a bookmarked starting point for onboarding a Florida project, but go to the underlying statute text for anything you plan to rely on in the field.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Treat this page as agency-published pointer material, not authority in itself; cite the underlying statute, not the webpage, in client-facing compliance documents.

### Parks and Public Lands — Florida DEP, Division of Recreation and Parks
*Advisory guidance | Category reviewed, no primary rule confirmed*

**Objective Summary:** No specific Florida DEP administrative rule confirming a statewide drone launch/landing policy for Florida State Parks was located in this research pass.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Call the specific park office before scheduling a launch/land flight — don't assume a blanket statewide answer exists, since individual parks may post their own model-aircraft or drone restrictions.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Treat this as an open item requiring direct written confirmation from DEP or the park manager before authorizing a flight; absence of a located statewide rule is not confirmation that no restriction applies.

### Fish and Wildlife — Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission
*Binding statute + official agency guidance*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits using a drone to take, harass, or assist in taking fish or wildlife (Fla. Stat. §379.401 and accompanying FWC rules); FWC guidance flags that low-altitude flights near nesting shorebirds/wading birds can constitute harassment. §330.41(7) separately prohibits drone contact with state wildlife management lands made with intent to harass.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* For environmental-monitoring and corridor/wetland mapping flights, plan altitude and flight paths to maintain standoff from known rookeries and nesting colonies during breeding season, and coordinate with the regional FWC office before any Wildlife Management Area project.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Document the environmental/monitoring purpose of flights near wildlife concentrations and any FWC coordination in the project file — the harassment standard is judgment-based, so contemporaneous documentation of flight purpose and altitude is the best defense if a complaint arises.

### Corrections — Fla. Stat. §944.47 and Critical-Infrastructure Overlap
*Binding law | Current*

**Objective Summary:** §944.47 criminalizes introducing contraband into a state correctional institution, including via drone (2nd/3rd-degree felony depending on contraband/facility). State correctional institutions, contractor-operated correctional facilities, county detention facilities, and juvenile secure-detention facilities are separately enumerated as "critical infrastructure facilities" under §330.41(2)(b)17.–19., meaning ordinary overflight is restricted absent the commercial/FAA exemption.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Identify correctional/detention facilities near any project site during pre-flight planning — route flight paths around them by default rather than relying on the commercial exemption unless the project specifically requires flying there.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* For any project genuinely requiring inspection/survey work at or adjacent to a correctional facility, obtain written facility-operator coordination in advance and retain FAA authorization/commercial-purpose documentation given the felony stakes.

### Professional Licensing — Florida Board of Professional Surveyors and Mappers
*Binding general licensing statute | No UAS-specific board rule located*

**Objective Summary:** Florida's "practice of surveying and mapping" definition under Ch. 472, Fla. Stat., already contemplates photogrammetric procedures as an accepted measurement method; a Florida-licensed Professional Surveyor and Mapper (PSM) must certify boundary surveys and official mapping deliverables regardless of collection method. No standalone UAS-specific board guidance document was located.

**Practical Interpretation**
- *AEC Industry UAS Expert Perspective:* Route drone-derived photogrammetry/LiDAR/orthophoto products through a Florida-licensed PSM for review before delivery on any boundary or official survey deliverable — the pilot flying the mission does not need to be a PSM, but sign-off does.
- *AEC Industry Legal Counsel Perspective:* Absent UAS-specific board guidance, rely on the general Ch. 472 licensure requirement and document PSM involvement in the QA/QC workflow for every certified deliverable; consider a formal inquiry to the Board if a project raises a novel certification question.
