## 1. State overview

Wisconsin has a comparatively mature collection of UAS-specific state authorities. They address correctional institutions, schools, utility facilities, privacy, weaponized aircraft, law-enforcement surveillance, local preemption, DNR property, hunting, and hunter interference. Two significant amendments became current in April 2026: one added school-property restrictions and the other added a low-altitude buffer around specified utility-facility parcels. The utility provision contains an express exception for a commercial-purpose operation conducted in compliance with FAA rules, authorizations, or exemptions, but the statute should still be checked against the planned mission facts.

WisDOT also has unusually detailed contract requirements. When section 107.27 is incorporated into a highway contract, it requires advance written engineer approval, extensive flight-plan documentation, incident protocols, specified liability insurance, and UAS/component compliance with federal security-drone guidance. No separate Wisconsin commercial UAS license, registration, or generally applicable statewide manufacturer restriction was located beyond federal requirements and contract-specific conditions.

## 2. Statewide laws

### WI-001 — Correctional-institution overflight

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 114.045(1)(a), (1m), and (2)  
**Status:** Current / in force

Wis. Stat. 114.045 prohibits operating a drone over a state or county correctional institution or its grounds without express authorization. Authorization must come from the secretary of corrections or designee for a state institution, or the county sheriff or designee for a county institution. A violation may produce a forfeiture of up to $5,000. An investigating officer must seize visual images or other data created or recorded by the drone in connection with the alleged violation and handle that material under the statute's transfer and retention provisions.

### WI-002 — School-property overflight

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 114.045(1b), (1m)(b), and (2), as amended by 2025 Wisconsin Act 189  
**Status:** Current / in force; enacted April 3, 2026

Act 189 prohibits drone operation over property owned by, or containing, a public, private, or tribal school while the school is open for pupil instruction or the property is used for a school-approved event. Express authorization from the applicable school authority or designee is an exception, and the authority must respond to a request within five business days. Narrow law-enforcement exceptions also apply. A violation may result in a forfeiture up to $5,000, and alleged-violation imagery or data is subject to statutory seizure and retention.

### WI-003 — Utility-facility parcel buffer

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 114.045(1)(b) and (3)(b), as amended by 2025 Wisconsin Act 194  
**Status:** Current / in force; enacted April 3, 2026

Act 194 prohibits drone operation below 300 feet over or within 500 feet of a parcel containing specified water, electric, natural-gas, telecommunications, broadband, cable, public-water, petroleum-refining, storage, or pipeline-pump facilities. Listed exceptions include owner-directed facility work, official local-government work, and a commercial-purpose operation conducted in compliance with applicable FAA regulations, authorizations, or exemptions. The provision excludes electric transmission lines and other structures used solely to carry electric power or product. The chapter's forfeiture and imagery-seizure provisions apply to alleged violations.

### WI-004 — Low flight, interference, danger, and landing consent

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 114.04  
**Status:** Current / in force

Wis. Stat. 114.04 makes flight over Wisconsin land and water lawful unless the altitude is low enough to interfere intentionally with an existing use of the land or water, or the flight is imminently dangerous or damaging to persons or property lawfully on the surface. Landing an aircraft on another person's land or water without consent is unlawful except in a forced landing, and the operator or owner is liable for resulting damage. WisDOT's official UAS page states that Wisconsin recognizes unmanned aircraft as aircraft and identifies this provision as applicable.

### WI-005 — Intoxicated or reckless aircraft operation

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 114.09  
**Status:** Current / in force

Wis. Stat. 114.09 prohibits operating or attempting to operate an aircraft while under the influence of an intoxicant, controlled substance, controlled-substance analog, or other drug to a degree that makes safe operation impossible, and prohibits operation with a prohibited alcohol concentration. It separately prohibits careless or reckless operation that endangers another's life or property and directs courts to consider federal safe-operation standards. Penalties escalate based on offense history and circumstances. WisDOT's official UAS page identifies section 114.09 among the Wisconsin statutes applicable to unmanned aircraft.

### WI-006 — Local-government preemption

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 114.105  
**Status:** Current / in force

Wis. Stat. 114.105 generally prohibits a city, village, town, or county from regulating drone ownership or operation. A political subdivision may adopt an ordinance in strict conformity with Wisconsin Chapter 114 and may impose the same penalty, but it may not suspend or revoke an aviation certificate or license. A political subdivision may also limit how the subdivision itself uses drones. The statute bars ordinances contrary to or inconsistent with Chapter 114 or federal law, substantially limiting local operator-facing UAS regulation while preserving the stated exceptions.

### WI-007 — Drone observation in places of expected privacy

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 942.10  
**Status:** Current / in force

Wis. Stat. 942.10 makes it a Class A misdemeanor to use a drone, with intent, to photograph, record, or otherwise observe another individual in a place or location where that individual has a reasonable expectation of privacy. The statute uses the drone definition in section 114.105 and excludes law-enforcement use authorized under section 175.55(2). The prohibition is directed to intentional observation or recording of an individual in a protected setting; it is not framed as a general ban on aerial imaging or incidental capture in public places.

### WI-008 — Weaponized drones

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 941.292  
**Status:** Current / in force

Wis. Stat. 941.292 defines a drone as a powered aerial vehicle that carries no human operator, uses aerodynamic forces for lift, and can fly autonomously or by remote piloting. Operating any weaponized drone is a Class H felony. The only stated exception applies to a member of the United States armed forces or National Guard acting in an official capacity. The provision regulates the aircraft's weaponization rather than ordinary payloads used for imaging, surveying, inspection, or remote sensing.

### WI-009 — Law-enforcement drone surveillance

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 175.55  
**Status:** Current / in force; public-agency rule

Wis. Stat. 175.55 prohibits a Wisconsin law-enforcement agency from using a drone to gather evidence or other information in a criminal investigation from or at a place where an individual has a reasonable expectation of privacy without first obtaining a search warrant. Exceptions cover use in a public place, active search and rescue, locating an escaped prisoner, surveillance to execute an arrest warrant, and reasonable suspicion that drone use is necessary to prevent imminent danger or imminent destruction of evidence. The definition includes state, local, tribal, and Department of Justice agencies.

### WI-010 — Drone-assisted interference with hunting, fishing, or trapping

**Authority:** Wis. Stat. 29.083(2)(a)8, limited by *Brown v. Kemp*, 86 F.4th 745 (7th Cir. 2023)  
**Status:** Current in part; subsection (2)(a)7 held unconstitutional

Section 29.083(2)(a)8 prohibits using a drone to conduct an interference activity prohibited by subdivisions 1 through 7, including harassing wildlife, physically impeding or obstructing a hunter or associated activity, and disturbing hunting property, blinds, stands, bait, or feed. In *Brown v. Kemp*, the Seventh Circuit held subdivision 7's proximity, confrontation, photography, recording, and monitoring restrictions unconstitutional, but the original physical-interference provisions were not challenged. Drone use therefore remains prohibited when used to carry out still-valid interference acts, while subdivision 7 cannot support enforcement as written.

### WI-014 — Judicial limit on hunter-monitoring restrictions

**Authority:** *Brown v. Kemp*, 86 F.4th 745 (7th Cir. 2023)  
**Status:** Binding federal appellate decision

The Seventh Circuit held Wis. Stat. 29.083(2)(a)7's restrictions on maintaining proximity to, approaching, confronting, photographing, recording, or monitoring hunters unconstitutional. The court found the clauses vague, overbroad, and viewpoint discriminatory and reversed the district court. The decision expressly distinguished the original statute's content-neutral prohibitions on physical interference and obstruction, which the plaintiffs did not challenge. Because subdivision 8 incorporates drone use to conduct activities in subdivisions 1 through 7, the decision narrows the drone provision by removing subdivision 7 as a valid enforcement predicate.

## 3. State agency requirements and guidance

### WI-011 — Drone use as an aid to hunting

**Authority:** Wisconsin DNR, 2026–27 Online Hunting Regulations, “Use of Devices”  
**Status:** Current agency regulation summary; updated annually

Wisconsin DNR's current online hunting regulations state that it is illegal to hunt any animal with the aid of any aircraft, expressly including unmanned aircraft and drones. This rule concerns use of aerial information or capability to aid hunting, rather than ordinary unrelated UAS work occurring near hunting activity. The online regulations are updated by season, so the current-year DNR materials should be checked before any wildlife, forestry, or public-land mission whose imagery, timing, personnel, or client purpose might connect the operation to locating or taking animals.

### WI-012 — UAS activity on specified DNR properties

**Authority:** Wis. Admin. Code NR 45.04(1)(c) and Wisconsin DNR flying-activities guidance  
**Status:** Current / in force

NR 45.04(1)(c) restricts flying-related activities, expressly including uncrewed aircraft systems, to areas posted for their use on state parks, state recreation areas, state natural areas, the Kettle Moraine and Point Beach state forests, and the Lower Wisconsin State Riverway. DNR's public flying-activities page repeats the restriction. The provision is framed around activity “on” the listed properties and posted-use areas; it does not identify a general statewide permit form or state that an unposted property manager may waive the rule informally.

### WI-013 — WisDOT contract UAS approval and equipment requirements

**Authority:** WisDOT ASP-6, section 107.27, effective with February 2026 lettings  
**Status:** Binding when incorporated into a WisDOT contract

WisDOT section 107.27 requires covered contractors to obtain written engineer approval at least three business days before operating a drone within the right-of-way. The application must document pilot and aircraft credentials, mission parameters, maps, notifications, preflight and incident procedures, and other specified details. The provision also establishes training, safety, incident-reporting, and liability-insurance requirements. For February 2026 lettings, it adds that UAS and components must comply with American Security Drone Act and OMB M-26-02 federal guidelines. Contract documents should be checked for incorporation and later revisions.
