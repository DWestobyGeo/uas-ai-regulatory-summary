# New Mexico UAS Regulatory Summary

## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

New Mexico does not currently have an enacted general state UAS statute establishing a commercial-flight permit, broad surveillance offense, critical-infrastructure buffer, or statewide equipment-manufacturer restriction. Its operative state-level controls are narrower and mission dependent. Wildlife rules prohibit drone-assisted hunting and wildlife harassment; professional-surveying rules expressly reach digital geospatial data from UAV/UAS sensors; State Parks can impose park-specific posted restrictions; and New Mexico State University and the University of New Mexico require advance approval for covered campus operations. Federal aviation requirements and ordinary property, privacy, contract, and professional-practice law remain independently relevant, but are not converted here into UAS-specific state records without a direct state UAS hook.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### NM-001 — Minimum Standards for Surveying in New Mexico

**Authority:** 12.8.2.2, 12.8.2.6-.8, 12.8.2.11, 12.8.2.14-.16 NMAC  
**Status:** Current / in force

New Mexico's minimum surveying standards expressly include digital geospatial data produced by UAV/UAS and airborne sensors. When a licensed surveyor—or an engineer authorized by the Engineering and Surveying Practice Act—undertakes surveying, the licensee must classify the work and follow the corresponding standards. Topographic maps must meet current ASPRS positional-accuracy standards and carry prescribed accuracy reporting; control surveys relied on by other professionals require a report with coordinates, datum, methods, dates, accuracy, and certification. Boundary, easement, and right-of-way work carries additional field, plat, supervision, certification, and sometimes recordation requirements. The rule does not make every drone-derived image a professional survey.

### NM-002 — Use of aircraft and drones in hunting and wildlife management

**Authority:** 19.31.10.7, 19.31.10.11(D), (F)-(H) NMAC  
**Status:** Current / in force

New Mexico prohibits pursuing, harassing, driving, or rallying protected species except as lawful hunting or otherwise authorized by wildlife law. It also prohibits using an aircraft or drone to spot or locate protected species and relay the location to anyone on the ground. No person may hunt, take, or assist in hunting or taking a protected species with location information gained from a drone at any time; the comparable aircraft restriction lasts 48 hours. The Department Director may grant an exemption for management purposes. Ordinary engineering, inspection, mapping, or environmental collection is not automatically hunting assistance, but flight conduct and later disclosure or reuse of wildlife-location data can trigger the rule.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### NM-003 — Park-specific drone restrictions under superintendent authority

**Authority:** 19.5.2.9(C) NMAC; New Mexico State Parks current restriction alerts  
**Status:** Current / park-specific postings change over time

New Mexico's park rule authorizes each superintendent to restrict or prohibit activities in specified park areas by posting or other designation; it does not itself state a blanket statewide drone ban. The Division's live alerts use that authority for drone-specific prohibitions. As reviewed, Bottomless Lakes and Rio Grande Nature Center have ongoing notices citing 19.5.2.9(C), and other individual park pages identify drone use as prohibited. Because the restriction list is dynamic and park-specific, a team should check the current alert and the exact park's page before mobilization and obtain written direction when commercial access, takeoff, landing, or an exception is proposed.

### NM-004 — Unoccupied Aircraft Systems (Drones)

**Authority:** NMSU Administrative Rules and Procedures 16.75  
**Status:** Current university policy

NMSU prohibits launching, landing, or flying a UAS within University Premises without University approval and applies the policy to outside parties. Outdoor requests use the Flight Request Form, normally submitted 3–10 business days before the activity, and authorization is not complete until EHS&RM gives final written approval. A third party needs an NMSU sponsoring department and supporting operator, aircraft, insurance, and safety documentation. The policy also restricts recording in private or sensitive spaces, requires incident reporting, and subjects grant or contract terms involving UAS purchase or flight to EHS&RM review for federally banned systems and systems not authorized in other states. University-resource aircraft have additional registration, insurance, training, and disposition requirements.

### NM-005 — Drone Flight Approval Request Form

**Authority:** UNM Risk Services drone-flight approval requirements  
**Status:** Current university policy

The University of New Mexico requires prior approval for every drone operation over University-owned property and every operation involving a University-owned aircraft. Its Risk Services request asks for pilot information including a Part 107 certificate, any certificate of authorization, aviation or drone liability-insurance evidence, acknowledgement of FAA commercial-operation requirements, and a complete flight plan. The University directs applicants to submit the form at least two weeks before the planned flight. University-owned drones must be insured through Risk Services before flight. An outside commercial team should treat the approval and lead time as mission prerequisites and confirm the property boundary and current documentary requirements before scheduling fieldwork.

## 4. Non-Regulatory Context

The 2026 Legislature considered S.B. 136, which would have created criminal offenses for drone surveillance of persons and operation over critical infrastructure, with exceptions relevant to legitimate commercial work, surveying, engineering, insurance, and government contractors. The official bill page records that the proposal died after action was postponed indefinitely. It is therefore not current law and is not a source-register record, but it identifies a subject that should be rechecked in later legislative sessions. Earlier New Mexico drone bills likewise should not be treated as enacted merely because bill text or legislative analysis remains online.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

1. **Surveying scope:** Will the UAS-derived deliverable constitute boundary, topographic, easement, right-of-way, control, or other professional surveying, and who is the New Mexico licensee in responsible charge?
2. **Wildlife interaction:** Could flight behavior pursue, harass, drive, or rally protected species, or could captured location data be relayed or later used to aid hunting?
3. **Park status:** Is any launch, landing, staging, or planned flight associated with a state park, and what do the park's current alerts, activity page, and superintendent say on the mission date?
4. **University property:** Does the project touch NMSU or UNM property, including overflight or a university-owned aircraft, and have the correct sponsor, approval, insurance, credential, safety-plan, and lead-time requirements been satisfied?
5. **Legislative change:** Has New Mexico enacted a successor to 2026 S.B. 136 or another general UAS statute since this review?
