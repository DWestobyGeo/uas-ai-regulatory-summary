## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

Illinois regulates UAS primarily through a small set of targeted statutes rather than one omnibus drone act. The Freedom from Drone Surveillance Act (725 ILCS 167) restricts *law enforcement* drone use, not private commercial operation, but is the state's most heavily litigated and amended UAS statute. Separately, 620 ILCS 5/42.1 makes UAS regulation an exclusive state function and preempts local ordinances statewide — except for Chicago (population over 1,000,000) and a narrower carve-out letting any local government adopt "reasonable rules" for UAS use over its own park/recreation/conservation land. The Wildlife Code (520 ILCS 5/2.33(i)) bars using a drone to harass wildlife, with a notable exception for utility/mobile-service infrastructure inspection. State-facility-specific rules exist for the Capitol Complex (which expressly recognizes construction-documentation flights as an approvable use) and for IDNR-administered state parks and land (which require site-specific landing authorization under a rule that does not, on its face, name drones). IDOT's Division of Aeronautics runs an active UAS program for consultants/contractors and, as of March 2026, has imposed an indefinite ban on FASC-listed foreign-manufactured UAS (including DJI and Autel) on any IDOT project with a federal-funding nexus — a currently disruptive, fast-moving policy for AEC firms doing IDOT-funded inspection and mapping work. Two additional bills — one addressing government-agency UAS procurement/country-of-origin restrictions and sensitive-site geofencing, another creating private-operator-facing critical-infrastructure and correctional-facility no-fly offenses — remain pending in the General Assembly and are not yet law.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### Freedom from Drone Surveillance Act — 725 ILCS 167
*Binding law | Current, originally P.A. 98-569 (2014), substantially amended by P.A. 103-101 (eff. 6-16-23)*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits a "law enforcement agency" from using a drone to gather information except under 10 enumerated exceptions in Section 15 (e.g., a Section 108-3 search warrant; exigent circumstances/imminent harm; disaster or public-health emergency; missing-person search and rescue; crime-scene/traffic-crash photography; infrastructure inspection at a local government's request; public-relations demonstration with no data collection; PSAP-dispatched emergency response; and monitoring a "routed event" or "special event" with advance public notice). Sections 17–18 separately bar onboard facial recognition and equipping a law-enforcement drone with any weapon. Section 20 requires destruction of gathered information within 30 days (24 hours for event-monitoring use), Section 25 restricts disclosure, Section 30 creates a presumption of inadmissibility for information gathered in violation of the Act, Section 35 requires annual public reporting to the Illinois Criminal Justice Information Authority, and Section 45 gives the Attorney General pattern-and-practice investigation and injunctive authority, including a 6–12 month drone-use forfeiture remedy.

### Regulation of Unmanned Aircraft Systems — State Preemption of Local Ordinances — 620 ILCS 5/42.1
*Binding law | Current, text as amended by P.A. 103-925 (eff. 8-9-24)*

**Objective Summary:** Makes UAS regulation "an exclusive power and function of the State" to the extent state oversight doesn't conflict with federal law, and bars any unit of local government — including home-rule units — from enacting an ordinance or resolution regulating UAS; this does not apply to a municipality of more than 1,000,000 inhabitants (currently only Chicago). Subsection (b-5) separately preserves a local government's right to adopt "reasonable rules" for a private party's use of airspace above its own recreational/conservation property (parks, playgrounds, wildlife areas, etc.), provided those rules don't conflict with state agency rules or federal law. Subsection (d) authorizes IDOT to adopt UAS safety rules.

### Wildlife Code — Prohibition on Using Unmanned Aircraft to Take, Pursue, or Harass Wildlife — 520 ILCS 5/2.33(i)
*Binding law | Current, provision most recently reflected in P.A. 103-154 (eff. 6-30-23)*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it unlawful to take, pursue, or intentionally harass or disturb wild birds or mammals by use or aid of a vehicle, conveyance, or unmanned aircraft (as defined by the Illinois Aeronautics Act), except as federally permitted for waterfowl taking. The subsection exempts using an unmanned aircraft to inspect a public utility facility, tower, or structure, or a mobile-service facility, tower, or structure, by the utility or provider itself.

### Unmanned Aerial Systems Security Act — SB 2364 (proposed — not enacted)
*Proposed or pending authority | Introduced 2/6/2025; last action 2/7/2025 (referred to Assignments); no further action recorded*

**Objective Summary:** Would bar a government agency from purchasing, acquiring, or using a drone (or related services/equipment) produced by a manufacturer domiciled in, or reasonably believed to be owned/controlled by, a "country of concern," using a 3-tier drone classification and a reimbursement fund for agencies holding non-compliant equipment. Would also require IDOT to identify "sensitive location" coordinates and require flight-mapping-software providers to geofence them, making unauthorized sensitive-location overflight (or software facilitation of one) a Class A misdemeanor. As introduced, proposes a January 1, 2026 effective date, contingent on passage. **This bill has not passed either chamber of the General Assembly and is not current law.**

### Drone Safety and Interference Prevention Act — SB 3930 / HB 5275 (proposed — not enacted)
*Proposed or pending authority | HB 5275 through House Judiciary-Criminal Committee and to Second Reading, then re-referred to Rules 4/17/2026 after a floor amendment; SB 3930 in Senate committee process, last action 3/27/2026*

**Objective Summary:** Would create three new offenses — unlawful imaging of an emergency-response activity, unlawful operation over a critical infrastructure facility, and unlawful operation over a correctional institution or facility — and would bar any private person or entity from equipping a drone with a firearm, weaponized laser, kinetic-impact projectile, chemical agent/irritant, or other lethal/non-lethal weapon. Provides for law-enforcement seizure and court-supervised forfeiture of a violating drone, with notice/hearing protections and civil immunity for good-faith enforcement. As introduced, proposes a January 1, 2027 effective date, contingent on passage. **Neither bill has passed a full chamber and neither is current law.**

## 3. State Agency and Facility-Specific UAS Requirements

### Illinois Capitol Complex — Operating Unmanned Aircraft on the Capitol Complex (71 Ill. Adm. Code 2005.65)
*Binding administrative regulation | Current, adopted 11-16-23*

**Objective Summary:** Closes the Capitol Complex (State-owned/occupied buildings, grounds, parking lots, and airspace within defined Springfield boundaries) to launching, landing, or operating a UAS, subject to five exceptions (emergency response; health/safety-emergency support; national defense; care-and-custody activities with prior Director approval; and construction-site documentation flights authorized by the Office of the Architect of the Capitol with advance notice to the Director). Absent an exception, a person may seek advance written approval from the Director of the Secretary of State's Department of Police, who evaluates public-safety, property-damage, interference, and scheduling-conflict factors and may impose time/place/manner conditions. All FAA requirements apply independently.

### Illinois Department of Natural Resources — State Park/Land Aircraft Landing Authorization (17 Ill. Adm. Code 110.160(i))
*Binding administrative regulation | Current*

**Objective Summary:** Makes it unlawful, except in emergencies, to land or attempt to land any aircraft on IDNR-owned, -leased, or -managed property (including State parks) without prior Department authorization for each landing or attempted landing. The provision's text uses the general term "aircraft," not a UAS-specific term; IDNR's own site-level practice treats this as applicable to drone launches/landings and requires site-by-site written permission (coordinated through the relevant Site Superintendent or IDNR's Office of Land Management) before any drone flight originating from or landing on IDNR land.

### Illinois Department of Transportation, Division of Aeronautics — UAS Program and Foreign-Made-UAS Restriction
*Official agency policy | Current; restriction memo dated March 18, 2026, in place indefinitely*

**Objective Summary:** IDOT's Division of Aeronautics administers an active UAS program and requires contractors/consultants operating UAS in support of an IDOT project to first contact the Division to confirm they may legally operate. As of March 18, 2026, IDOT — implementing the federal American Security Drone Act of 2023 and OMB Memo M-26-02 — prohibits use of Federal Acquisition Security Council (FASC)-prohibited-manufacturer UAS (named examples include DJI and Autel) on any IDOT project or activity with a federal-funding nexus, including phase 1/2 work likely to draw federal funding later. This applies to IDOT-owned equipment and expressly to third-party contractor/consultant UAS. IDOT states it is coordinating with FHWA toward a resolution but has been given no timeline; the restriction is described as in place indefinitely.

### Professional Licensing — Illinois Professional Land Surveyor Act of 1989 (225 ILCS 330/5)
*Binding general regulation | No UAS-specific board guidance located*

This illustrates how Illinois law enforcement agencies are operationalizing the PSAP-dispatch and event-monitoring exceptions in 725 ILCS 167 (IL-001) in current practice. ## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **620 ILCS 5/42.1(b-5) local park/recreation-land rules:** Any Illinois park district, forest preserve district, or municipality could have adopted its own "reasonable rules" for UAS use over its own recreational/conservation land under this subsection; no statewide inventory of such local rules was compiled in this pass — confirm directly with the relevant local land-managing agency before assuming state preemption fully controls a specific site.
- **17 Ill. Adm. Code 110.160(i) "aircraft" scope:** This IDNR park-land rule does not use a UAS-specific term; its application to drones rests on IDNR site practice and general inference, not on rule text naming drones — confirm directly with IDNR's Office of Land Management before relying on this citation.
- **IDOT FASC-prohibited-manufacturer restriction:** Described by IDOT as in place "indefinitely" with no resolution timeline; AEC firms with pending or planned IDOT-funded UAS work should confirm current status and permitted airframes directly with the Division of Aeronautics before mobilizing equipment, as this is a fast-moving, currently unresolved policy.
- **SB 2364 and SB 3930/HB 5275 (pending legislation):** Neither the procurement/country-of-origin bill nor the critical-infrastructure/correctional-facility bill has been enacted; both should be monitored for advancement in the current or a future General Assembly session, since either would materially change the regulatory landscape for AEC UAS work in Illinois if passed.
- **225 ILCS 330/5 photogrammetric-methods exception:** Whether a specific UAS-derived deliverable falls inside or outside licensed land-surveying practice is fact-specific (accuracy achieved, operator certification) and was not resolved against current IDFPR/Board guidance in this pass.
- **Local ordinances (Chicago and elsewhere):** Chicago's own drone ordinance (available under the 620 ILCS 5/42.1(b) population exception) and any local 42.1(b-5) rules are out of scope for this Phase 1, state-level pass.
