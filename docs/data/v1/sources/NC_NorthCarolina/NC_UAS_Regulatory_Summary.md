## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

North Carolina has one of the most extensively codified state UAS regulatory frameworks reviewed to date, built out over multiple session laws since 2014 (S.L. 2014-100, 2015-232, 2016-90, 2017-160, 2017-179, and 2023-63) and recently simplified by a significant 2024 deregulatory change. The core framework spans privacy/surveillance restrictions (§ 15A-300.1), launch/recovery site consent with a narrow local-ordinance carve-out (§ 15A-300.2), a correctional-facility buffer with a detailed commercial-operator exception (§ 15A-300.3), a newer and unusually severely penalized forest-fire buffer (§ 15A-300.4, enacted 2023), interference with manned aircraft (§ 14-280.3), weapon-attached UAS and hunting/fishing restrictions (§ 14-401.24), thermal-imaging distribution restrictions (§ 14-401.25), and wildlife-harassment-by-UAS (§ 113-295(a1)). Importantly, effective December 1, 2024, the state repealed its separate UAS knowledge-test and commercial-operator-permit requirement (formerly §§ 63-95/63-96) after a legislative veto override — commercial and government operators now need only comply with FAA Part 107, a materially favorable simplification for AEC firms operating in North Carolina. State parks independently prohibit UAV launch/landing absent a Special Activity Permit.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-300.1 — Restrictions on Use of Unmanned Aircraft Systems (Privacy/Surveillance)
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2014, amended 2017*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits any person, entity, or State agency from using a UAS to conduct surveillance of a person, dwelling and curtilage, or private real property without consent, or to photograph an individual without consent for publication (with a newsgathering/public-event exception). Law enforcement and emergency-management exceptions apply. Creates a civil cause of action with $5,000 statutory damages per photograph/video, plus costs and fees; violating evidence is generally inadmissible in criminal prosecutions.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Routine aerial mapping and inspection is not the targeted conduct, but avoid incidental surveillance-style capture of neighboring private property or individuals without consent.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** This creates a private civil cause of action with statutory damages distinct from any criminal exposure — document flight purpose and project boundaries near residential areas.
### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-300.2 — Regulation of Launch and Recovery Sites
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2014*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits launching or recovering a UAS from State or private property without consent. Separately authorizes a unit of local government to adopt an ordinance regulating UAS launch/recovery from property the local government owns — the primary statutory basis for local UAS ordinances in North Carolina, narrower than a general local police-power grant.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Obtain documented consent before launching/recovering from any State or private property outside the client's project site; check for a locally-owned-property launch/recovery ordinance if the project involves city/county land.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** This is the primary hook for North Carolina's narrower, property-based local ordinance authority — distinguish it from a broad local police-power grant for clients.
### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-300.3 — UAS Near a Confinement or Correctional Facility Prohibited
*Binding law | Current, effective December 1, 2017*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits UAS operation within 500 ft horizontal / 250 ft vertical of a local confinement facility or State/federal correctional facility. Exceptions include written facility consent, law enforcement, and a detailed commercial/public-utility exception (150 ft horizontal/vertical buffer, 24-hour notice, qualifying business purpose, FAA and Chapter 63 Article 10 compliance, no unescorted entry). Penalties range from a Class I misdemeanor up to a Class H felony (weapon delivery) or Class I felony (contraband delivery).

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Directly relevant to infrastructure work near a North Carolina jail or prison — the narrower commercial exception (150 ft buffer + 24-hour notice) may apply to qualifying utility/infrastructure inspection work, but document each required element.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** The commercial exception's condition (d) cross-references Chapter 63, Article 10 compliance, which was substantially repealed in 2024 (see below) — confirm what that cross-reference now requires before relying on the exception.
### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-300.4 — UAS Near a Forest Fire Prohibited
*Binding law | Current, enacted 2023*

**Objective Summary:** Prohibits UAS operation within 3,000 ft horizontal or vertical of any forest fire within North Carolina Forest Service jurisdiction. Exceptions for consent from the official in charge, law enforcement, and Forest Service personnel. Penalties are severely tiered by consequence — up to a Class D felony if the violation proximately causes death, down to a Class A1 misdemeanor, each with a mandatory minimum $1,000 fine.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Directly relevant to post-wildfire damage assessment work — do not fly within 3,000 ft of an active forest fire without consent from the managing official, given the severe injury-tiered felony exposure.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** This is one of the most severely penalized state UAS statutes reviewed to date; document specific consent and warrant conservative compliance.
### N.C. Gen. Stat. §§ 14-280.3, 14-401.24, 14-401.25, 113-295(a1) — Additional Criminal Code Provisions
*Binding law | Current*

**Objective Summary:** § 14-280.3 makes willful interference with a manned aircraft via UAS a Class H felony. § 14-401.24 makes weapon-attached UAS possession/use a Class E felony and UAS-assisted fishing/hunting a Class 1 misdemeanor. § 14-401.25 makes publishing/disseminating non-consensual thermal/infrared UAS imagery revealing interior building activity a Class A1 misdemeanor. § 113-295(a1) makes UAS-assisted wildlife-taking interference a Class 1 misdemeanor.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Weapon and hunting/fishing provisions have low relevance to standard AEC payloads; the thermal-imaging distribution restriction is directly relevant to building-envelope/energy-audit inspection work — obtain owner consent before publishing thermal deliverables revealing interior activity.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Distinguish conducting a thermal inspection (not itself prohibited) from publishing/disseminating imagery without consent (prohibited); maintain airspace deconfliction documentation given § 14-280.3's felony exposure.
## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### Repeal of State UAS Knowledge Test and Commercial Operator Permit
*Binding law (repeal) | Effective December 1, 2024*

**Objective Summary:** Session Law 2024-15 (House Bill 198, enacted via veto override) repealed Chapter 63, Article 10 (formerly §§ 63-95, 63-96), eliminating the separate North Carolina UAS knowledge test and State commercial-operator permit that had applied on top of FAA Part 107. Commercial and government operators now need only comply with FAA Part 107 or other applicable federal authorization.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Do not plan for a North Carolina State UAS permit or knowledge test — this requirement no longer exists. FAA Part 107 remains the operative licensing requirement.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Update pre-2024 compliance materials referencing the old permit/test; note the unresolved cross-reference in § 15A-300.3(b)(3)(d) to "Chapter 63, Article 10" compliance, which is now a largely repealed article.
### Parks — North Carolina Division of Parks and Recreation
*Binding administrative regulation | Current, amended 2022*

**Objective Summary:** 07 NCAC 13B .1204 prohibits voluntarily bringing, landing, or taking off with a UAV/drone on Division of Parks and Recreation property, except under a Special Activity or Special Use Permit (07 NCAC 13B .0104).

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Apply for a Special Activity/Special Use Permit well before any AEC UAS work planned within a state park boundary.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Retain the permit in the project file; confirm current procedure and any park-specific variations directly with the Division.
### Professional Licensing — NC Board of Examiners for Engineers and Surveyors
*Binding general regulation | No UAS-specific board guidance located*

**Objective Summary:** North Carolina's licensing framework (Chapter 89C) requires PE/PLS licensees to practice only within their demonstrated competency. No standalone UAS-specific board guidance was located.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Route UAS-derived photogrammetry/LiDAR products through a North Carolina-licensed PE or PLS under the existing general competency framework.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** Document licensed-professional review given the absence of North Carolina-specific UAS guidance.
## 4. State/Local Regulatory Boundary

**Objective Summary:** North Carolina occupies a middle position: § 15A-300.2(b) gives local governments narrow authority to regulate UAS launch/recovery from property they own (city/county parks, facilities), but does not grant a broad power to regulate UAS operation generally within their jurisdiction. Municipal examples located include Raleigh's and Wake Forest's park-specific UAS guidelines.

**Practical Interpretation**
- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Treat local-ordinance risk as concentrated on locally owned property rather than a broad citywide patchwork; confirm whether a project site is on locally owned park/government land.
- **Agency Practitioner:** Not applicable — no agency process.
- **UAS Procurement Expert:** Not applicable — no procurement or equipment-selection implication identified.
- **AEC Industry Legal Counsel:** A property-specific municipal-ordinance check remains warranted in a later research phase, particularly for city/county park land.

## 5. Unresolved Operational Questions

- **Local ordinances:** Out of scope for this phase; North Carolina's narrow property-based local authority (Section 4) still warrants a project-specific check for locally owned land.
