## 1. State UAS Regulatory Overview

South Dakota expressly permits federally compliant commercial UAS operations and limits state and local regulation of equipment, airspace, flight paths, and pilot qualifications. Operators remain subject to state criminal rules involving private-place observation, careless endangerment, harassment, unauthorized landings, correctional and military facilities, contraband, highway rights-of-way, and wildlife. Small UAS are exempt from the state aircraft-registration chapter. State-agency layers are important for AEC work: commercial imaging in state parks uses a permit process, Capitol Complex operations need prior written approval, and SDDOT service providers generally must use company-owned aircraft and comply with agency policy.

## 2. Statewide UAS Laws and Regulations

### SD-001 — Federal compliance, commercial operation, and limits on state and local UAS regulation
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** South Dakota requires drone operations to comply with applicable FAA regulations and expressly permits a business lawfully operating in the state to use a drone commercially when federally compliant. State and local governments generally may not regulate UAS equipment, registration, airspace, flight paths, or pilot qualifications. Political subdivisions may adopt operation rules consistent with federal and state law, regulate their own UAS, and enforce federal restrictions. Title 50 also disclaims intrusion on exclusive federal airspace authority, so the statute does not eliminate property, facility, or other state restrictions.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Use the state rule to identify what a political subdivision may regulate, then check only the controlling property's published operating terms before launch. Record the applicable boundary, property owner, designated-use area, notice, and permission status in the flight packet without assuming that state preemption eliminates property-use conditions.

### SD-002 — Airspace overflight liability and preservation of privacy and trespass torts
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** A federally compliant UAS operation in airspace over South Dakota does not, standing alone, create liability under state or local law. That protection is narrow: the same chapter expressly preserves claims when a drone is used as an instrumentality of a privacy tort or trespass under federal or state law. The provisions therefore distinguish lawful overflight from the operator's imaging, observation, physical intrusion, landing, or other conduct. They do not create a general right to enter property, use a site, collect any desired data, or disregard the chapter's separate criminal restrictions.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Use a mission-specific collection plan for Statewide that limits camera angle, dwell time, audio, zoom, thermal capture, and retention to the contracted purpose. Brief the crew on aborting or redirecting collection when people, residences, or unrelated activity enter the sensor footprint.

### SD-003 — Intentional UAS observation in a private place and business-purpose exception
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Except as authorized by law, a person may not intentionally use a drone to photograph, record, or otherwise observe another person in a private place where that person has a reasonable expectation of privacy. A violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. The statute excepts lawful law-enforcement activity, designated emergency-management work within assigned duties, and a bona fide business or governmental operation when the covered photography, recording, or observation is unintentional or incidental. The business exception is limited to incidental collection and does not authorize intentional surveillance or negate separate restrictions on data use, property access, or landing.

### SD-004 — Careless operation, endangerment, voyeurism, and harassment
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** South Dakota makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to intentionally or willfully operate a drone in a careless manner that endangers the life or property of another person. The same section prohibits operating a drone for voyeuristic or harassment purposes. The statutory standard addresses the purpose and manner of the flight rather than imposing a separate state flight certificate or numeric operating limit. Compliance with FAA rules does not replace this state obligation, and an otherwise commercial or professionally planned mission can still violate the provision if intentionally or willfully conducted in the prohibited manner.

### SD-005 — Landing on another owner's property or water without consent
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** A person generally may not land a drone on another owner's real or personal property, or on waters overlying the owner's real property, without consent; a violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. A forced landing or technical malfunction is an affirmative defense, but the operator remains liable for resulting damage. Chapter-wide exceptions cover landings reasonably necessary for an emergency or for maintenance support involving public or private critical infrastructure, qualifying public-safety duties, and federal government or federal-contract duties. The exceptions do not erase damage exposure or create general access permission for routine commercial work.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Make active emergency operations a dispatch and in-field stop-work check for Statewide. The remote pilot should have a clear deconfliction contact and an immediate land-or-relocate procedure if responders, temporary restrictions, or crewed aircraft appear.

### SD-006 — Drone operations over correctional, detention, and military facilities; countermeasure authority
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** No person may operate a drone over the grounds of a prison, correctional facility, jail, juvenile detention facility, or military facility without express authorization from the facility administrator. Effective July 1, 2026, a violation is a Class 6 felony rather than a misdemeanor. Law-enforcement officers may use mitigation techniques and countermeasures authorized under § 50-15-3.1, which permits the Department of Public Safety to adopt rules defining authorized measures and training. No final implementing rule was located in the current administrative-rule collection, so operators should not infer the available countermeasures from the statute alone.

### SD-007 — Drone delivery of contraband to correctional facilities
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Using a drone to deliver contraband or a controlled substance to a state prison or other correctional facility is a Class 6 felony in addition to the penalty for the underlying contraband or controlled-substance offense. This is a payload-delivery provision, separate from the broader prohibition on operating over covered correctional grounds without administrator authorization. It does not create an exception for a commercial operator, contractor, accidental routing decision, or federally compliant flight. Ordinary AEC payloads are not the statute's target, but the provision reinforces the need to control attached equipment and avoid covered grounds.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** This record has no routine effect on an AEC mission or ordinary fleet operation because it applies to a separately regulated person or activity. Do not treat it as a general UAS operating rule; address it only if the stated regulated-party condition is actually present.

### SD-008 — Takeoff and landing in highway rights-of-way and controlled-access facilities
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** Drone takeoff or landing within a controlled-access highway facility is prohibited unless conducted by a state agency or its agent. Within any other public-highway right-of-way, takeoff or landing is prohibited when conducted carelessly or in a manner that endangers persons or property. A violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor. Chapter-wide exceptions apply to qualifying emergency or critical-infrastructure maintenance support, forced or malfunction landings that comply with FAA rules, sworn public-safety duties, and federal government or federal-contract duties. The statute does not itself grant site access or define how a consultant establishes state-agent status.

### SD-009 — State aircraft registration exemption for small unmanned aircraft systems
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** South Dakota's aircraft-registration chapter requires registration and weight-based fees for covered aircraft, but § 50-11-29 exempts an individual operating a small unmanned aircraft system. Title 50 defines a small unmanned aircraft as an unmanned aircraft weighing not more than 55 pounds, including everything onboard or attached. Accordingly, the ordinary small-UAS fleet used for AEC work is outside this state aircraft-registration scheme, although federal registration remains separate. A heavier unmanned aircraft may fall within the general state registration and fee provisions, and the text should be rechecked for uncommon or heavy-platform acquisitions.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Do not add a Virginia aircraft-registration task for the unmanned aircraft covered by this express exemption. Keep the exemption citation with the fleet compliance record while continuing to screen for mission-specific property, facility, wildlife, and client requirements.

### SD-010 — Use of aircraft and drones to hunt, locate, or spot wildlife
*Binding statute or regulation | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** South Dakota makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to kill or attempt to kill wildlife from an aircraft or to use an aircraft to hunt, take, concentrate, drive, rally, stir up, locate, or spot a wild bird or animal. A drone may be used to locate or spot a predator or varmint only on privately owned land, by the landowner or with the landowner's or lessee's permission, outside September through November, and in full FAA compliance. The exception does not authorize general wildlife surveys performed for hunting purposes or relax separate property and resource-agency requirements.

## 3. State Agency UAS Requirements

### SD-011 — Commercial UAS filming and photography on or above South Dakota state park lands
*Permit or property-use requirement | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks directs commercial drone filming and photography on or above state park lands to the local park manager and its Filming/Photography Permit process. Its current drone page also bars eavesdropping, contraband delivery, prohibited wildlife activity, nuisance or traffic obstruction, and interference with area management or authorized uses. The linked department policy states that commercial use requires a commercial-use permit. Administrative rule 41:03:01:01 independently prohibits unauthorized use of park land and conduct interfering with management, so FAA compliance alone does not establish permission for a commercial state-park mission.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** Treat property authorization for South Dakota state park lands as a pre-mobilization gate and obtain conditions that cover launch, landing, route, dates, aircraft, crew, and sensor purpose. ### SD-012 — Capitol Complex UAS prohibition and use permit
*Permit or property-use requirement | Current / in force*

**Objective Summary:** The South Dakota Capitol Complex is closed to UAS launch, landing, and operation, including indoor operation, unless a policy exception or prior written Bureau approval applies. Exceptions address law enforcement, fire response, health or safety emergencies, national defense, care and custody of the complex, and permitted activity. The current permit form requests the purpose, operator credentials, aircraft information, location, date, and time; imposes FAA, visual-line-of-sight, separation, privacy, liability, and indemnification conditions; and directs submission at least ten days before the activity. An approved permit must be carried and may be revoked.

### SD-013 — SDDOT UAS policy for department work and service providers
*Official agency policy | Current agency manual*

**Objective Summary:** SDDOT permits UAS use for department functions when it improves cost efficiency, data quality, or personnel safety. Employees may not use privately owned UAS for department business, and a UAS service provider must use company-owned UAS unless a written agreement with SDDOT provides otherwise. Consultants are directed to the Consultant Service Manual. Operations must comply with Part 107 requirements for operations, pilot certification, aircraft registration, waivers or authorizations, and airworthiness; the remote pilot in command retains final authority and responsibility for safety and compliance. Emergency deployment remains subject to FAA emergency requirements.

**Practical Interpretation**

- **AEC Industry UAS Expert:** This record does not directly regulate an ordinary privately controlled AEC flight, but it can govern a mission performed for or integrated into the named public agency's program. Define in the scope and flight plan who authorizes the mission, controls the aircraft, receives the data, and is responsible for the cited records or use restrictions.
- **Agency Practitioner:** N/A — no agency process involved

If the agency policy expects company-owned equipment, obtain a written project exception before mobilizing a rented, employee-owned, affiliated-company, or subcontractor system.
